## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Moderate.** This chapter has the strongest external support in Ezra 1–6. The governor **Tattenai** is attested in a Babylonian document, the date under Darius I agrees with Haggai and Zechariah, and the procedure of a local inquiry referred to the king matches Persian administrative practice. The quoted letter has probably been edited, but its core is credible.

## What the Text Claims

- The prophets **Haggai** and **Zechariah** urge the people to build, and Zerubbabel and Jeshua resume work (5:1–2).
- **Tattenai, governor of Beyond the River**, with **Shethar-bozenai** and colleagues, asks who authorized the building and records the names of the builders, but does not stop the work (5:3–5).
- Their letter to Darius reports the building of "the house of the great God" with large stones and timber (5:6–8).
- The elders reply that Cyrus authorized the Temple, returned the vessels to Sheshbazzar, and that Sheshbazzar laid the foundation (5:11–16).
- Tattenai asks that the royal archives in Babylon be searched (5:17).

## External Evidence

### Tattenai

A Babylonian cuneiform tablet dated to the 20th year of Darius I (502 BCE) mentions **Tattannu**, *pāḫat Ebir-nāri*, "governor of Beyond the River." This is one of the few officials in Ezra with direct extra-biblical confirmation. His title and the period fit the text exactly. He appears to have been a deputy under the satrap of Babylon and Beyond the River, which was then a single great satrapy.

### Darius I and the year 520 BCE

Darius I came to power in 522 BCE after suppressing a series of revolts, which he records in the **Bisitun inscription**. Imperial attention to loyalty in the provinces in 521–520 BCE makes an official inquiry into new building in Jerusalem natural. Haggai and Zechariah 1–8 are dated to 520–518 BCE and confirm the resumption of building under Zerubbabel and Joshua.

### Administrative procedure

The Elephantine papyri show the same pattern: local officials investigate, write to higher authority, and await a ruling. The **Petition to Bagavahya** (TAD A4.7, 407 BCE) concerning the Elephantine temple is a close parallel in genre, a Jewish community seeking official permission to rebuild a temple.

## Points of Agreement

- Tattenai's name, title, and date are confirmed.
- The timing (Darius's second year, 520 BCE) agrees with Haggai and Zechariah.
- The tone of the inquiry, cautious rather than hostile, fits Persian bureaucratic norms better than the hostility of chapter 4.
- Building "with great stones and timber laid in the walls" (5:8) matches a known construction technique of courses of stone alternating with wooden beams (compare 1 Kgs 6:36).

## Points of Conflict

| Ezra 5 | Problem |
|--------|---------|
| Sheshbazzar laid the foundation (5:16) | Ezra 3 and Zechariah 4:9 credit Zerubbabel |
| Jews tell Persian officials they were exiled because they angered "the God of heaven" (5:12) | A theological confession unlikely in an official report; probably edited by the Judean compiler |
| Zerubbabel absent from the letter | Perhaps deliberate. Some suggest his royal Davidic status had become politically sensitive |

## Assessment

Ezra 5 likely rests on a genuine document or a close memory of a real inquiry. Even sceptical scholars such as Grabbe allow that the Tattenai correspondence may have an authentic basis, while noting the pro-Jewish framing. Williamson considers the letter substantially authentic. The Sheshbazzar–Zerubbabel discrepancy is best explained by the compiler preserving two traditions side by side. This chapter, together with Haggai and Zechariah, is the firmest ground for dating the Second Temple's construction to 520–515 BCE.
