## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Moderate.** The completion of the Second Temple in the sixth year of Darius I (515 BCE) is widely accepted. The Aramaic memorandum of Cyrus is regarded by many scholars as resting on an authentic record, though Darius's reply contains elements that look like Judean expansion. The dedication and Passover scenes are idealized.

## What the Text Claims

- A scroll is found not in Babylon but at **Ecbatana** in Media, recording Cyrus's first-year decree (6:1–2).
- The memorandum orders the house rebuilt as a place of sacrifice, gives its dimensions (60 cubits high, 60 wide), specifies three courses of stone and one of timber, provides costs from the royal treasury, and orders the vessels returned (6:3–5).
- Darius orders Tattenai not to interfere, to fund the work from provincial taxes, and to supply sacrificial animals, with impalement for anyone who alters the decree (6:6–12).
- The Temple is finished on 3 Adar in Darius's sixth year (6:15).
- A dedication with 100 bulls, 200 rams, 400 lambs, and 12 goats for Israel's tribes is followed by Passover (6:16–22).

## External Evidence

### Ecbatana

Ecbatana (Hamadan) was the former Median capital and a royal summer residence of the Achaemenids. Greek sources say Persian kings moved between Susa, Babylon, and Ecbatana by season. Cyrus spending part of his first year there is plausible, and finding a document in the archive of a different city from the one first searched is the kind of detail a later fabricator would be unlikely to invent. The memorandum is introduced with the Aramaic term *dikrônâ* ("record" or "memorandum"), a genuine administrative genre.

### Persian royal support for temples

- Darius I is known to have supported temples in Egypt, including the temple of **Hibis** in the Kharga Oasis.
- The **Udjahorresnet** inscription records royal sponsorship of the cult of Neith at Sais.
- The Greek **Gadatas inscription**, a letter attributed to Darius praising the Apollo cult, is sometimes cited as a parallel, but its authenticity is disputed.

These show that royal funding of a local cult is credible.

### The curse formula

Threats against anyone who alters a decree, invoking the gods, are standard in Near Eastern royal inscriptions, including Darius's own **Bisitun inscription**. Impalement as punishment is attested in Bisitun as well.

### The date

3 Adar of Darius's 6th year corresponds to about March 515 BCE (1 Esdras 7:5 gives 23 Adar). This date is consistent with Haggai and Zechariah and is accepted by most historians.

## Points of Agreement

- The memorandum's style (dated heading, location, brief instructions, building specifications) suits an archival note.
- The Temple's completion around 515 BCE fits the prophetic books and the general chronology.
- Persian royal patronage of local temples is well documented.

## Points of Conflict

| Ezra 6 | Problem |
|--------|---------|
| Temple 60 cubits high and 60 wide | Larger than Solomon's Temple (60 x 20 x 30 cubits, 1 Kgs 6:2), contradicting Haggai 2:3 and Ezra 3:12 where the new Temple is smaller; possibly textual corruption |
| Darius requests prayers "for the life of the king and his sons" (6:10) | Plausible; the Elephantine petition promises similar prayers for the governor |
| Full imperial funding from taxes | Haggai describes a struggling community with no mention of royal funds |
| Offerings for "twelve tribes of Israel" | Reflects the compiler's ideology of an all-Israel restored community |

## Assessment

Williamson and many others accept the Cyrus memorandum as substantially authentic. Grabbe considers it possible but notes that Darius's reply is so favourable that it looks like Judean wish-fulfilment. A reasonable view is that the compiler had access to a genuine core, perhaps preserved in Jerusalem, and expanded it. The Temple's dedication in 515 BCE is one of the most secure dates in the book.
