## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Low to mixed.** The restoration of the altar soon after the return is plausible. Dating the laying of the Temple foundation to the second year after the return (c. 537/536 BCE) conflicts with the prophets Haggai and Zechariah, who place it in 520 BCE. The chapter probably projects later events back into the reign of Cyrus.

## What the Text Claims

- In the seventh month, Jeshua and Zerubbabel build the altar and resume sacrifices, including the Feast of Booths (3:1–6).
- They hire masons and carpenters and pay Sidonians and Tyrians to bring cedar from Lebanon by sea to **Joppa**, by permission of Cyrus (3:7).
- In the second year after arrival, the Temple's foundation is laid with priests, Levites, trumpets, and the singing of psalms (3:8–11).
- Old men who had seen the first Temple weep while others shout for joy (3:12–13).

## External Evidence

### Haggai and Zechariah

The two prophets are the nearest contemporary sources. **Haggai** dates his oracles precisely to the second year of Darius I (520 BCE). He rebukes the people for living in panelled houses while the Temple lies in ruins (Hag 1:4) and treats the foundation as laid on 24 Kislev of that year (Hag 2:18). **Zechariah 4:9** says that "the hands of Zerubbabel have laid the foundation of this house." Neither prophet mentions any earlier foundation under Cyrus.

### Sheshbazzar

Ezra 5:16, quoting a Persian letter, credits **Sheshbazzar** with laying the foundation. Ezra 3 credits Zerubbabel and Jeshua and never mentions Sheshbazzar. This tension suggests the compiler merged two separate traditions.

### Timber from Lebanon and Joppa

Lebanese cedar was the prestige building timber of the ancient Near East, used by Egyptian, Assyrian, and Babylonian kings. In the Persian period the Phoenician cities controlled the coast. The sarcophagus inscription of **Eshmunazar II** of Sidon records that the "lord of kings" (the Persian king) granted Dor and Joppa to the Sidonians. That fits the picture of Sidonian and Tyrian shippers delivering timber to Joppa. The verse also deliberately echoes Solomon's arrangement with Hiram (1 Kgs 5; 2 Chr 2:16).

## Points of Agreement

- Restoring an altar and sacrifice before rebuilding a temple is natural; worship may never have fully ceased at the ruined site (compare Jer 41:5).
- Jeshua (Joshua) son of Jozadak and Zerubbabel son of Shealtiel are well attested as leaders around 520 BCE in Haggai and Zechariah.
- Persian-period Phoenician control of Joppa is confirmed by the Eshmunazar inscription.

## Points of Conflict

| Ezra 3 | Other evidence |
|--------|----------------|
| Foundation laid in the second year after return (c. 537 BCE) | Haggai 2:18 and Zechariah 4:9 imply 520 BCE |
| Zerubbabel and Jeshua lay the foundation | Ezra 5:16 credits Sheshbazzar |
| A grand ceremony on the Solomonic model | Haggai describes neglect and a community too poor to build |

## Assessment

Williamson suggests that the compiler, working from Haggai, Zechariah, and the Aramaic documents, placed Zerubbabel's work under Cyrus in order to show continuous obedience to the decree and to make the opposition of chapter 4 explain the delay. The ceremony itself is modelled on Chronicles' account of Solomon's Temple. Conservative scholars harmonize the sources by positing an initial foundation under Sheshbazzar and Zerubbabel in 537 and a renewed start in 520. That reading is possible but goes beyond the evidence; most historians date the effective rebuilding to 520–515 BCE.
