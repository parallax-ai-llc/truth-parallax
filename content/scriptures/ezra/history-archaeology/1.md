## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Moderate in substance, low in detail.** A Persian permission for Judeans to return and restore their temple fits what is known of Cyrus's policy. The wording of the decree, however, is Judean in theology, and the Cyrus Cylinder, often cited in support, says nothing about Judah or the Jews.

## What the Text Claims

- In his first year, Cyrus king of Persia issues a written proclamation that "the LORD, the God of heaven" has given him all kingdoms and charged him to build a house in Jerusalem (1:1–2).
- Any of God's people may go up to rebuild; their neighbours are to support them with silver, gold, goods, and livestock (1:3–4).
- Heads of families from Judah, Benjamin, the priests, and Levites prepare to go (1:5–6).
- Cyrus returns the Temple vessels taken by Nebuchadnezzar through **Mithredath the treasurer** to **Sheshbazzar, the prince of Judah** (1:7–8).
- An inventory lists 30 gold basins, 1,000 silver basins, and other items, totalling 5,400 (1:9–11).

## External Evidence

### The Cyrus Cylinder

The clay **Cyrus Cylinder**, written in Babylonian cuneiform after the capture of Babylon in 539 BCE, presents Cyrus as chosen by Marduk to replace the impious Nabonidus. Near its end Cyrus says he returned the gods of cities "beyond the Tigris" (naming places such as Ashur, Susa, Akkad, and Eshnunna) to their sanctuaries and gathered their former inhabitants back to their homes.

The Cylinder names **no** western city and does not mention Judah, Jerusalem, or Yahweh. Amélie Kuhrt showed that it follows established Mesopotamian royal conventions, comparable to Assyrian building inscriptions. It is propaganda addressed to Babylon, not a general human-rights charter or a decree of return for all exiles.

### Persian religious policy more broadly

Persian kings did support local cults when this served stability. Darius I funded Egyptian temples, and the Egyptian official **Udjahorresnet** records that Cambyses and Darius let him restore the temple of Neith at Sais. These parallels make royal backing for a Jerusalem temple credible in principle.

### Names and titles

- **Mithredath** is a good Persian name ("given by Mithra"), and a royal treasurer (*gizbar*, a Persian loanword) is an appropriate official.
- **Sheshbazzar** is a Babylonian name, probably *Šin-ab-uṣur* or similar. Some scholars identify him with **Shenazzar**, a son of Jehoiachin (1 Chr 3:18), but the linguistic match is disputed. Sara Japhet has argued for keeping him distinct from Zerubbabel.

## Points of Agreement

- Cyrus captured Babylon in 539 BCE, and his "first year" as king of Babylon (538 BCE) is a reasonable date for a decree.
- Allowing displaced groups and their cults to return is consistent with the Cylinder's stated policy toward Mesopotamian cities.
- Judean communities did exist in Babylonia, as the Al-Yahudu tablets ("town of Judah") from the 6th–5th centuries BCE confirm.
- Temple vessels as war booty, and their return as a sign of restoration, are well attested in Near Eastern practice.

## Points of Conflict

| Ezra 1 | Evidence and analysis |
|--------|-----------------------|
| Cyrus confesses that "the LORD, the God of heaven" gave him all kingdoms | The Cylinder credits Marduk. Persian kings honoured local gods, but this wording is Judean theology |
| Neighbours are commanded to supply the returnees | Echoes the "despoiling of the Egyptians" motif (Exod 3:21–22; 12:35–36), suggesting literary shaping |
| Itemized vessels total 2,499 | The stated total is 5,400 (1:11). 1 Esdras 2:13–14 gives different numbers |
| Decree in Hebrew addressed to Judeans | The parallel memorandum in Aramaic (6:3–5) concerns only rebuilding and funding, not return |

## Assessment

Ezra 1 is best read as the compiler's theological framing of a real policy. Elias Bickerman defended the Hebrew text as a genuine herald's proclamation in local idiom. Most critical scholars, following Kuhrt and Grabbe, regard the Hebrew form as a free composition modelled on Isaiah 44:28–45:1 and the Exodus tradition, while allowing that the Aramaic memorandum of chapter 6 may preserve an authentic core. The Cyrus Cylinder shows what kind of policy Cyrus advertised; it does not confirm this decree.
