## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Mixed.** Ephraim's boundary follows real geography, and the territory was the heartland of Iron I highland settlement. The note that the Canaanites of Gezer were not driven out is supported by the Merneptah Stele, the Amarna letters and 1 Kings 9:16. All of these show Gezer as a non-Israelite city until the 10th century or later.

## What the Text Claims

- The lot of the "sons of Joseph" runs from the Jordan at Jericho up through the hill country to Bethel, then west past Lower Beth-horon to Gezer and the sea (16:1–4).
- Ephraim's own boundary passes Ataroth-addar, Upper Beth-horon, Michmethath, Taanath-shiloh, Janoah and Tappuah, and follows the brook Kanah to the sea (16:5–9).
- "They did not drive out the Canaanites who lived in Gezer; so the Canaanites have lived within Ephraim to this day but have been made to do forced labour" (16:10).

## External Evidence

### Gezer

Gezer (Tell Jezer) was a major Canaanite city on the edge of the Shephelah.

- **Amarna letters:** Its rulers, including **Milkilu**, appear often as Egyptian vassals in the 14th century BCE.
- **Merneptah Stele** (c. 1208 BCE): Pharaoh boasts that "Gezer is seized," in the same passage that names "Israel" as a people.
- **1 Kings 9:16** says a pharaoh captured and burned Gezer and gave it to Solomon as a dowry. A 10th- or 9th-century gate and fortifications at Gezer are associated by some (e.g. Dever) with Solomon and dated later by others (e.g. Finkelstein).

All three sources agree that Gezer was not Israelite in the Late Bronze and early Iron Ages.

### Highland surveys

Israel Finkelstein's surveys of the hill country of Ephraim recorded a dramatic increase in small sites in Iron I, from a handful of Late Bronze sites to well over a hundred. Ephraim was the core of early Israel, and this allotment corresponds to the area where the settlement process is archaeologically clearest.

### Clan and place names

The "Archites" (16:2) reappear in David's adviser Hushai the Archite (2 Sam 15:32). The "Japhletites" (16:3) are otherwise unknown. Such obscure local names suggest that genuine traditional material underlies the boundary.

## Points of Agreement

- Gezer remained Canaanite and was subject to Egypt, not Israel, as Amarna, Merneptah and 1 Kings 9 all show.
- The boundary follows real topography, such as the Beth-horon ascent and the brook Kanah (Wadi Qanah).
- The Ephraim highlands were the demographic centre of Iron I Israel.

## Points of Conflict

| Joshua 16 | Historical record |
|----------|------------------|
| Gezer's Canaanites do forced labour for Ephraim | Gezer was an independent or Egyptian-controlled city until at least the 10th century |
| Joseph receives a single lot later split in two | Reflects later tribal genealogy of Ephraim and Manasseh |
| Allotment by Joshua | Boundaries probably reflect later administrative or tribal realities |

## Assessment

Joshua 16 is short but contains one of the book's most reliable notices. Gezer was not conquered, which directly undermines the claim of total victory in 10:33 and 12:12. The mention of forced labour reflects the Solomonic or later situation (1 Kgs 9:20–21), not Joshua's era.

The boundary description preserves accurate highland geography and some obscure clan names. It fits the archaeological picture of Ephraim as the cradle of early Israel.
