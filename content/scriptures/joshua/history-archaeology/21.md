## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Low to mixed.** The list of 48 Levitical cities uses real places. Scholars disagree on whether it reflects any historical arrangement. Some date it to the united monarchy, but many critical scholars see it as a late, schematic ideal. The closing summary of complete fulfilment (21:43–45) contradicts the book's own admissions.

## What the Text Claims

- The Levites ask for towns to live in, with pasture lands (21:1–3).
- Lots assign 13 towns to the Aaronic priests in Judah, Simeon and Benjamin, 10 to the rest of the Kohathites, 13 to the Gershonites and 12 to the Merarites (21:4–8).
- The towns are listed by tribe, four from each tribe in most cases, for a total of 48 (21:9–42).
- "Thus the LORD gave to Israel all the land... not one of all the good promises... had failed" (21:43–45).

## External Evidence

### Dating proposals

- **W. F. Albright** (1945) and **Benjamin Mazar** (1960) argued that the list reflects a real arrangement under David or Solomon. On this view, Levites were settled in frontier and newly annexed towns as royal administrators.
- **Julius Wellhausen** and many later critics regarded it as a post-exilic, utopian construction tied to the priestly laws of Numbers 35:1–8.
- **Nadav Na'aman** (1986) analysed the list as a composite drawn from other town lists in Joshua. **A. Graeme Auld** argued that the version in 1 Chronicles 6:54–81 preserves an earlier form.

### Archaeology of listed towns

The list includes towns occupied in very different periods, so no single period fits all of them. Some, such as **Gezer** (21:21) and **Taanach** (21:25), were non-Israelite during the early Iron Age. **Heshbon** (21:39) had no Late Bronze settlement. **Shechem** and **Hebron** also appear as refuge cities (21:13, 21).

### The Septuagint

The Septuagint adds after 21:42 a note that Joshua received Timnath-serah and that the flint knives of the circumcision (chapter 5) were placed there. It also differs in some town names. Both show that the list was still being edited.

## Points of Agreement

- Most named towns are real and identifiable, and many were significant Iron II centres.
- The idea that Levites lived scattered among the tribes, without a territory of their own, is consistent with Deuteronomy 18:6–8 and Judges 17:7–9; 19:1.

## Points of Conflict

| Joshua 21 | Historical and textual record |
|----------|-------------------------------|
| 48 cities, 4 per tribe, assigned by lot | Highly schematic; no narrative shows the system working |
| Gezer and Taanach given to Levites | Canaanite or Egyptian in the early Iron Age; Gezer Israelite only from the 10th century at the earliest |
| "Not one promise failed" (21:43–45) | Contradicts 13:1–6; 15:63; 16:10; 17:12 |

## Assessment

The chapter's symmetry is the strongest argument against reading it as a straightforward record: four towns from each tribe, 48 in total, matching the 1,000-cubit pasture scheme of Numbers 35. It may preserve memories of priestly families at certain towns, such as Anathoth (21:18), the home of Jeremiah's priestly family (Jer 1:1). As a whole, however, it reflects a priestly ideal.

The closing summary is a Deuteronomistic theological statement. Its claim of total fulfilment stands in deliberate tension with the realism of the allotment chapters.
