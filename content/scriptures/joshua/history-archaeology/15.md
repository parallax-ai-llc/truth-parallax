## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Moderate as a document of the late Judahite kingdom; low as a record of Joshua's era.** The town list divides Judah into administrative districts that correspond to the kingdom of the 8th–7th centuries BCE. It is a valuable historical source, but for a period six centuries after its narrative setting.

## What the Text Claims

- Judah's boundaries run from the Dead Sea and the wilderness of Zin to the Mediterranean, with a northern line passing just south of Jerusalem (15:1–12).
- Caleb takes Hebron, and Othniel captures Debir (Kiriath-sepher) and marries Caleb's daughter Achsah, who asks for springs (15:13–19; compare Judg 1:11–15).
- More than 100 towns are listed in regional groups: the Negev, the Shephelah, the hill country and the wilderness (15:20–62).
- "The Jebusites, the inhabitants of Jerusalem... live with the people of Judah in Jerusalem to this day" (15:63).

## External Evidence

### The district list

In 1925 **Albrecht Alt** argued that the towns in 15:21–62, together with Benjamin's in 18:21–28, form twelve administrative districts of the kingdom of Judah. He dated them to Josiah's reign. **Frank Moore Cross and G. Ernest Wright** (1956) proposed a 9th-century date under Jehoshaphat. **Nadav Na'aman** (1991) revived a late 7th-century date.

Key evidence for the late date is that several towns in the list were founded or flourished only in Iron II. The wilderness district (15:61–62), including Middin, Secacah and the "City of Salt," matches a line of Iron II forts and settlements in the Buqei'a valley and along the Dead Sea.

### Royal Judahite evidence

- The **lmlk** stamped jar handles (late 8th century BCE) name Hebron, Ziph and Socoh, all of which appear in the list as district centres or towns.
- The **Arad ostraca** (c. 600 BCE) show Judahite administration in the Negev. Several Negev towns in the list (15:21–32) were occupied in Iron II, not the Late Bronze Age.

### The Septuagint plus

After 15:59 the Septuagint has an additional district of eleven towns, including **Tekoa, Ephrathah (Bethlehem)** and Etam. Its absence from the Hebrew text is probably accidental, caused by a scribe's eye skipping. The plus completes Alt's twelve-district scheme.

## Points of Agreement

- The boundary description is geographically coherent. It follows real valleys and ridges, such as the Valley of Hinnom south of Jerusalem (15:8).
- Ekron, Ashdod and Gaza appear (15:45–47), but the text concedes only their "villages," consistent with Philistine independence.
- Jerusalem remaining Jebusite (15:63) agrees with Judges 1:21 and 2 Samuel 5:6–9.

## Points of Conflict

| Joshua 15 | Historical record |
|----------|------------------|
| Towns allotted in Joshua's time | Many sites founded or flourishing only in Iron II |
| Philistine cities within Judah's lot | Never controlled by Judah except briefly |
| Debir captured by Othniel | Debir's location (Khirbet Rabud or Tell Beit Mirsim) and conquest are uncertain |

## Assessment

Joshua 15 shows how administrative records from the late kingdom were projected back into the founding age. The list is one of the most important sources for Judah's historical geography. Alt's analysis, refined by Na'aman, remains the mainstream view.

The admission about Jerusalem in 15:63 preserves an older tradition in tension with the book's claims of total conquest. So does the folk tale of Achsah's springs, the "upper and lower basins" (*gullot*), which explains how a Calebite family held certain water sources near Debir.
