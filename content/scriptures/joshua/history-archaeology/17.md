## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Mixed.** The clan names of Manasseh are confirmed as real districts by the 8th-century Samaria ostraca. The admission that Israel could not take the valley cities, including Beth-shean, Megiddo, Taanach and Dor, matches archaeology and Egyptian sources. The chapter's framework of allotment by Joshua is later.

## What the Text Claims

- Manasseh's clans are Abiezer, Helek, Asriel, Shechem, Hepher and Shemida. Hepher's grandson Zelophehad had only daughters: Mahlah, Noah, Hoglah, Milcah and Tirzah, who receive an inheritance (17:1–6).
- Manasseh could not take Beth-shean, Ibleam, Dor, Endor, Taanach and Megiddo. "The Canaanites persisted in living in that land" until later put to forced labour (17:11–13).
- The tribe of Joseph complains that the land is too small. Joshua tells them to clear the forest in the hill country (17:14–18).
- The Canaanites in the valleys have "chariots of iron" (17:16, 18).

## External Evidence

### The Samaria ostraca

More than 100 ostraca from Samaria (early 8th century BCE) record deliveries of wine and oil from rural districts. The district names include **Abiezer, Helek, Asriel, Shechem and Shemida**, which are five of the six clans in 17:2. They also include **Noah and Hoglah**, two of Zelophehad's daughters in 17:3. Tirzah is a known city (Tell el-Far'ah North).

This shows that the genealogy of Manasseh is a map of real territorial units in the kingdom of Israel, expressed as kinship. The story of Zelophehad's daughters (compare Num 27; 36) turns place names into persons.

### The valley cities

- **Beth-shean** was an Egyptian garrison town from the 18th to the 20th dynasties. Stelae of Seti I and Ramesses II and a statue of Ramesses III were found there. It remained Egyptian until the mid-12th century.
- **Megiddo** Stratum VIIA continued as a Canaanite city with Egyptian ties until c. 1130 BCE or later.
- **Dor** was a coastal city linked to the Sea Peoples. The Egyptian tale of Wenamun (c. 1075 BCE) calls it a town of the Tjekker.
- **Taanach** appears in Thutmose III's account of the Megiddo campaign and in cuneiform letters found at the site.

The text's claim that these cities were not taken is thus confirmed.

### Clearing the forest

The instruction to clear woodland in the hill country (17:15, 18) fits Iron I archaeology. New highland villages expanded into wooded slopes, clearing land and building agricultural terraces and plastered cisterns, as Lawrence Stager and others have described.

## Points of Agreement

- Manasseh's clans correspond to real districts in the Samaria ostraca.
- The valley cities remained Canaanite or Egyptian into the 12th century.
- Settlement spreading into the forested highlands matches Iron I survey data.

## Points of Conflict

| Joshua 17 | Historical record |
|----------|------------------|
| "Chariots of iron" | Late Bronze chariots were wood and leather, sometimes with bronze fittings; iron becomes common only in Iron II |
| Daughters of Zelophehad as individuals | Their names include districts and a city, suggesting personified geography |
| Megiddo and Taanach defeated (12:21) | Contradicted here by 17:11–12 |

## Assessment

Joshua 17 is one of the more historically informative chapters in the book. The Samaria ostraca show that the clan list reflects 8th-century Israelite districts. The admission of unconquered valley cities matches the evidence that Egypt and Canaanite city-states held the lowlands into the 12th century.

The Joseph tribes' complaint preserves a memory of settlers clearing the highlands, which is exactly where archaeology finds early Israel. "Iron chariots" is probably a later writer's way of expressing Canaanite military superiority.
