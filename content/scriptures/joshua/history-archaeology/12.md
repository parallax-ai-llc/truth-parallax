## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Low to mixed.** The list of 31 defeated kings is a summary composition. It includes cities that the book itself says were not taken, and sites that were unoccupied in the Late Bronze Age. Its form resembles Egyptian lists of conquered towns, and many of its toponyms are real.

## What the Text Claims

- Moses defeated Sihon king of the Amorites at Heshbon and Og king of Bashan, "one of the remnant of the Rephaim," who lived at Ashtaroth and Edrei (12:1–6).
- Joshua defeated 31 kings west of the Jordan, including those of Jericho, Ai, Jerusalem, Hebron, Lachish, Gezer, Debir, Arad, Hormah, Bethel, Megiddo, Taanach, Hazor, Dor and Tirzah (12:7–24).

## External Evidence

### List form

The list resembles Egyptian topographical lists, such as the list of about 119 Canaanite towns that **Thutmose III** set up at Karnak after Megiddo (c. 1457 BCE), and the later list of **Sheshonq I** (c. 925 BCE). Such lists record claimed conquests in a set format and often include places that were never actually taken.

### Sites without Late Bronze occupation

- **Heshbon** (Tell Hesban): excavations by Andrews University found no Late Bronze occupation. Its earliest remains are Iron I. This undermines a Late Bronze king Sihon ruling there.
- **Arad** (Tel Arad): Ruth Amiran and Yohanan Aharoni found an Early Bronze city and an Iron Age fortress, with nothing in between. Some propose that Canaanite Arad lay elsewhere (e.g. Tel Malhata), but this is an attempt to save the text.
- **Jericho and Ai:** see chapters 6 and 8.

### Og and the Rephaim

Ugaritic texts (13th century BCE) link the god or deified ancestor **Rapiu** with **Ashtaroth and Edrei** (KTU 1.108). This is the same pair of towns Joshua gives for Og, "of the remnant of the Rephaim." Ashtaroth also appears in the Amarna letters. The biblical tradition therefore preserves an authentic old association of the Bashan region with the Rephaim, the shades of the dead, but in the form of legend about a giant king.

## Points of Agreement

- Most toponyms are real, and many can be identified.
- The Bashan tradition about Og draws on genuinely ancient regional lore attested at Ugarit.
- Tirzah (Tell el-Far'ah North), Megiddo, Taanach and Dor were all important Canaanite centres.

## Points of Conflict

| Joshua 12 | Record elsewhere |
|----------|------------------|
| Kings of Jerusalem, Gezer, Megiddo, Taanach and Dor defeated | The same book says these cities were not taken (15:63; 16:10; 17:11–12) |
| Sihon rules from Heshbon | No Late Bronze settlement at Tell Hesban |
| King of Arad defeated | Tel Arad unoccupied in the Late Bronze Age |
| 31 kings | The Septuagint list differs in places, indicating textual fluidity |

## Assessment

Joshua 12 is a scribal summary that turns the narratives of chapters 6–11, plus additional names, into a single register of victory. Some kings listed here appear nowhere in the stories. Its form follows the conventions of royal conquest lists, which were ideological documents.

Internal contradictions and site data show that the list cannot be taken as a record of actual conquests. Its value lies in its geography and in the old regional traditions it preserves.
