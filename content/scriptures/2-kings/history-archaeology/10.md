## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Moderate.** The massacres of the Omride family and of Baal worshippers cannot be checked directly. Their scale and theological framing are open to question. The chapter's closing notes are well founded, however. Jehu reigned in Samaria from about 841 BCE, paid tribute to Assyria, and lost Transjordan to Hazael, which matches Assyrian and archaeological evidence. The chapter is silent about the Assyrian submission, which the Black Obelisk shows.

## What the Text Claims

- Jehu demands the heads of **Ahab's seventy sons** from Samaria's elders; they are piled in two heaps at Jezreel's gate (10:1–11).
- He kills **42 kinsmen of Ahaziah** of Judah at Beth-eked (10:12–14).
- He allies with **Jehonadab son of Rechab** (10:15–17).
- He gathers all worshippers of Baal into the **temple of Baal in Samaria**, slaughters them, and turns the temple into a latrine (10:18–28).
- He keeps the golden calves of Bethel and Dan (10:29).
- **Hazael** strikes Israel throughout **Gilead**, "from Aroer by the Wadi Arnon" (10:32–33).
- Jehu reigns **28 years** (10:36).

## External Evidence

### Jehu and Assyria

- **Black Obelisk** (Nimrud): the second register shows a bearded figure prostrate before Shalmaneser III. The caption reads "tribute of **Jehu of Bit-Humri**": silver, gold, a gold bowl, gold vessels, tin, a royal staff and javelins (in paraphrase). It is the only known contemporary picture of an Israelite king.
- **Shalmaneser III's annals** for his 18th year (841 BCE) record that after besieging Hazael in Damascus, he marched to Mount Ba'li-ra'si and received tribute from Tyre, Sidon and **Jehu**.

### Hazael's conquests

Shalmaneser campaigned against Damascus again in 838 BCE and then turned elsewhere. That left Hazael free to expand. The archaeological picture fits Hazael's dominance in the late 9th century. Destruction or Aramean takeover is attested at sites such as Tel Dan, Hazor, Jezreel and Tell es-Safi (Gath, see chapter 12). In northern Transjordan, Mesha had already taken Israelite towns south of Gilead. The territory Hazael took between the Arnon and Bashan (10:33) is broadly consistent with this.

### "Son of Omri"

The Assyrians called Israel *Bit-Humri* (House of Omri) long after Omri's line ended. "Jehu of Bit-Humri" is therefore not a claim that Jehu descended from Omri. Older translations rendered it "son of Omri."

## Points of Agreement

- Jehu ruled Israel from about 841 BCE.
- Hazael dominated Israel and took Transjordan during Jehu's reign.
- Heaps of severed heads at a city gate (10:8) reflect real Near Eastern practice. Assyrian reliefs and annals of the 9th century depict such piles of heads.
- Keeping the Bethel and Dan shrines fits the continued use of Dan's sanctuary in the 9th–8th centuries, which excavation has shown.

## Points of Conflict

| 2 Kings 10 | Evidence or problem |
|------------|---------------------|
| Jehu's reign described with no mention of Assyria | The Black Obelisk records his submission and tribute in 841 |
| Baal worship eradicated from Israel | Hosea (8th century) still condemns Baal worship. Names with *Baal* appear in the 8th-century **Samaria ostraca** |
| Seventy sons and 42 kinsmen | Formulaic numbers (compare Judg 9:5; 2 Kgs 2:24) |
| Temple of Baal in Samaria | No Baal temple has been identified at Samaria |

## Assessment

The chapter is written to legitimize Jehu as the agent of prophetic judgment on Ahab's house (10:10, 17, 30). It then criticizes him for keeping Jeroboam's shrines. Outside sources show a different side: a usurper who immediately became an Assyrian tributary and then lost much of his kingdom to Damascus. The editor recorded the territorial losses (10:32–33) but not the submission. Either the editor's sources omitted it or it did not fit the narrative. The Baal purge is probably exaggerated, since Baal-names and Baal worship continued in the next century. Still, it may reflect a genuine move against the Tyrian-backed court cult of the Omrides.
