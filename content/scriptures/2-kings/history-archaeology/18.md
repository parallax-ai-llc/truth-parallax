## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: High.** Hezekiah's rebellion against Assyria, his dealings with the Philistines, Sennacherib's capture of Judah's fortified cities, and the payment of **30 talents of gold** are all confirmed. The sources are Sennacherib's own annals, the **Lachish reliefs** and the excavated destruction of **Lachish Level III**. The Rabshakeh's speech is a literary composition, but it uses accurate Assyrian titles and plausible arguments.

## What the Text Claims

- **Hezekiah** removes the high places and breaks up **Nehushtan**, the bronze serpent (18:4).
- He rebels against Assyria and strikes the Philistines as far as **Gaza** (18:7–8).
- A recap of Samaria's fall (18:9–12).
- In Hezekiah's **14th year**, **Sennacherib** takes all the fortified cities of Judah (18:13).
- Hezekiah, at **Lachish**, admits wrongdoing and pays **300 talents of silver and 30 talents of gold**, stripping the temple doors (18:14–16).
- Sennacherib sends the **Tartan, Rab-saris and Rabshakeh** to Jerusalem. The Rabshakeh speaks at the conduit of the upper pool in "Judean." He mocks reliance on Egypt as a "broken reed" and on YHWH (18:17–37).

## External Evidence

### Sennacherib's annals

Sennacherib's account of his third campaign (701 BCE) survives on the **Taylor Prism** (British Museum), the **Oriental Institute Prism** (Chicago) and the Jerusalem Prism. In paraphrase:

- Hezekiah of Judah did not submit. Sennacherib besieged and captured **46 of his strong walled cities** and countless villages, and took **200,150** people as spoil (a figure many think inflated).
- Hezekiah himself he shut up in Jerusalem "**like a bird in a cage**," surrounding the city with earthworks.
- He gave Hezekiah's towns to the kings of Ashdod, Ekron and Gaza.
- **Padi** king of Ekron, an Assyrian loyalist, had been handed over to Hezekiah and held in Jerusalem. Sennacherib restored him.
- Hezekiah later sent **30 talents of gold, 800 talents of silver**, precious stones, ivory furniture, his daughters and musicians to Nineveh.

### Lachish

- The **Lachish reliefs** from Sennacherib's Southwest Palace at Nineveh show the siege, the storming of the gate, impaled defenders, and families deported with their goods. A caption says Sennacherib sat on his throne while the spoil of Lachish passed before him.
- Excavations by David Ussishkin found **Level III** destroyed by fire, with the only Assyrian **siege ramp** yet excavated, a Judean counter-ramp, hundreds of arrowheads and sling stones.

### Hezekiah's preparations

- **LMLK jars**: hundreds of storage-jar handles stamped "for the king" (*lmlk*) with one of four place names (Hebron, Ziph, Socoh, *mmšt*). They are concentrated in Level III at Lachish and other sites destroyed in 701, and are widely linked to Hezekiah's war logistics.
- A royal **bulla of Hezekiah** ("Belonging to Hezekiah [son of] Ahaz, king of Judah") was found in 2015 in Eilat Mazar's excavation on the Ophel.
- The **Broad Wall** and **Siloam tunnel** are discussed in chapter 20.

### Cult reform

At **Beersheba**, a large horned altar was found dismantled, its stones reused in a later wall (Aharoni). At **Arad**, the revised stratigraphy of Ze'ev Herzog suggests that the shrine went out of use in the late 8th century. Both are often connected with Hezekiah's reform, but the dating is debated.

| Hezekiah rebels | Yes | Yes |
| Philistine involvement | Hezekiah strikes Philistia to Gaza | Hezekiah held Padi of Ekron; his towns are given to Philistine kings |
| Judah's fortified cities taken | "All" | 46 cities |
| Lachish as headquarters | Yes (18:14, 17) | Lachish reliefs |
| Gold tribute | 30 talents | 30 talents |
| Assyrian officials | Tartan, Rab-saris, Rabshakeh | *turtānu*, *rab ša rēši*, *rab šāqê* are genuine Assyrian titles |

## Points of Conflict

| 2 Kings 18 | Evidence or problem |
|------------|---------------------|
| 300 talents of silver | Sennacherib says 800, perhaps including other valuables or a different talent standard |
| Tribute paid before the envoys come | Sennacherib places it after his withdrawal, sent to Nineveh |
| Sennacherib's invasion in Hezekiah's "14th year" (18:13) | Implies accession in 715. But 18:1, 9–10 synchronize Hezekiah with Hoshea, implying accession around 727 |
| Rabshakeh's speeches | Free compositions with Deuteronomistic theology (18:22), though the arguments about Egypt are realistic |

## Assessment

18:13–16 is one of the clearest cases of a biblical passage matching a contemporary foreign record. Scholars often call it the "A" account. It is brief, unflattering to Hezekiah and accurate in its details. The speeches that follow belong to the "B" narratives, which were composed later to interpret the crisis theologically. Even so, they keep authentic elements, such as the Assyrian titles and the issue of an alliance with Egypt.
