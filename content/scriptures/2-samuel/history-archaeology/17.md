## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Mixed.** The war-council speeches are literary creations. The geographic and political framework is plausible: David retreats to Gilead and is supplied by Transjordanian allies, including an Ammonite. No external evidence touches the chapter.

## What the Text Claims

- **Ahithophel** advises an immediate strike with 12,000 men to kill David alone. **Hushai**, David's secret agent, persuades Absalom to wait and muster all Israel "from Dan to Beersheba" (17:1–14).
- Messengers hide in a well at Bahurim and warn David, who crosses the Jordan (17:15–22).
- Ahithophel goes home and hangs himself (17:23).
- At **Mahanaim**, David is supplied by **Shobi son of Nahash of Rabbah**, **Machir son of Ammiel of Lo-debar**, and **Barzillai the Gileadite** (17:24–29).

## External Evidence

- **Mahanaim** is often identified with Tell edh-Dhahab el-Gharbi on the Jabbok. The identification is probable but not certain. Survey and limited excavation indicate Iron Age occupation.
- **Shobi son of Nahash** would be a brother of the Hanun who insulted David's envoys (10:1–4). This implies that after Rabbah's capture (12:26–31) David installed a cooperative member of the Ammonite royal family. Such client-king arrangements are common, but Shobi is not attested outside the Bible.
- **"From Dan to Beersheba"** (17:11) is a stock formula for the ideal extent of Israel (compare 3:10; 24:2). Tel Dan in the 10th century was a modest settlement. Its monumental Israelite phase belongs to the 9th–8th centuries, the period of the Tel Dan Stele and the high place excavated by Biran. The formula may reflect later geography.

## Points of Agreement

- Retreat to Transjordan as a refuge recurs in Israelite history: Ishbaal at Mahanaim (2:8), and Jeroboam I's later building at Penuel (1 Kgs 12:25). It fits the geography of Gilead as a separate region with its own loyalties.
- The supplies listed (beds, basins, wheat, barley, flour, lentils, honey, curds, sheep) are realistic Iron Age produce.

## Points of Conflict

- **The council scene:** No one could have recorded a secret war council inside Absalom's camp. The paired speeches are rhetorical showpieces, like speeches in Greek historians.
- **Theological framing:** "The LORD had ordained to defeat the good counsel of Ahithophel" (17:14) is an explicit statement of divine causation by the narrator. Von Rad singled it out as the narrative's restrained theology. It is interpretation, not record.
- **Scale:** "All Israel… as the sand by the sea" (17:11) exaggerates the population base of Iron IIA Israel.

## Assessment

The chapter is a skillfully constructed contest of advisers, built to show divine providence working through human politics. Its geographic setting and its picture of a client network in Transjordan are credible. Its detailed content cannot be verified.
