## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Mixed.** That David made Jerusalem his seat is very probably historical. Jerusalem was the capital of the "House of David" by the 9th century. The account of the capture is too brief and obscure to reconstruct, however. The famous identification of the *tsinnor* with Warren's Shaft has been disproved, and the Hiram synchronism in 5:11 is chronologically difficult.

## What the Text Claims

- All the tribes anoint David king over Israel at Hebron. He is 30 and reigns 40 years: seven and a half in Hebron and 33 in Jerusalem (5:1–5).
- The **Jebusites** mock David ("the blind and the lame will turn you back"). David takes "the stronghold of Zion," and whoever strikes the Jebusites must go up "by the **tsinnor**" (5:6–9).
- **Hiram king of Tyre** sends cedar, carpenters, and masons to build David a house (5:11).
- A list of sons born in Jerusalem follows, then two victories over the Philistines in the **Valley of Rephaim** (5:13–25).

## External Evidence

### Jerusalem before David

The **Amarna letters** (14th c. BCE) include six letters from **Abdi-Heba**, ruler of *Urusalim*, to the pharaoh. They show Jerusalem as a Canaanite city-state centuries before David. The name **"Jebus"** for the city is not attested in any extra-biblical source. J. Maxwell Miller (1974) argued that the equation of Jebus with Jerusalem (Judg 19:10; 1 Chr 11:4) is a late biblical inference. The "Jebusites" may have been one population group among others.

### The *tsinnor* and Warren's Shaft

Charles Warren discovered a vertical rock shaft above the Gihon spring in 1867. In the 20th century it was widely identified with the *tsinnor*, the "water shaft" through which Joab supposedly entered the city (compare 1 Chr 11:6). Excavations by **Ronny Reich and Eli Shukron** from 1995 showed that the shaft is a natural karstic fissure. It was not reachable or usable in the Bronze and early Iron Ages, because the tunnel floor above it was lowered only later, probably in the 8th century BCE. Reich and Shukron found a different early water-access system: rock-cut channels and massive towers guarding the spring. They dated these to the Middle Bronze Age. Radiocarbon dating published in 2017 has suggested that some of the spring fortifications are later, and this dating is debated.

The meaning of *tsinnor* is itself uncertain. Proposed translations include "water channel," "gutter," "grappling hook," and "windpipe/throat," as in an order to strike the enemy at the throat.

### David's house

Eilat Mazar identified her **Large Stone Structure** in the City of David as the palace of 5:11. Critics, among them Finkelstein, Herzog, Singer-Avitz, and Ussishkin (2007), dispute both the dating and the identification (see the Overview).

### Hiram of Tyre

Josephus (*Against Apion* 1.117–126), citing Menander of Ephesus and the Tyrian annals, gives Hiram a reign of 34 years. He says the Jerusalem Temple was begun in Hiram's 12th year. Since 1 Kgs 6:1 dates the Temple to Solomon's 4th year, Hiram would have come to the throne only about eight years before Solomon. That would be late in David's 40-year reign. The embassy in 5:11, however, is placed immediately after the capture of Jerusalem, about 33 years before David's death. Proposed solutions:
- Chapters 5–8 are arranged by **theme**, not chronology. This is the most common view.
- The Tyrian or biblical chronologies are inexact.
- 5:11 is a projection of the later Hiram–Solomon alliance back onto David.

### The Philistine wars

The Valley of Rephaim, southwest of Jerusalem, is a natural approach from the Philistine plain. Iron Age farmsteads have been excavated there, but no battle evidence has been found. Gezer (5:25) shows Iron IIA occupation, and its dating is disputed in the low-chronology debate.

## Points of Agreement

- By the 9th century, Judah's kings ruled from Jerusalem as the "House of David" (Tel Dan Stele). A Davidic seizure of the city is therefore the simplest explanation.
- Jerusalem was a pre-Israelite Canaanite center (Amarna letters), so taking it rather than founding it fits the record.
- Phoenician craftsmanship in Israelite royal building is well attested in the 9th century, for example in Samaria's ashlar masonry.

## Points of Conflict

| 2 Samuel 5 | Historical record |
|------------|-------------------|
| Joab enters via the *tsinnor* (traditionally Warren's Shaft) | Warren's Shaft was not usable in the period (Reich & Shukron) |
| Hiram builds David's palace early in his Jerusalem reign | Tyrian chronology (Josephus/Menander) makes Hiram a contemporary of Solomon and of David's last years only |
| The city is called "Jebus" (1 Chr 11:4; Judg 19:10) | Extra-biblical sources call it Urusalim/Jerusalem only |
| Forty-year reign | A conventional round number (compare Saul, Solomon) |

## Assessment

The core of the chapter is that David moved his seat from Hebron to the old Canaanite town of Jerusalem. That claim fits everything later known about Judah's dynasty, and it is probably historical. The capture story is fragmentary and poorly preserved in the Hebrew text, and its topography cannot be reconstructed. The Hiram notice is best read as a thematic summary placed out of sequence.
