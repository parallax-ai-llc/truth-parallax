## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Mixed.** The outline, a victory over Edom followed by a disastrous war with Israel and Amaziah's assassination at Lachish, comes from Kings and is plausible. Joash of Israel is confirmed by an Assyrian stela. The Chronicler's additions, including a hired Israelite army sent home on a prophet's advice and the worship of captured Edomite gods, have no outside support and serve to explain Amaziah's defeat.

## What the Text Claims

- Amaziah executes his father's assassins but spares their children, citing the law of Moses (25:3–4; Deut 24:16).
- He musters 300,000 men of Judah and hires 100,000 Israelite soldiers for 100 talents of silver, then dismisses them after a man of God warns him (25:5–10).
- He kills 10,000 Edomites in the Valley of Salt and throws another 10,000 from "the top of the rock" (25:11–12).
- The dismissed Israelites raid Judahite towns from Samaria to Beth-horon (25:13).
- Amaziah brings back the gods of Seir and worships them (25:14–16).
- Joash of Israel defeats him at Beth-shemesh, breaks down 400 cubits of Jerusalem's wall, and takes Temple treasure and hostages (25:17–24).
- Amaziah is killed at Lachish after a conspiracy (25:27–28).

## External Evidence

### Joash of Israel

The **Tell al-Rimah Stela** of the Assyrian king Adad-nirari III (c. 796 BCE) lists tribute from "Joash (Iu'asu) the Samarian." This confirms that Amaziah's rival was a real king of Israel in the early 8th century, at a time when Assyrian pressure on Damascus allowed Israel to recover.

### Edom and "the rock"

The "rock" (Hebrew *sela*) is often linked with Sela, the Edomite stronghold of 2 Kgs 14:7. Proposed sites include es-Sela near Busayra and Umm el-Biyara at Petra. Crystal Bennett's excavations at Umm el-Biyara found a 7th-century Edomite settlement and a seal impression of Qos-gabar, king of Edom, known from Assyrian texts of the early 7th century. No early 8th-century remains connected with a Judahite attack have been identified. The Valley of Salt is usually placed south of the Dead Sea.

### Beth-shemesh and Lachish

Both sites were important Judahite towns in the 8th century, and the geography of a battle at Beth-shemesh on Judah's western approach is realistic. However, no destruction layer has been securely linked to the war with Joash.

## Points of Agreement

- Amaziah defeated Edom in the Valley of Salt (2 Kgs 14:7).
- He challenged Joash of Israel, was beaten at Beth-shemesh, and part of Jerusalem's wall was broken down (2 Kgs 14:8–14).
- He was killed at Lachish after fleeing a conspiracy (2 Kgs 14:19).
- Joash of Israel is confirmed by Assyrian evidence.

## Points of Conflict

| Element | Comment |
|---------|---------|
| 300,000 Judahite troops plus 100,000 mercenaries | Far beyond the population of 8th-century Judah, estimated by Magen Broshi and Israel Finkelstein at roughly 100,000 people |
| Hired Israelite army sent home | Not in Kings; serves the Chronicler's theme that alliances with Israel are sinful |
| Second 10,000 Edomites thrown from a cliff | Not in Kings; no evidence |
| Worship of Edomite gods | Not in Kings; supplies a reason for defeat in Chronicler fashion |
| Defeat as divine punishment (25:20) | Kings explains it by Amaziah's arrogance alone |

## Assessment

The parallel with 2 Kings 14 provides a firm frame, and the Assyrian mention of Joash anchors it in the early 8th century. The Chronicler's additions follow a clear pattern. Amaziah obeys a prophet and wins, then sins with foreign gods and loses. Army numbers are inflated throughout Chronicles, and most scholars do not take them literally. Conservative scholars sometimes read the Hebrew *'elef* ("thousand") as "military unit," which would reduce the numbers greatly. This proposal has some support but is not used consistently in Chronicles.

The quotation of Deuteronomy 24:16 in 25:4 also appears in Kings. It shows that both writers judged Amaziah by Deuteronomic law, which is likely later than his reign.
