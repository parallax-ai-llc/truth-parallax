## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Low to mixed.** The chapter rewrites the building list of 1 Kings 9:10–28 and makes it more ambitious. It turns the cities Solomon *gave* Hiram into cities Hiram gave Solomon, adds a conquest of Hamath-zobah, and places a building project at Tadmor (Palmyra) in the Syrian desert. Archaeology does not support a Solomonic presence in central Syria, and the debate over 10th-century building elsewhere remains unresolved.

## What the Text Claims

- Solomon rebuilds the cities **Huram gave him** and settles Israelites there (8:2).
- He conquers **Hamath-zobah** and builds **Tadmor** in the wilderness and store cities in Hamath (8:3–4).
- He fortifies Upper and Lower Beth-horon and Baalath (8:5–6).
- Non-Israelite peoples are made forced labour; Israelites are not (8:7–10).
- Solomon organises worship by the law of Moses and David's divisions (8:12–16).
- His ships sail from **Ezion-geber and Eloth** with Huram's sailors and bring 450 talents of gold from **Ophir** (8:17–18).

## External Evidence

### Tadmor

Tadmor (Palmyra) is attested very early. It appears in texts from Mari (18th century BCE) and in the inscriptions of the Assyrian king Tiglath-pileser I (c. 1100 BCE), as *Tadmar*. No Iron Age Israelite or Judean presence there is known. In 1 Kings 9:18 the written Hebrew text (*ketiv*) reads **Tamar**, a place in the southern Judean desert (compare Ezek 47:19). Most scholars think the Chronicler, or the tradition he followed, read the better-known Tadmor.

### Gates and fortifications

Yigael Yadin attributed the six-chambered gates at **Hazor, Megiddo and Gezer** to Solomon, based on 1 Kings 9:15. Israel Finkelstein's Low Chronology dates them to the 9th century, under the Omrides. Radiocarbon studies have narrowed but not settled the dispute. Amihai Mazar's Modified Conventional Chronology allows an Iron IIA range covering both centuries. Gezer's gate is still often dated to the 10th century. The Chronicler notably omits Hazor, Megiddo and Gezer, which Kings names.

### Ezion-geber and Ophir

Nelson Glueck identified Ezion-geber with **Tell el-Kheleifeh** near Aqaba and called it Solomon's copper refinery. Gary Pratico's re-examination of Glueck's finds (1993) showed the site's occupation began no earlier than the 8th century BCE. No 10th-century port has been found at the head of the Gulf of Aqaba.

An ostracon from **Tell Qasile** (8th century BCE) reads in part "gold of Ophir to Beth-horon, 30 shekels." This shows that "Ophir gold" was a recognised term in Iron Age Israel. Ophir itself has not been located; proposals include south-western Arabia, the Horn of Africa and India.

## Points of Agreement

- Beth-horon and Baalath are real places on the western approaches to Jerusalem.
- Phoenician partnership in Red Sea trade is plausible in principle, and Ophir gold was a known commodity.

## Points of Conflict

| 2 Chronicles 8 | Assessment |
|----------------|------------|
| Huram gives cities to Solomon (8:2) | Reverses 1 Kgs 9:10–14, where Solomon gives Hiram twenty Galilean towns |
| Conquest of Hamath-zobah (8:3) | Solomon's only recorded war; not in Kings, no evidence |
| Building Tadmor (8:4) | Kings reads Tamar; no Israelite remains at Palmyra |
| Ezion-geber port (8:17) | No 10th-century occupation known at Tell el-Kheleifeh |

## Assessment

The Chronicler tidies his source to remove anything unflattering, such as the giving away of Israelite land, and enlarges Solomon's realm to the borders of Lebo-hamath described in chapter 7. The material that might reflect real activity (the forts on the Beth-horon road, Red Sea trade) comes from Kings. For the 10th century, the archaeological picture is still disputed, but nothing supports a Judean empire reaching into central Syria.
