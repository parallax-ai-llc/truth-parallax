## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Moderate.** An Egyptian campaign by Sheshonq I (biblical Shishak) into Canaan in the later 10th century BCE is firmly attested by his own monument at Karnak. The Chronicler's additions to 1 Kings 14:25–28, especially the Libyan and other contingents in the Egyptian army, fit the 22nd Dynasty well. But the Egyptian list does not mention Jerusalem, and the theological framework of sin, repentance and partial rescue belongs to the Chronicler.

## What the Text Claims

- In Rehoboam's fifth year, because Judah had abandoned God's law, **Shishak** king of Egypt attacks Jerusalem (12:1–2).
- His army has **1,200 chariots, 60,000 horsemen** and countless troops of **Libyans, Sukkiim and Cushites** (12:3).
- He takes the fortified cities of Judah and reaches Jerusalem (12:4).
- When the princes humble themselves, God grants partial deliverance; Shishak carries off the Temple and palace treasures, including Solomon's gold shields (12:6–9).
- Rehoboam replaces the gold shields with bronze ones (12:10–11).

## External Evidence

### The Karnak relief

On the **Bubastite Portal** at the temple of Amun at Karnak, Sheshonq I (founder of the 22nd Dynasty, reigned c. 945–924 BCE on many reckonings) recorded a triumph over Asiatic peoples, with a list of about 150 place names in name-rings. Legible names include **Gezer, Aijalon, Beth-horon, Gibeon, Rehob, Beth-shan, Taanach, Megiddo, Mahanaim and Penuel**, and many sites in the **Negev**, among them a "Field of Abram" and several places called *hgr* ("fort").

### The Megiddo stele

A fragment of a victory stele bearing Sheshonq's name was found at **Megiddo** in the 1920s. It confirms that his army reached the Jezreel valley and set up a monument there.

### What the list does not show

- **Jerusalem is not named** in the legible portions, nor are most Judean hill-country towns. Many scholars think Rehoboam paid tribute to avoid a siege, as 12:9 implies.
- The heaviest focus falls on the **northern kingdom** (Jeroboam's realm) and the **Negev**, perhaps to control the Arabah copper trade.
- Destruction layers at several sites have been attributed to Sheshonq, but this depends on chronology and is debated.

### The troop contingents

The 22nd Dynasty was of **Libyan** origin, so Libyans (*Lubim*) in its army are natural. Many scholars identify the **Sukkiim** with the *Tjukten*, Libyan auxiliaries known from Egyptian texts of the New Kingdom and later. Nubians (Cushites) served in Egyptian armies throughout this era.

## Points of Agreement

- A campaign by Sheshonq I into Canaan around Rehoboam's time is confirmed, and it is the earliest event in the history of Judah attested outside the Bible.
- The army's makeup in 12:3 fits the 22nd Dynasty and may come from a good source.
- Tribute instead of conquest of Jerusalem is consistent with the Karnak list.

## Points of Conflict

| 2 Chronicles 12 | Assessment |
|----------------|------------|
| The campaign is aimed at Judah and Jerusalem (12:2–4) | Karnak emphasises the north and Negev; Jerusalem absent |
| Caused by Judah's sin, softened by repentance (12:1, 5–7) | The Chronicler's retribution scheme, not in Kings |
| 1,200 chariots and 60,000 horsemen (12:3) | Inflated; Egyptian cavalry on this scale is not attested for the period |
| "Rehoboam's fifth year" (12:2) | From 1 Kgs 14:25; consistent with a date of c. 925 BCE, but absolute dates depend on uncertain Egyptian and Israelite chronologies |

## Assessment

This chapter shows the Chronicler at his most useful and his most theological at once. He kept Kings' brief report and probably added accurate detail about the Libyan character of Sheshonq's army. He then framed the invasion as punishment and the partial rescue as reward for humility. The Karnak list confirms the event while suggesting that the Bible's Jerusalem-centred view gives an incomplete picture of a campaign that mostly struck Israel and the Negev.
