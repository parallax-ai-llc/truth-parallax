## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Mixed.** A palace coup that ended Athaliah's rule and restored a Davidic heir is plausible and comes from Kings. Chronicles, however, rewrites the event so that Levites and priests carry it out, replacing the foreign royal guard of Kings. That change reflects Second Temple rules of holiness, not 9th-century history.

## What the Text Claims

- In the seventh year, the priest Jehoiada makes a covenant with army commanders, who gather the Levites and heads of families from the cities of Judah (23:1–3).
- Priests and Levites coming on duty on the Sabbath guard the king; "no one shall enter the house of the LORD except the priests and ministering Levites" (23:4–7).
- Joash is crowned and given "the testimony"; Athaliah cries "Treason!" and is killed outside the Temple at the Horse Gate (23:11–15).
- Jehoiada makes a covenant; the people tear down the house of Baal and kill Mattan, its priest (23:16–17).
- Jehoiada restores the priestly and Levitical divisions "as David had ordained" (23:18–21).

## External Evidence

### No direct evidence

No inscription mentions Athaliah, Jehoiada or Joash's accession. The Temple Mount has never been excavated, so there is no archaeological evidence for the Temple, its gates, or a temple of Baal in Jerusalem.

### The religious setting

Inscriptions from **Kuntillet Ajrud** in the northern Sinai, dated to about 800 BCE, mention "Yahweh of Samaria and his Asherah" and "Yahweh of Teman and his Asherah," and one text mentions Baal alongside El. They show that worship in Israel and Judah in this period was more varied than the biblical writers approved. A Baal cult in Jerusalem under an Omride queen is therefore plausible, though not proven.

### The royal guard

In 2 Kings 11:4, 19 the coup relies on "the Carites and the guards." The Carites are often understood as foreign mercenaries, possibly Carians from Anatolia, though this is debated. Royal guards made up of foreign soldiers are well known in the ancient Near East.

## Points of Agreement

- Athaliah ruled Judah for about six years after Ahaziah's death, and was overthrown in favour of a boy king (2 Kgs 11).
- The priest Jehoiada led the coup.
- The cult of Baal promoted by the Omride dynasty was attacked at about the same time in both kingdoms (compare Jehu's purge, 2 Kgs 10).

## Points of Conflict

| 2 Kings 11 | 2 Chronicles 23 |
|------------|-----------------|
| Jehoiada works with the Carites and the guards | Jehoiada works with Levites and heads of families from all Judah |
| Soldiers enter the Temple | Only priests and Levites may enter (23:6) |
| Coup is an affair of the palace and guard | Coup becomes a national, cultic event with Davidic order restored |
| Covenant between king and people | Same, but with heavy emphasis on Levitical duties "as David ordained" |

## Assessment

Comparing the two versions is one of the clearest ways to see how the Chronicler worked. Faced with a story in which foreign soldiers entered the sanctuary, he replaced them with Levites so that the holiness rules of his own time were not broken. The roles assigned to the Levites, as gatekeepers, singers and guards in courses, match the Second Temple organisation described in 1 Chronicles 23–26.

Sara Japhet and H. G. M. Williamson both treat these changes as theological reworking of Kings rather than evidence from an independent source. The historical event, if accepted, should be reconstructed from Kings. Chronicles 23 is better evidence for the Temple personnel of Persian-period Jerusalem than for the 9th century.
