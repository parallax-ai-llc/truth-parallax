## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Low to mixed.** Jotham is a credible king, known as Uzziah's regent and Ahaz's father, but no contemporary inscription names him with certainty. The building work is partly confirmed by Kings. The war and tribute from Ammon appear only in Chronicles and have no external support.

## What the Text Claims

- Jotham becomes king at 25 and reigns 16 years; he does right but does not enter the Temple like his father (27:1–2).
- He builds the upper gate of the Temple and much on the wall of Ophel (27:3).
- He builds cities in the hill country and forts and towers in the wooded areas (27:4).
- He defeats the Ammonites, who pay 100 talents of silver, 10,000 cors of wheat and 10,000 of barley each year for three years (27:5).
- He becomes mighty "because he ordered his ways before the LORD" (27:6).

## External Evidence

### Inscriptions

No Assyrian text names Jotham. His son Ahaz appears in a summary inscription of Tiglath-pileser III as "Jehoahaz (Ia-u-ḫa-zi) of Judah," paying tribute about 734 BCE, which places Jotham's reign just before.

An unprovenanced clay bulla reading "Belonging to Ahaz son of Jotham, king of Judah" was published in the 1990s. It would mention Jotham, but because it came from the antiquities market its authenticity cannot be fully confirmed. A signet ring inscribed *lytm* ("belonging to Jotham"), found by Nelson Glueck at Tell el-Kheleifeh, has been linked to the king, but the name was common and the link is speculative.

### The Ophel

Excavations by Kathleen Kenyon and later Eilat Mazar on the Ophel, between the City of David and the Temple Mount, uncovered Iron Age II fortifications. They show that this area was fortified during the monarchy, though the dates and builders of specific walls are debated. Nothing ties them to Jotham.

### Ammon

Ammon was a growing kingdom in the 8th century, centred on Rabbah (modern Amman). A 100-talent tribute in silver, about three tonnes, is very large for a small kingdom and comparable to the tribute Assyria demanded from much larger states. No Ammonite or Assyrian source records Judahite control of Ammon.

## Points of Agreement

- Jotham reigned in Jerusalem as son of Uzziah and father of Ahaz (2 Kgs 15:32–38).
- Jotham "built the upper gate of the house of the LORD" (2 Kgs 15:35).
- Ahaz's appearance in Assyrian records confirms the succession.

## Points of Conflict

| Element | Comment |
|---------|---------|
| Ammonite war and tribute | Not in Kings; no external evidence; amount implausibly high |
| Building of cities and towers | Standard Chronicler motif for righteous kings |
| Peaceful, mighty reign | Kings mentions that Rezin and Pekah began attacking Judah in his days (2 Kgs 15:37) |
| 16-year reign | Fits only if it includes the co-regency with Uzziah; Judahite chronology of this period is notoriously difficult |

## Assessment

This short chapter shows the Chronicler's method in miniature. A good king is rewarded with building projects, a military victory and tribute. The note that Jotham did not enter the Temple contrasts him with his father and reinforces the lesson of chapter 26.

Chronologists such as Edwin Thiele (conservative position) reconstructed overlapping co-regencies to make the reign lengths of Kings work. Critical scholars such as Hayim Tadmor accept some co-regency but note that the biblical numbers cannot all be reconciled. The core facts about Jotham are credible, but the specific Chronicler additions remain unconfirmed.
