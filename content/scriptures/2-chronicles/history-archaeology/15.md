## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Low to mixed.** The prophecy of Azariah and the covenant assembly are unique to Chronicles and written in the Chronicler's style. One element, Asa's removal of the queen mother Maacah for making an Asherah image (15:16), comes from Kings and fits what archaeology reveals about the worship of Asherah in Judah and Israel.

## What the Text Claims

- The prophet **Azariah son of Oded** tells Asa that God is with him while he seeks God (15:1–7).
- Asa removes idols and repairs the altar; people from Ephraim, Manasseh and Simeon join Judah (15:8–9).
- In Asa's **15th year** an assembly sacrifices 700 oxen and 7,000 sheep and swears a covenant; whoever does not seek God is to be put to death (15:10–15).
- Asa deposes **Maacah**, his grandmother, for making an image for **Asherah**, and burns it in the Kidron valley (15:16).
- There is no war until Asa's **35th year** (15:19).

## External Evidence

### Asherah in Israel and Judah

- **Kuntillet 'Ajrud** (northern Sinai, c. 800 BCE): inscriptions on storage jars bless people "by Yahweh of Samaria and his Asherah" and "Yahweh of Teman and his Asherah."
- **Khirbet el-Qom** (near Hebron, 8th century BCE): a tomb inscription blesses a man "by Yahweh and his Asherah."
- Hundreds of **Judean pillar figurines**, mostly 8th–7th centuries BCE, are often linked to a goddess, perhaps Asherah.

This evidence shows that the worship of Asherah alongside Yahweh was common and could enjoy royal support, as the Maacah episode implies.

### The queen mother

The office of queen mother (*gebirah*) was an influential position at many ancient Near Eastern courts, including the Hittite court and Ugarit. Kings regularly records the mother of each Judean king, suggesting she had an official role.

### Covenant assemblies

The covenant renewal resembles Josiah's (2 Kgs 23:1–3) and Ezra's and Nehemiah's assemblies (Neh 8–10). The death penalty for not seeking God (15:13) echoes Deuteronomy 13 and 17:2–7. Ancient Near Eastern loyalty oaths, such as the **Succession Treaty of Esarhaddon** (672 BCE), provide the background for this kind of sworn commitment, which is usually dated to the 7th century or later in Judah.

## Points of Agreement

- Asa's action against Maacah's Asherah image is in Kings (1 Kgs 15:13) and fits Judean religious practice.
- Northern migrants moving to Judah is plausible in periods of instability in the north.

## Points of Conflict

| 2 Chronicles 15 | Assessment |
|----------------|------------|
| Azariah's prophecy (15:1–7) | Unknown outside Chronicles; a sermon in the Chronicler's style, echoing Jer 29:13–14 |
| Covenant with death penalty (15:12–15) | Modelled on Deuteronomic law and later covenant ceremonies |
| No war until Asa's 35th year (15:19) | 1 Kgs 15:16 has continual war with Baasha; and Baasha died in Asa's 26th year (1 Kgs 16:8) |
| Many from Ephraim and Manasseh join Asa (15:9) | Reflects the Chronicler's "all Israel" ideal |

## Assessment

The chapter is largely the Chronicler's construction, showing the right response to prophetic warning: seeking God, renewing the covenant, and removing idolatry. It includes a real tradition from Kings about Maacah, which archaeology makes easy to believe, since Asherah was widely worshipped in Israel and Judah. The dating notices (15th and 35th years) belong to the Chronicler's scheme for Asa's reign and conflict with Kings' chronology, as the next chapter makes clear.
