## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Moderate.** The names, the war with Aram at Ramoth-gilead, and the violent end of Ahaziah fit the 9th-century setting and are indirectly supported by the Tel Dan Stele. But Chronicles' version of Ahaziah's death differs from Kings, the Tel Dan Stele credits the killing to an Aramean king rather than Jehu, and the king's age in 22:2 is a textual error.

## What the Text Claims

- The people of Jerusalem make Ahaziah, the youngest son, king, aged **42** (22:1–2).
- His mother is Athaliah, "granddaughter" (literally "daughter") of Omri, who counsels him to do evil (22:2–4).
- He goes with Joram of Israel to fight Hazael of Aram at Ramoth-gilead; Joram is wounded and goes to Jezreel (22:5–6).
- Jehu, anointed to destroy the house of Ahab, kills Judahite princes, then finds Ahaziah hiding in **Samaria**, has him brought and killed, and he is buried (22:7–9).
- Athaliah destroys the royal family, but Jehoshabeath hides the infant Joash in the Temple for six years (22:10–12).

## External Evidence

### The Tel Dan Stele

Fragments of an Aramaic victory stele found at Tel Dan in 1993–1994 preserve the words of an Aramean king, most likely **Hazael**. The usual restoration reads that he killed "[Jeho]ram son of [Ahab], king of Israel" and "[Ahaz]iahu son of [Jehoram], king of the House of David." The phrase *bytdwd*, "House of David," is the earliest known extra-biblical reference to David's dynasty.

The restorations of the royal names are widely accepted but not certain, and a minority have questioned the reading "House of David." If the standard reading is right, the stele confirms the two kings and their deaths at the same time, but credits Hazael, not Jehu.

### Hazael and Ramoth-gilead

Hazael is well attested. Shalmaneser III's inscriptions call him "son of a nobody," that is, a usurper, and record Assyrian campaigns against him in 841 and 838 BCE. A war over Ramoth-gilead in Transjordan fits Aram's expansion in this period.

### Jehu

The **Black Obelisk** of Shalmaneser III (841 BCE) shows "Jehu son of Omri" (actually a usurper) bowing before the Assyrian king. Jehu's coup therefore happened by 841 BCE, matching the biblical timing.

## Points of Agreement

- Ahaziah of Judah and Joram of Israel were allies and died at about the same time in 841 BCE.
- Hazael of Aram was their enemy.
- Jehu took power in Israel and paid tribute to Assyria soon afterwards.
- The Tel Dan Stele confirms that Judah's kings were known as the "House of David" in the 9th century.

## Points of Conflict

| Issue | 2 Chronicles 22 | Other sources |
|-------|-----------------|---------------|
| Ahaziah's age | 42 (MT) | 22 in 2 Kgs 8:26; some ancient versions of Chronicles read 20 or 22. 42 would make him older than his father (2 Chr 21:20) |
| Place of death | Captured hiding in Samaria, killed | 2 Kgs 9:27: wounded near Ibleam, fled to Megiddo and died there |
| Burial | Buried by Jehu's men (place unstated) | 2 Kgs 9:28: carried to Jerusalem and buried in the city of David |
| Who killed him | Jehu | Tel Dan Stele: the Aramean king claims the kill |

The Tel Dan conflict can be reconciled in several ways. Jehu may have acted as Hazael's agent, or the Aramean king may have claimed credit for deaths that followed his victory. Ancient royal inscriptions often claimed others' deeds. Still, the two accounts differ.

## Assessment

This chapter rests on Kings, and its outline is historically sound. Chronicles compresses the story and moves Ahaziah's death to Samaria, perhaps to tie him more closely to the condemned house of Ahab. The impossible age of 42 is best explained as a scribal error.

The Tel Dan Stele is the key external witness. It shows that the deaths of the two kings were remembered outside Israel, but it also reminds us that the Bible tells the story from a single, prophetic point of view.
