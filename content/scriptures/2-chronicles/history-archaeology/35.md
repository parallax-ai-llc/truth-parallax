## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Moderate.** Josiah's death at Megiddo in 609 BCE, at the hands of Pharaoh Necho II, fits the international events recorded in the Babylonian Chronicle. But Chronicles' account of the battle differs from Kings in important ways, and its version, with a disguised king, archers, and a pharaoh speaking for God, is shaped by literary and theological models. The great Passover is a Chronicler expansion of a brief note in Kings.

## What the Text Claims

- Josiah keeps Passover in Jerusalem on the 14th of the first month, with priests and Levites in their divisions and the singers in their places "according to the command of David" (35:1–17).
- No Passover like it had been kept "since the days of Samuel the prophet" (35:18).
- Necho king of Egypt goes up "to fight at Carchemish on the Euphrates," and Josiah goes out against him (35:20).
- Necho sends envoys saying God has commanded him to hurry and warning Josiah not to oppose God (35:21).
- Josiah disguises himself, does not listen "to the words of Necho from the mouth of God," and fights in the plain of Megiddo (35:22).
- Archers wound him; he is moved to his second chariot, brought to Jerusalem, and dies there (35:23–24).
- Jeremiah utters a lament, and the singers still mourn him "to this day" (35:25).

## External Evidence

### The Babylonian Chronicle

The **Babylonian Chronicle** covering 616–609 BCE (Grayson's ABC 3) records the end of Assyria. After Nineveh fell in 612, the last Assyrian king, Ashur-uballit II, held out at Harran. In 610 the Babylonians took Harran, and in 609 "a great Egyptian army" crossed the Euphrates with Ashur-uballit to try to retake it. Egypt was then an ally of Assyria against Babylon.

This explains why Necho was marching north through the Levant in 609. 2 Kings 23:29 says Necho went up "to the king of Assyria" at the Euphrates. The older rendering "against the king of Assyria" is now known to be wrong, as the Chronicle shows Egypt supporting Assyria.

### Necho and Megiddo

Herodotus (2.159) says that Necho defeated the "Syrians" at "Magdolos," often taken as a garbled reference to Megiddo. Megiddo was a key pass on the coastal route to the north. No inscription mentions Josiah, and no destruction at Megiddo can be firmly linked to 609.

### Carchemish

The famous battle of Carchemish, in which Nebuchadnezzar defeated the Egyptians, took place in 605 BCE (ABC 5). Chronicles' mention of Carchemish in 609 may reflect the fact that Egypt used it as a base, or a later writer's association of Necho with that battle.

## Points of Agreement

- Josiah died at Megiddo in 609 BCE in a confrontation with Necho II, who was on his way north to the Euphrates.
- Necho's march coincided with the Egyptian–Assyrian campaign to retake Harran, as the Babylonian Chronicle shows.
- Josiah's death was a national trauma, remembered in lament (compare Zech 12:11).

## Points of Conflict

| Element | 2 Kings 23:29–30 | 2 Chronicles 35:20–25 |
|---------|------------------|------------------------|
| Necho's goal | "To the king of Assyria" at the Euphrates | "To fight at Carchemish" |
| Nature of encounter | Necho kills Josiah "when he saw him"; no battle described | A battle with archers, after Necho's warning |
| Place of death | Megiddo; body brought to Jerusalem | Wounded at Megiddo, dies in Jerusalem |
| Why he died | Not explained | He disobeyed God's word through Necho |

Kings may describe an execution or a meeting gone wrong rather than a battle. Some scholars think Josiah went to Megiddo as a vassal to meet the new Egyptian overlord and was killed.

## Assessment

Chronicles needed to explain why the most righteous king since David died violently, against its doctrine of retribution. Its answer is that Josiah disobeyed a divine message, even one delivered through a foreign king. The battle scene closely echoes the death of Ahab in 1 Kings 22:30–37, where the king disguises himself, is shot by an archer, and asks to be taken out of the battle in his chariot. Zipora Talshir and others see this as literary modelling rather than independent evidence.

The Passover description, with its Levitical organisation, reflects Second Temple practice. The historical core, Josiah's death confronting Necho at Megiddo in 609, is secure. The details of how it happened are uncertain.
