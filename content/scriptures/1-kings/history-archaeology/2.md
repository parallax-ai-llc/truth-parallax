## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Mixed.** The elimination of rivals after a contested succession is historically typical, and the flight of Shimei's slaves to Gath fits the Iron IIA political map. The chapter's attempt to blame the killings on David's deathbed instructions is apologetic, and the regnal total for David is schematic.

## What the Text Claims

- David instructs Solomon to deal with Joab and Shimei and to reward the sons of Barzillai (2:1–9).
- David dies after reigning forty years, seven in Hebron and thirty-three in Jerusalem (2:10–12).
- Adonijah asks for Abishag as his wife; Solomon treats this as a claim to the throne and has Benaiah kill him (2:13–25).
- Abiathar the priest is banished to Anathoth (2:26–27).
- Joab flees to the altar and is killed there; Benaiah becomes army commander and Zadok priest (2:28–35).
- Shimei is confined to Jerusalem, leaves to recover runaway slaves from **Achish son of Maacah, king of Gath**, and is executed (2:36–46).

## External Evidence

### Gath in the Iron IIA

Gath is identified with **Tell es-Safi**, excavated since 1996 by Aren Maeir. In the 10th and 9th centuries it was one of the largest cities in the southern Levant, perhaps 40–50 hectares, far larger than contemporary Jerusalem. It was destroyed in the late 9th century, probably by Hazael of Damascus (cf. 2 Kgs 12:17), and never fully recovered. A reference to a powerful king of Gath therefore fits the 10th–9th centuries much better than the 7th or 6th, when Gath was insignificant. This is one of the details suggesting that the Succession Narrative preserves early material.

### The name Achish

The name Achish is attested outside the Bible. The **Ekron royal dedicatory inscription** (found 1996) names *ʾkyš* (Ikausu in Assyrian records), son of Padi, as ruler of Ekron in the early 7th century BCE. The name is probably of Aegean or Anatolian origin, fitting Philistine ancestry. However, its 7th-century attestation means it could also have been familiar to a later writer. 1 Samuel 27:2 calls David's patron "Achish son of Maoch"; whether he is the same king is uncertain.

### Asylum at the altar

Joab's appeal to the altar and his killing there (2:28–34) raises the question of asylum. Exodus 21:14 denies asylum to a deliberate murderer, and the narrative frames Joab's killing of Abner and Amasa as exactly that. The chapter seems aware of the law, or of the custom behind it.

### Textual variants

The Septuagint adds two long passages after 2:35 and 2:46 (3 Kgdms 2:35a–o and 2:46a–l). They gather notices about Solomon's wisdom, buildings, and administration that appear elsewhere in the Hebrew text. They show that the Solomon material circulated in different arrangements.

## Points of Agreement

- Purges after a disputed succession are well attested. Assyrian, Hittite, and later Persian kings commonly eliminated brothers and their supporters.
- Requesting a former king's concubine as a bid for the throne parallels Absalom taking David's concubines (2 Sam 16:21–22) and Abner taking Saul's (2 Sam 3:7).
- Anathoth (Ras el-Kharrubeh or nearby ʿAnata) as a priestly village fits Jer 1:1, which makes Jeremiah a priest from Anathoth.

## Points of Conflict

| Claim | Comment |
|-------|---------|
| David orders the killings (2:5–9) | Transfers responsibility from Solomon to the dying king; a classic apologetic move |
| 40-year reign (2:11) | A conventional "generation," matching Solomon's 40 (11:42), Saul's partial 40 (Acts 13:21), and many judges |
| Abiathar's banishment "fulfills" the word against Eli (2:27) | A Deuteronomistic linking note, not an independent record |

## Assessment

The chapter reads as a straightforward account of a new king consolidating power by removing his brother, his brother's supporters, and a hostile member of the previous dynasty's clan. Historians generally consider this the most realistic part of the Solomon story. The framing, though, defends Solomon: David ordered it, each victim brought death on himself, and "the kingdom was established in the hand of Solomon" (2:46). Gath as a powerful independent kingdom is a genuinely early-looking detail. The chronological totals belong to the editor's scheme.
