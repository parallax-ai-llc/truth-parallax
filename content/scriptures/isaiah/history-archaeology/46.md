## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Mixed.** The chapter correctly names the two chief gods of Babylon, **Bel (Marduk)** and **Nebo (Nabu)**, and the practice of carrying their statues in procession. Its prediction that these gods would be carried off as captives on animals did not come true under Cyrus. Cyrus honoured Marduk and posed as his chosen servant.

## What the Text Claims

- "Bel bows down, Nebo stoops." Their images are loaded on beasts of burden and "go into captivity" (46:1–2).
- By contrast, YHWH has carried Israel from birth (46:3–4).
- People hire a goldsmith to make a god, then carry it on their shoulders (46:6–7).
- YHWH calls "a bird of prey from the east," the man of his purpose (46:11), meaning Cyrus.

## External Evidence

### Bel and Nebo

- **Bel** ("Lord") was the standard title of **Marduk**, god of Babylon, whose temple was the Esagila. **Nabu** (Nebo), his son, was god of writing, with his temple Ezida at Borsippa. Their names appear in royal names such as Nabû-kudurri-uṣur (Nebuchadnezzar) and Nabû-naʾid (Nabonidus).
- At the **Akitu** (New Year) festival, Nabu's statue travelled from Borsippa to Babylon and Marduk's statue was carried in procession. The **Nabonidus Chronicle** repeatedly notes that during the king's stay in Tayma "Nabu did not come to Babylon, Bel did not come out," so the festival was not held.

### The gods in 539 BCE

- The **Nabonidus Chronicle** reports that before Cyrus's arrival, the gods of several Babylonian cities were brought into Babylon, probably to protect them. It adds that the gods of Borsippa, Cutha and Sippar did *not* enter.
- After the conquest, Cyrus returned these gods to their cities (Nabonidus Chronicle; Cyrus Cylinder).
- The **Cyrus Cylinder** presents Cyrus as Marduk's chosen king who restored his worship. Cyrus and his son Cambyses took part in Babylonian rites.

## Points of Agreement

- The names, ranking and processional use of Bel and Nebo are exactly right for Neo-Babylonian religion.
- The image of statues being moved on carts may reflect what exiles had seen: statues taken in festival processions, or the evacuation of city gods into Babylon in 539 BCE.

## Points of Conflict

| Isaiah 46 | Historical record |
|-----------|-------------------|
| Bel and Nebo are carried away into captivity | Cyrus left Marduk's cult in place and claimed his patronage |
| The gods of Babylon are humiliated | Marduk and Nabu worship flourished under the Persians and continued into the Seleucid period |

Herodotus (1.183) says a later king, Xerxes, removed a statue from Marduk's sanctuary after Babylonian revolts in 484 BCE. Whether this was the cult statue of Marduk is debated (Kuhrt and Sherwin-White, 1987). In any case it happened more than 50 years after this oracle.

## Assessment

Isaiah 46 shows close knowledge of Babylonian religion, which supports a Babylonian setting for its author. It is a polemical expectation, written before 539 BCE, that Cyrus's conquest would humiliate Babylon's gods. Events went the other way: Cyrus entered Babylon as Marduk's champion. That outcome is good evidence that the oracle was not written after the event.
