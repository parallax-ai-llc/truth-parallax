## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: High (for the setting).** The chapter continues the Syro-Ephraimite crisis of c. 734–732 BCE. Its prediction that Damascus and Samaria would be plundered by Assyria was fulfilled within a few years, and its reference to the gently flowing waters of Shiloah fits Jerusalem's water system before Hezekiah's tunnel.

## What the Text Claims

- Isaiah writes "Belonging to Maher-shalal-hash-baz" ("Swift is booty, speedy is prey") on a large tablet before witnesses, **Uriah the priest** and Zechariah son of Jeberechiah (8:1–2).
- Before his son can say "Father" or "Mother," the wealth of Damascus and Samaria will be carried off to the king of Assyria (8:3–4).
- Because the people rejected "the waters of Shiloah that go gently," Assyria will flood Judah "up to the neck" (8:5–8).
- Isaiah seals his teaching among his disciples (8:16) and condemns consulting the dead (8:19).

## External Evidence

### Fulfilment by 732 BCE

A child learns to say "father" and "mother" within a year or two. Tiglath-pileser III took Damascus in 732 BCE and stripped Israel of Galilee and Gilead in 733–732 BCE, deporting part of the population (2 Kgs 15:29). Assyrian inscriptions describe both campaigns. The timescale fits a prophecy given c. 734 BCE.

### Uriah the priest

Uriah is presumably the same priest who built a new altar for Ahaz modelled on one in Damascus (2 Kgs 16:10–16). His appearance as Isaiah's witness is a small but coherent link between the two books.

### The waters of Shiloah

Before the Siloam Tunnel was cut (c. 700 BCE), water from the Gihon Spring was carried along the eastern slope of the City of David by an open or partly covered channel, often called **Channel II** (the "Siloam Channel"), which ran gently to the south. Isaiah's image of "waters that flow gently" fits this earlier system, and therefore a date before Hezekiah's works.

### Consulting the dead

Verse 19 condemns those who seek "mediums and spirits that chirp and mutter." Judahite burial customs (bench tombs with provisions for the dead) and texts such as 1 Samuel 28 point to real practices of ancestor veneration and necromancy in Iron Age Judah.

## Points of Agreement

- The prophecy's timeframe matches the Assyrian conquest of Damascus and northern Israel.
- The named witness Uriah fits the priest of Ahaz's reign known from Kings.
- The Shiloah image matches the pre-Hezekian water system.

## Points of Conflict

- No significant conflict. The flood imagery of 8:7–8 (Assyria reaching "up to the neck" of Judah) may reflect hindsight from 701 BCE, when Assyria overran Judah but did not take Jerusalem.

## Assessment

Chapter 8 belongs to the "Isaiah Memoir" (6:1–9:6) and is among the most securely 8th-century material in the book. The written tablet with witnesses (8:1–2) and the sealed testimony (8:16) also offer rare internal evidence for *how* prophetic words were recorded and preserved: in writing, among a circle of disciples, from the prophet's own lifetime.
