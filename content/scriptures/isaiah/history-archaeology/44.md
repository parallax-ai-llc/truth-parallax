## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Mixed (prophecy with checkable claims).** The chapter names **Cyrus** as YHWH's "shepherd" who will order Jerusalem rebuilt and the Temple's foundation laid (44:28). Cyrus did allow Judean return and Temple restoration, according to Ezra. But naming a Persian king about 150 years before his birth is the clearest internal sign that this part of Isaiah was written in Cyrus's own lifetime, not in the 8th century.

## What the Text Claims

- A long satire mocks an idol-maker who burns half a log for cooking and carves the other half into a god (44:9–20).
- YHWH "frustrates the omens of liars" and makes fools of diviners (44:25).
- YHWH says of Cyrus, "He is my shepherd, and he shall carry out all my purpose," saying of Jerusalem "It shall be rebuilt," and of the Temple "Your foundation shall be laid" (44:28).

## External Evidence

### Cyrus and the return

- **Ezra 1:2–4** (Hebrew) and **Ezra 6:3–5** (an Aramaic memorandum said to be found at Ecbatana) record Cyrus's permission to rebuild the Jerusalem Temple. Elias Bickerman (1946) argued that the Aramaic memorandum has the form of a genuine Persian administrative record. The Hebrew proclamation in Ezra 1, with its confession of "YHWH the God of heaven," is widely seen as a Judean rewriting.
- **The Cyrus Cylinder** (see chapter 45) shows Cyrus restoring cult statues and deportees in Mesopotamia. It does not mention Judah or Jerusalem.
- **Josephus** (*Antiquities* 11.1.2, 11.5–6) claims that Cyrus read his own name in Isaiah's prophecy and was moved to free the Jews. No Persian source supports this. Historians treat it as Jewish legend built on this very verse.

### Mesopotamian divination

The "omens" and "diviners" of 44:25 refer to the vast Babylonian divination industry: liver omens (extispicy), celestial omens (*Enūma Anu Enlil*), and the professional scholars who interpreted them. Thousands of such texts survive.

## Points of Agreement

- Cyrus became ruler of Babylon in 539 BCE and, according to the biblical record, authorized the return. The broad policy is consistent with what the Cylinder shows of his approach to subject peoples' cults.
- A Second Temple was in fact built, though only completed in 515 BCE under Darius I (Ezra 6:15).

## Points of Conflict

| Isaiah 44 | Historical record |
|-----------|-------------------|
| Cyrus will rebuild the city and lay the Temple's foundation | The Temple project stalled for nearly 20 years (Ezra 4:24; Haggai 1). Jerusalem's walls were not rebuilt until Nehemiah, c. 445 BCE |
| The idol satire implies worshipers think the wood itself is a god | Mesopotamian theology distinguished the god from the statue, though the statue, once ritually animated, was treated as the god's living presence |

## Assessment

For mainstream scholarship, the naming of Cyrus settles the date of this section: it was written c. 545–539 BCE by someone who knew of Cyrus and hoped he would free the exiles. The text's expectation was broadly but not fully met.

**Conservative view.** **Oswalt**, **Motyer** and **E. J. Young** hold that Isaiah of Jerusalem named Cyrus by divine revelation, and they point to 1 Kings 13:2, where Josiah is named centuries in advance, as a parallel. They argue that ruling out such prediction is a philosophical assumption, not a historical finding.

**Critical reply.** Historians cannot evaluate miracles, but they can note that the Cyrus oracles speak of him as a present, active figure whose victories are already known (41:2–3; 45:1–4). The 1 Kings 13 passage is itself widely regarded as a later insertion from Josiah's time. On ordinary historical method, a text naming a king and his deeds is dated to that king's time.
