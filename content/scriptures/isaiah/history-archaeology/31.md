## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: High (for the setting).** Like chapter 30, this short oracle opposes the alliance with Egypt in 705–701 BCE. Egypt's reputation for horses and chariots is well attested in Assyrian documents. The prediction that Assyria would fall "by a sword not of man" anticipates the deliverance story of chapter 37.

## What the Text Claims

- Woe to those who go down to Egypt for help and rely on **horses**, chariots, and horsemen (31:1).
- "The Egyptians are human, and not God; their horses are flesh, and not spirit" (31:3).
- God will protect Jerusalem like a lion over its prey or like hovering birds (31:4–5).
- "Assyria will fall by a sword, not of man" (31:8).

## External Evidence

### Egyptian and Kushite horses

Egypt, and particularly Kush, was known for large horses suitable for chariotry. The **Nimrud Horse Lists**, administrative texts from the reign of Sargon II, include a class of chariot horses called *kusaya*, usually understood as "Kushite." Stephanie Dalley has shown how these horses were prized. Sargon also received horses as tribute from Egypt. Judah's interest in Egyptian horses (compare Deut 17:16; 1 Kgs 10:28) is therefore realistic.

### The defeat of Egyptian forces

Sennacherib's annals record his victory over Egyptian and Kushite chariotry at **Eltekeh** in 701 BCE (see chapter 30), confirming the futility of trusting in Egypt's horses.

### "A sword not of man"

Verse 8 predicts Assyria's fall by divine rather than human means. This anticipates the story of the angel striking the Assyrian camp (37:36). Assyrian records give no hint of such a disaster, though Sennacherib did withdraw without taking Jerusalem (see chapter 37). Assyria as an empire eventually fell to Babylonians and Medes in 614–609 BCE — by a very human sword.

## Points of Agreement

- Judah's reliance on Egyptian chariotry is consistent with Assyrian evidence on Kushite and Egyptian horses.
- Egypt's failure to protect Judah is confirmed by Sennacherib's annals.
- Jerusalem was spared in 701 BCE, as 31:5 expects.

## Points of Conflict

- "Assyria will fall by a sword not of man" was not fulfilled in any historically verifiable sense in 701 BCE. Sennacherib withdrew with tribute, and Assyria remained dominant for nearly another century.

## Assessment

Chapter 31 is widely regarded as authentic Isaianic material from 705–701 BCE. Verses 1–3 sum up the prophet's political position: reliance on Egyptian military power is a betrayal of trust in God. Verses 5–9 may have been shaped or expanded by later editors who knew the tradition of Jerusalem's miraculous deliverance.
