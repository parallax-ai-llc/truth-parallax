## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Not applicable (prophecy).** Chapter 65 divides the community into YHWH's faithful "servants" and apostates who sit among tombs, eat pork and set tables for the gods **Gad** ("Fortune") and **Meni** ("Destiny"). It ends with "new heavens and a new earth." It reveals a post-exilic community split into factions, and it shows early signs of apocalyptic thinking.

## What the Text Claims

- A rebellious people sacrifice in gardens, sit in tombs, spend nights in secret places and eat swine's flesh (65:3–4).
- Some "set a table for Fortune [Gad] and fill cups of mixed wine for Destiny [Meni]" (65:11).
- YHWH's servants will eat and rejoice while the apostates go hungry and are shamed (65:13–15).
- YHWH creates "new heavens and a new earth" (65:17). Long life, peace, and "the wolf and the lamb shall graze together" (65:25).

## External Evidence

### Gad and Meni

- **Gad** as a god of luck is well attested in West Semitic sources: Phoenician and Punic names, the place-names Baal-Gad (Joshua 11:17) and Migdal-Gad (Joshua 15:37), and later Aramaic and Palmyrene inscriptions where *gd* is a protective "Fortune."
- **Meni** is less certain. Some scholars connect it with the Arabian goddess **Manāt**, known from much later sources. Others relate it to the Hebrew root *mnh*, "to count/allot." The identification is not settled.

### The pig taboo

Zooarchaeological studies show that pigs were rare at Iron Age sites in the Judean highlands but more common at Philistine and some northern sites (Sapir-Hen et al., 2013). Eating pork as a mark of apostasy (65:4; 66:17) fits a community where avoiding pork was already a sign of identity. The theme became central in the Maccabean period (1 Maccabees 1:47; 2 Maccabees 6–7).

### Reuse of First Isaiah

Isaiah 65:25 quotes Isaiah 11:6–9 almost word for word ("the wolf and the lamb… they shall not hurt or destroy in all my holy mountain"). This kind of deliberate reuse is evidence that chapters 56–66 were written by authors who knew earlier parts of the book and were shaping it as a whole.

## Points of Agreement

- West Semitic worship of Gad is well documented.
- Pork avoidance as a Judean marker is supported by animal bone evidence.

## Points of Conflict

None directly. The promised longevity and peace (65:20–25) are, naturally, unrealized.

## Assessment

Chapter 65 is a key text for the internal divisions of the post-exilic community. Paul Hanson read it as the voice of a marginalized prophetic group opposed to the Temple leadership. Others see the "servants" as a pious group without such a sharp class division. The "new heavens and new earth" is an early step toward the apocalyptic worldview found in later Jewish texts and in Revelation 21:1.
