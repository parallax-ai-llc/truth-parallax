## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Not applicable (visionary oracles), with real settings that are hard to date.** The cry "Fallen, fallen is Babylon" (21:9) could refer to Babylon's fall to Cyrus in 539 BCE or to one of its Assyrian conquests (710, 703, or 689 BCE). The short oracles on Arabian peoples (21:11–17) name real places and tribes well attested in Assyrian and Babylonian sources.

## What the Text Claims

- "The oracle concerning the **wilderness of the sea**": a harsh vision comes; "Go up, O **Elam**; lay siege, O **Media**" (21:1–2).
- A watchman sees riders and announces: "Fallen, fallen is **Babylon**, and all the images of her gods lie shattered" (21:9).
- An oracle on **Dumah**: "Watchman, what of the night?" (21:11–12).
- An oracle on Arabia: caravans of **Dedan**, the people of **Tema** bringing water, and the end of the glory of **Kedar** within a year (21:13–17).

## External Evidence

### "Wilderness of the sea"

The phrase probably plays on the **Sealand** (*mat tamti*), the marshy region of southern Babylonia. It was the home base of **Merodach-baladan** (Marduk-apla-iddina II), the Chaldean leader who repeatedly seized Babylon from Assyria (see chapter 39).

### Which fall of Babylon?

- **710 BCE:** Sargon II drove Merodach-baladan from Babylon.
- **703 BCE:** Sennacherib defeated Merodach-baladan and his **Elamite** allies.
- **689 BCE:** Sennacherib sacked Babylon after a revolt supported by Elam.
- **539 BCE:** Cyrus of Persia took Babylon (Nabonidus Chronicle; Cyrus Cylinder). Persia had absorbed **Media**, and Elam (Anshan) was the Persian royal homeland.

"Elam and Media" attacking Babylon fits 539 BCE best, and most scholars date the oracle to the 6th century. A minority argue for an 8th-century setting, since Elam was then Babylon's *ally* against Assyria, and read the oracle differently. The shattering of Babylon's gods (21:9) does not fit 539 BCE, when Cyrus honoured Marduk, but it does fit 689 BCE, when Sennacherib boasted of destroying Babylon's gods.

### Arabian peoples

- **Dumah** is Dumat al-Jandal in northern Arabia, the *Adummatu* of Assyrian texts. Sennacherib and Esarhaddon campaigned there and took its gods.
- **Kedar** (*Qedar*) was a powerful Arab confederation frequently mentioned in Assyrian royal inscriptions of the 8th–7th centuries BCE.
- **Tema** (Tayma) was an important oasis. The Babylonian king **Nabonidus** lived there for about ten years (c. 552–543 BCE).
- **Dedan** (al-'Ula) was a caravan centre on the incense route.

## Points of Agreement

- All the Arabian place names are real and important in the period.
- The pairing of Elam and Media against Babylon accurately reflects the Persian conquest.

## Points of Conflict

- The date and occasion of the Babylon oracle are uncertain. If it refers to 539 BCE, the shattered gods do not match the record of Cyrus's respectful treatment of Babylon's cults.

## Assessment

Chapter 21 shows how difficult it can be to date short oracles. It contains accurate geography and plausible situations, but competing settings fit different details. Most critical scholars place 21:1–10 in the mid-6th century, alongside chapter 13 and the Babylon oracles of Jeremiah 50–51. The Arabian oracles may come from the Assyrian period or from the time of Nabonidus's stay at Tema.
