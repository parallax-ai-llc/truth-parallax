## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Mixed to high (for the historical setting).** Isaiah 45 calls Cyrus YHWH's *mashiach*, "anointed" (45:1), whose hand YHWH grasps and before whom gates open. The **Cyrus Cylinder** uses strikingly similar language about Marduk choosing Cyrus, and confirms that Babylon opened to him without a fight. The chapter is a genuine 6th-century text reflecting real events and real Persian propaganda.

## What the Text Claims

- YHWH has grasped Cyrus's right hand, subdues nations before him, and opens doors so that "gates shall not be closed" (45:1).
- YHWH will break bronze doors and iron bars and give Cyrus hidden treasures (45:2–3).
- YHWH calls Cyrus by name "though you do not know me" (45:4–5).
- YHWH forms light and darkness, makes peace and creates evil/woe (45:7).
- Cyrus will build YHWH's city and free the exiles "not for price or reward" (45:13).

## External Evidence

### The Cyrus Cylinder (British Museum, found 1879)

- Written in Akkadian after the conquest of 539 BCE in the voice of Cyrus and the Babylonian priesthood. It accuses Nabonidus of neglecting Marduk.
- Marduk "searched" for a righteous king, **took the hand of Cyrus, king of Anshan, and called him by name** for rule over everything.
- Marduk made Cyrus enter Babylon **"without fighting or battle."**
- Cyrus returned gods to sanctuaries in cities across the Tigris (Ashur, Susa, Agade, Eshnunna, Der and others) and **gathered their former inhabitants and returned them to their homes.**
- Fragments of a clay tablet copy of the same text, identified in the British Museum in 2010, show that it circulated beyond the cylinder.

### Babylon's gates

The **Nabonidus Chronicle** records that Cyrus defeated the Babylonian army at Opis, then took Sippar and Babylon in October 539 BCE "without battle." Herodotus (1.179) describes Babylon's walls as having a hundred bronze gates.

## Points of Agreement

- **Divine election language.** Both texts present Cyrus as chosen by a god who takes his hand and calls his name. Most scholars see a shared Mesopotamian royal idiom; some (e.g., Morton Smith) suggest Deutero-Isaiah knew Persian propaganda directly.
- **"Though you do not know me"** (45:4) matches reality: Cyrus credited Marduk in Babylon and the moon god Sin in inscriptions from Ur, not YHWH.
- **Opened gates** (45:1) fits a city that surrendered rather than fell by siege.
- **Repatriation policy.** The Cylinder shows Cyrus returning displaced peoples and gods, which makes the Judean return historically plausible.

## Points of Conflict

| Isaiah 45 | Historical record |
|-----------|-------------------|
| Cyrus acts "for the sake of Jacob" (45:4) | The Cylinder names only Mesopotamian cities east of the Tigris. No source outside the Bible mentions Judah |
| Bronze doors broken (45:2) | Babylon was entered without assault |
| Egypt and Cush will submit to Israel (45:14) | Never happened |

### Was Cyrus specifically pro-Judean?

Older scholarship called the Cylinder a charter of religious freedom. **Amélie Kuhrt** (1983) showed that it follows standard Mesopotamian conventions for a new king and that its restoration clauses are local to Babylonia. Persian rulers did support local cults when it served stability, and the Judean return fits that pattern. But the idea of a special Persian favour toward the Jews comes from Ezra and Isaiah, not from Persian sources.

### Light and darkness (45:7)

Some scholars read 45:7 as a rebuttal of Persian dualism (a good god of light opposing an evil one). Others reply that early Achaemenid religion is poorly documented and that Zoroastrian dualism in its developed form is attested later. The link is possible but unproven.

## Assessment

Isaiah 45 is one of the best-anchored prophetic texts in the Bible. It was clearly written by someone aware of Cyrus's propaganda and conquests, around 539 BCE. Calling a foreign king "messiah" is an extraordinary theological move, and its closest parallel is the Babylonian priests' own claim about Marduk.
