## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Not applicable (prophecy), with a recognisable setting.** The siege of "Ariel" (Jerusalem) followed by a sudden deliverance (29:1–8) fits the events of 701 BCE. The word *Ariel* itself has an intriguing parallel in the 9th-century Moabite **Mesha Stele**.

## What the Text Claims

- "Woe, **Ariel**, Ariel, the city where David encamped!" (29:1).
- God will besiege the city, bring it down to the dust (29:2–4).
- Suddenly, the enemy multitude will vanish like a dream (29:5–8).
- The prophetic vision is like a **sealed book** that no one can read (29:11–12).
- The people honour God with their lips while their hearts are far away (29:13).
- Those who hide their plans from the LORD are condemned (29:15).
- In a little while Lebanon will be a fruitful field, the deaf will hear, and Jacob will no longer be ashamed (29:17–24).

## External Evidence

### "Ariel"

In Ezekiel 43:15–16 *'ari'el* means the **altar hearth**. The Mesha Stele (c. 840 BCE, line 12) says Mesha took from Ataroth the **'r'l dwdh**, probably an "altar-hearth of Dawdoh" (or of "its beloved"), and dragged it before the god Chemosh. The term was therefore a real cultic word in the region. Isaiah applies it to Jerusalem as the city of God's altar, with a pun: the city will become "like an altar hearth" (29:2), a place of burning.

### The siege and its sudden end

Sennacherib's annals say that in 701 BCE he shut Hezekiah in Jerusalem "like a bird in a cage," set up blockades against the city, and made it difficult for anyone to leave. But they do not claim to have taken it. Hezekiah instead sent heavy tribute to Nineveh. The idea of a sudden lifting of the siege (29:5–8) matches the outcome, though the annals present it as a negotiated submission and the Bible as a divine rescue (see chapter 37).

### Hidden plans

Verse 15 ("Woe to those who hide deep from the LORD their counsel") is often linked to secret negotiations with Egypt during 705–701 BCE, the same policy condemned in chapters 30–31.

## Points of Agreement

- Jerusalem was besieged but not captured in 701 BCE, as the oracle expects.
- The word *Ariel* as an altar term is attested independently in Moabite.

## Points of Conflict

- None external.
- Verses 17–24 (transformation of nature, healing of the deaf and blind) share language with chapters 35 and 40–55 and are widely viewed as a later addition.

## Assessment

The core of chapter 29 (29:1–8, 13–16) is commonly accepted as Isaianic, from around 701 BCE. Some scholars think the motif of a sudden rescue (29:5–8) was added after the event, as part of a "Jerusalem deliverance" tradition that grew up during Josiah's reign (R. E. Clements). Others see it as Isaiah's own expectation. Either way, the outcome — a city humbled but spared — matches the historical record.
