## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Not applicable (prophecy).** A speaker anointed by YHWH's spirit proclaims liberty to captives and "the year of the LORD's favour." The chapter makes no historical claims. Its main historical significance lies in its long and well-documented reception, from the Dead Sea Scrolls to the Gospel of Luke.

## What the Text Claims

- "The Spirit of the Lord GOD is upon me, because the LORD has anointed me" to bring good news to the poor, liberty to captives and comfort to mourners (61:1–3).
- The returnees will rebuild ancient ruins (61:4).
- Foreigners will tend Israel's flocks and fields, and Israel will be "priests of the LORD" (61:5–6).
- YHWH will make an everlasting covenant with them (61:8).

## External Evidence

### Anointing and release

- The prophet's "anointing" is unusual. Kings and priests were anointed, and prophets only rarely (1 Kings 19:16). The speaker is usually taken to be the prophet of Trito-Isaiah.
- "Proclaim liberty" (*deror*) uses the language of the jubilee year (Leviticus 25:10). It is related to Akkadian *andurāru*, the royal cancellation of debts and release of debt slaves, attested in Mesopotamian edicts (for example the Edict of Ammi-ṣaduqa).

### Reception in the Dead Sea Scrolls and the New Testament

- **11QMelchizedek (11Q13)** combines Isaiah 61 with the jubilee laws of Leviticus 25 to describe an end-time release by a heavenly Melchizedek.
- **4Q521** ("Messianic Apocalypse") says the Lord will "proclaim good news to the poor," echoing 61:1.
- **Luke 4:16–21** has Jesus read 61:1–2 in the Nazareth synagogue and claim its fulfilment.

## Points of Agreement

The use of release and debt-cancellation language is well grounded in ancient Near Eastern law and practice.

## Points of Conflict

None. The promise that foreigners will labour for Israel (61:5) was not realized in the Persian period.

## Assessment

The chapter is a prophetic self-commissioning, probably from the late 6th or early 5th century BCE. The Qumran texts show that by the 1st century BCE it was being read as an end-time and messianic text. That background helps explain how the passage was used in Luke.
