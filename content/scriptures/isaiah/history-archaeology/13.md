## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Low (as prediction).** The oracle expects Babylon to be destroyed by the **Medes** and left permanently uninhabited. In fact Babylon was taken in 539 BCE by the Persian Cyrus II, essentially without a fight, and remained a major inhabited city for centuries. The oracle's concern with Babylon and the Medes points to a 6th-century date, not the 8th.

## What the Text Claims

- The superscription: "The oracle concerning Babylon which Isaiah son of Amoz saw" (13:1).
- God musters an army from a distant land for the Day of the LORD; the heavens darken (13:2–16).
- "I am stirring up the **Medes** against them, who have no regard for silver" (13:17).
- Babylon will be like Sodom and Gomorrah, never again inhabited; no Arab will pitch a tent there and wild animals will live in its palaces (13:19–22).

## External Evidence

### Who conquered Babylon?

- The **Nabonidus Chronicle** (ABC 7) records that in October 539 BCE "the army of Cyrus entered Babylon without battle."
- The **Cyrus Cylinder** presents Cyrus as welcomed by the god Marduk and the citizens, entering "without fighting."

Cyrus was Persian. He had absorbed the Median kingdom c. 550 BCE, and Medes served in his army, so "Medes" could be loosely used for his forces (compare Jer 51:11, 28). But there was no destruction.

### Babylon after 539 BCE

Babylon remained a royal capital under the Persians. It revolted against Darius I and Xerxes I and was punished, but was not abandoned. Alexander the Great planned to make it his capital and died there in 323 BCE. Under the Seleucids it declined as Seleucia on the Tigris took over, and by the Roman period it was largely deserted. The desolation came gradually, over some six centuries.

### The 689 BCE alternative

**Sennacherib** did sack Babylon in 689 BCE, flooding the city, and his inscriptions boast of its destruction. A minority of scholars connect some of the oracle's imagery to that event, though Assyrians, not Medes, carried it out. Babylon was rebuilt by Esarhaddon.

## Points of Agreement

- Babylon did fall, and Medes were part of the force that ended the Neo-Babylonian empire.
- Babylon's eventual desolation is real, though centuries later and through decline rather than conquest.

## Points of Conflict

| Isaiah 13 | Historical record |
|-----------|-------------------|
| The Medes destroy Babylon | Persian Cyrus II takes it peacefully, 539 BCE |
| Slaughter of the population, children dashed to pieces (13:16) | No massacre; Cyrus claims a peaceful entry |
| Never again inhabited | Inhabited and important until the Hellenistic era |

## Assessment

In the 8th century BCE, Babylon was usually an ally of Judah against Assyria (see chapter 39), and the Medes were remote. An oracle expecting Babylon's overthrow by the Medes makes sense only in the 6th century, when Babylon was Judah's oppressor and Median power was a recent memory. Most scholars therefore date 13:2–22 to c. 550–540 BCE, before Cyrus actually took the city. The attribution to Isaiah in 13:1 is an editorial heading.

Conservative scholars argue that Isaiah foresaw events beyond his time. Even on that view, the specifics of destruction by the Medes and immediate desolation did not occur as described.
