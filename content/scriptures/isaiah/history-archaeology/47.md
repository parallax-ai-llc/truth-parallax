## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Low (as a prediction).** This taunt song pictures Babylon stripped, shamed, widowed and destroyed "in a moment, in one day" (47:9). In fact Babylon surrendered to Cyrus in 539 BCE without a battle and remained a leading city for two more centuries. The poem's knowledge of Babylonian divination is accurate, but its forecast is not.

## What the Text Claims

- "Virgin daughter Babylon" must sit in the dust, grind meal and be exposed (47:1–3).
- Babylon showed no mercy to Israel and made the yoke heavy on the aged (47:6).
- Loss of children and widowhood will come on her in one day (47:9).
- Her sorcerers, astrologers and "monthly prognosticators" cannot save her (47:12–15).

## External Evidence

### How Babylon actually fell (539 BCE)

- **Nabonidus Chronicle (ABC 7):** Cyrus fought the Babylonian army at **Opis** on the Tigris and plundered it. On 14 Tashritu Sippar was taken "without battle," and on 16 Tashritu (12 October 539 BCE) Ugbaru and the army of Cyrus "entered Babylon without battle." Cyrus arrived on 3 Arahsamnu, and "green twigs were spread before him." The Chronicle records that the temples' rites continued without interruption.
- **Cyrus Cylinder:** Marduk let Cyrus enter Babylon "without fighting or battle," and the people welcomed him.
- **Herodotus (1.191)** describes a siege ended by diverting the Euphrates. This later account is not supported by the cuneiform sources.

### Babylon after 539 BCE

- Babylon became a Persian royal residence and capital of a wealthy satrapy.
- The **Behistun inscription** of Darius I records two Babylonian revolts in 522–521 BCE (Nidintu-Bel and Arakha, each claiming to be "Nebuchadnezzar"). Another revolt against Xerxes followed in 484 BCE.
- Alexander the Great made Babylon a capital and died there in 323 BCE. The city declined only gradually after the founding of Seleucia on the Tigris (c. 305 BCE).

### Babylonian divination

The astrologers and "those who make predictions month by month" (47:13) accurately describe Babylonian scholarly practice. The celestial omen series *Enūma Anu Enlil*, and the **Astronomical Diaries** kept in Babylon from at least the 7th century BCE, are exactly this kind of monthly sky-watching.

## Points of Agreement

- Babylon's dependence on omen science and astrology is correctly described.
- Babylon did lose its independence and its native kingship in 539 BCE. In that limited sense its "queenship" (47:5) ended.

## Points of Conflict

| Isaiah 47 | Historical record |
|-----------|-------------------|
| Sudden catastrophe "in one day" with loss of children (47:9, 11) | Peaceful surrender, no sack, no massacre in the city |
| Babylon humbled into slavery | Babylon stayed rich and important under Persian rule |
| Its diviners are helpless and discredited | Babylonian astronomy and astrology flourished into the Hellenistic era |

## Assessment

The poem is a war taunt written before the fall, probably c. 545–540 BCE, when an attack by Cyrus looked likely. Had it been written after 539, its author would surely have known the city surrendered quietly. Its hopes for Babylon's destruction were not realized then, though Babylon's later decline allowed later readers to see the prophecy as fulfilled in the long run. Historically, it is a vivid record of exilic resentment of Babylon, not a description of what happened.
