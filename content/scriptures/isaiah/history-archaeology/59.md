## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Not applicable (prophecy).** Chapter 59 explains why salvation has been delayed: the community's injustice. It moves from accusation to confession to a vision of YHWH as a warrior putting on armour to deliver Zion. It makes no historical claims, but its mood of disappointment fits the early post-exilic period.

## What the Text Claims

- YHWH's arm is not too short to save; the people's sins have separated them from him (59:1–2).
- Violence, lies and corrupt courts are widespread (59:3–8).
- The community confesses: "We wait for light, and behold darkness" (59:9–15).
- YHWH puts on righteousness as a breastplate and comes as Redeemer to Zion (59:16–20).
- His spirit and words will stay with the people forever (59:21).

## External Evidence

- **Delayed restoration:** The grand promises of Isaiah 40–55 had not been realized. Haggai 1:6–11 describes drought and poor harvests, and Zechariah 1:12 asks how long YHWH will withhold mercy from Jerusalem. Isaiah 59's "we wait for light" belongs to the same disillusionment.
- **Social injustice:** Nehemiah 5 records exploitation of the poor by "nobles and officials," confirming the kind of abuses condemned here.
- **Divine warrior imagery:** Pictures of a god arming for battle are common in ancient Near Eastern literature. The motif later shaped Wisdom of Solomon 5:17–20 and Ephesians 6:14–17.

## Points of Agreement

The disappointment and social tension in the chapter are consistent with Haggai, Zechariah and Nehemiah.

## Points of Conflict

None.

## Assessment

Chapter 59 is a theological response to the failure of the exilic hopes, best dated in the late 6th or 5th century BCE. It explains the gap between prophecy and reality as the community's own fault. Historically, that gap is real: the restoration was modest.
