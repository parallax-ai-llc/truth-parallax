## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Not applicable (poetry/prophecy).** This short chapter uses legal images (a certificate of divorce, sale to creditors) and contains the third Servant Song (50:4–9), in which the servant is beaten and insulted but trusts in YHWH. It makes no historical claims. Its legal metaphors match real ancient Near Eastern practice.

## What the Text Claims

- YHWH asks where Zion's "bill of divorce" is, or to which creditor he sold her children (50:1). Exile was punishment, not divorce.
- The servant was given "the tongue of those who are taught," gave his back to those who struck him and his cheeks to those who pulled out the beard (50:4–6).
- The servant is confident of vindication (50:7–9).

## External Evidence

### Divorce documents and debt slavery

- Deuteronomy 24:1–4 requires a written certificate of divorce.
- The **Elephantine papyri** (5th century BCE) include Judean marriage contracts with divorce clauses, for example in the archive of Mibtahiah.
- A Jewish bill of divorce from Wadi Murabba'at (Mur 19, 1st or 2nd century CE) shows the form of such documents later.
- Selling children to pay debts is attested in Mesopotamian law (Code of Hammurabi §117) and in the Bible (2 Kings 4:1; Nehemiah 5:5).

### The suffering of the servant

Beard-plucking and spitting (50:6) were standard acts of humiliation in the ancient Near East. The passage may reflect the real persecution of a prophet or of the faithful, but no external source names anyone.

## Points of Agreement

The legal imagery is accurate to the period's practice, and the servant's mistreatment fits a prophet unpopular with his own community, as Jeremiah was (Jer 20; 37–38).

## Points of Conflict

None.

## Assessment

The third song is the most clearly individual and autobiographical of the four. Many critical scholars therefore take the servant here to be the prophet himself, speaking of opposition he met among the exiles. Others continue to read it collectively of Israel. This is an interpretive, not historical, question.
