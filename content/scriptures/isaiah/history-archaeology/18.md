## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: High (for the setting).** The chapter presupposes envoys from **Cush** (Nubia) seeking allies in Judah. This fits the late 8th century exactly, when the Kushite **25th Dynasty** ruled Egypt and was organising resistance to Assyria.

## What the Text Claims

- A land "of whirring wings beyond the rivers of Cush" sends ambassadors "by sea, in vessels of papyrus on the waters" (18:1–2).
- They are "a people tall and smooth, feared near and far, a nation mighty and conquering, whose land the rivers divide" (18:2, 7).
- Isaiah tells them to go back: God will act in his own time (18:4–6).
- At that time gifts will be brought to Mount Zion from this people (18:7).

## External Evidence

### The Kushite 25th Dynasty

Kings from Napata in Nubia (modern Sudan) conquered Egypt in the late 8th century. **Piye** (c. 744–714 BCE) campaigned as far as the Delta c. 728 BCE, and his **Victory Stela** (found at Gebel Barkal, now in Cairo) describes the conquest. His successors **Shabaka** and **Shabataka** (Shebitku) ruled all of Egypt, followed by **Taharqa** (690–664 BCE). Under them Egypt opposed Assyria in Philistia and Judah. In 701 BCE an Egyptian–Kushite army fought Sennacherib at **Eltekeh**, according to his annals.

### Diplomacy with the Levant

Assyrian texts show Kushite rulers involved in the politics of the Levant. Sargon II's **Tang-i Var inscription** says the king of Meluhha (Kush), **Shapataku**, extradited the rebel Iamani of Ashdod c. 707–706 BCE (see chapter 20). Envoys seeking support for anti-Assyrian alliances in Jerusalem are therefore entirely plausible.

### "Tall and smooth"

Greek writers also describe the Ethiopians (Kushites) as the tallest and most handsome of peoples (Herodotus 3.20). Egyptian and Assyrian art depicts Kushites with distinctive features. The papyrus boats fit Nile travel.

## Points of Agreement

- A powerful Kushite kingdom with interests in Palestine existed exactly when Isaiah was active.
- Kushite diplomacy against Assyria is attested in Assyrian sources.
- The description of the people matches ancient ethnographic stereotypes of the Kushites.

## Points of Conflict

- None with the external record.
- Verse 7, with its tribute to Zion, is generally regarded as a later addition reflecting the hope that nations would bring gifts to Jerusalem (compare 45:14; 60:5–9).

## Assessment

Isaiah 18 is one of the clearest pieces of evidence that First Isaiah reflects real 8th-century international politics. It fits the years c. 713–701 BCE, when Judah was being courted to join revolts supported by the Kushite pharaohs. Its message (wait, do not join the coalition) matches Isaiah's warnings against relying on Egypt in chapters 20 and 30–31.
