## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Not applicable (oracle).** Like chapter 15, this chapter laments a disaster in Moab that cannot be dated. Its main historical interest is the postscript (16:13–14), which openly updates an earlier prophecy with a new deadline — direct evidence that prophetic texts were reused and revised.

## What the Text Claims

- Moab is told to send a lamb (tribute) "from Sela, by way of the wilderness, to the mount of the daughter of Zion" (16:1).
- Moabite refugees ask Judah for shelter. A just Davidic ruler will reign once the oppressor is gone (16:3–5).
- Moab's pride is condemned. The vineyards of **Sibmah** and the fields of **Heshbon** and **Kir-hareseth** are mourned (16:6–11).
- Postscript: "This is the word that the LORD spoke concerning Moab in the past. But now … within three years, like the years of a hired worker, Moab's glory will be brought into contempt" (16:13–14).

## External Evidence

### Moabite tribute

2 Kings 3:4 says Mesha of Moab paid Israel tribute in lambs and rams. Moab paid tribute to Assyria: **Salamanu** of Moab appears in Tiglath-pileser III's tribute list (c. 734 BCE), and **Kammusu-nadbi** of Moab in Sennacherib's annals (701 BCE). A call for Moab to send a lamb to Zion assumes a time when Judah could expect such tribute, but no record confirms it.

### Viticulture

Moab's hills were known for vineyards (16:8–10). The Mesha Stele refers to the region's towns, and archaeological survey on the Moabite plateau has found agricultural installations, though few can be closely dated. Sibmah has not been securely identified.

### The parallel in Jeremiah 48

Verses 6–11 reappear, with variations, in **Jeremiah 48:29–36**. This shows that the lament over Moab circulated in more than one prophetic collection.

## Points of Agreement

- Moab's tribute relationship with Israel, Judah, and Assyria is well attested.
- The place names are genuine Moabite toponyms.

## Points of Conflict

- None external. Whether the "three years" deadline of 16:14 was fulfilled cannot be checked, because the occasion is unknown.

## Assessment

Verses 13–14 are among the clearest examples in the Hebrew Bible of a prophecy being **reapplied**. A later prophet or editor states plainly that the older oracle was spoken "in the past" and sets a new, short deadline. Historians see this as evidence that prophetic oracles were living texts that were updated when circumstances changed. Some scholars link the postscript to an Assyrian campaign under Sargon II or Sennacherib, but this is speculation.
