## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Not applicable (lament/oracle), with accurate geography.** The towns of Moab named in the chapter are real, and several are also named in the 9th-century **Mesha Stele**. But the disaster described cannot be tied to a specific datable event.

## What the Text Claims

- **Ar** and **Kir** of Moab are laid waste in a night (15:1).
- Mourners go up to **Dibon** and **Nebo** and wail over **Medeba**, with shaved heads and sackcloth (15:2–3).
- **Heshbon**, **Elealeh**, and **Jahaz** cry out; refugees flee to **Zoar**, **Eglath-shelishiyah**, the ascent of **Luhith**, and the road to **Horonaim** (15:4–5).
- The waters of **Nimrim** are desolate; the waters of **Dimon** are full of blood (15:6–9).

## External Evidence

### The Mesha Stele

The **Mesha Stele** (Moabite Stone), found at Dhiban (biblical Dibon) in 1868 and now in the Louvre, records the deeds of King Mesha of Moab (c. 840 BCE). It names several towns that appear in Isaiah 15–16, including **Dibon, Nebo, Medeba, Jahaz,** and **Horonaim**, as well as Aroer and Kiriathaim. This confirms that the place names in Isaiah reflect genuine Moabite geography.

### Archaeology of Moab

Excavations at **Dhiban** (Dibon) have uncovered Iron Age remains, including monumental structures probably connected to Mesha's building works. Many other sites, such as Madaba (Medeba), Hesban (Heshbon), and Kerak (often identified with Kir-hareseth), have Iron Age levels. Some identifications (for example Luhith, Eglath-shelishiyah, Dimon) are uncertain.

### Mourning customs

Shaved heads, cut beards, sackcloth, and wailing on rooftops (15:2–3) are standard West Semitic mourning rites, attested in the Bible (Jer 48:37; Ezek 7:18) and in Ugaritic literature, where the god El mourns Baal by gashing himself.

### Which disaster?

Moab was attacked or threatened repeatedly: by Israel in the 9th century (2 Kgs 3), by Assyria (it paid tribute to Tiglath-pileser III and Sennacherib), by Arab tribes in the 7th century, and by Babylon in the 6th century (Josephus, *Antiquities* 10.181, reports Nebuchadnezzar subdued Moab in 582 BCE). Scholars have proposed each of these settings. None can be confirmed.

## Points of Agreement

- The geography is accurate and partly confirmed by the Mesha Stele and modern survey.
- The mourning practices match well-attested regional customs.

## Points of Conflict

- None with external sources, but the chapter cannot be dated with confidence.

## Assessment

Isaiah 15–16 is closely paralleled by **Jeremiah 48** (for example 15:2–7 and 16:6–11 reappear in Jer 48:29–38). Either one borrowed from the other or both draw on an older lament about Moab. This shared material suggests that oracles against Moab circulated independently and were attached to more than one prophetic book. The geography is genuine, but the event is unknown. The poem is best read as a stylised lament over the ruin of a neighbouring people.
