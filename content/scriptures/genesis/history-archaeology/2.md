## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Not applicable (non-historical genre: origin myth).** Genesis 2 is a second, independent creation story with its own order of events. Eden cannot be located on any real map, and the creation of one man from dust and one woman from his side is contradicted by human genetics and palaeoanthropology. The chapter draws on Mesopotamian motifs but uses them to tell a distinctively Israelite story.

## What the Text Claims

- God ends the work of creation and rests on the seventh day (2:1–3, the close of the Priestly account).
- "These are the generations of the heavens and the earth" (2:4a) marks a transition. A new account begins in 2:4b, when no plants exist and no rain has fallen.
- The LORD God forms the man (*adam*) from the dust of the ground (*adamah*) and breathes life into him (2:7).
- God plants a garden "in Eden, in the east," with the tree of life and the tree of the knowledge of good and evil (2:8–9).
- A river flows out of Eden and divides into four: Pishon, Gihon, Tigris, and Euphrates (2:10–14).
- Animals are formed and named; the woman is built from the man's side or rib (2:18–23).

## External Evidence

### Two creation accounts

Genesis 2:4b–25 differs from chapter 1 in style, vocabulary, and sequence. It uses the divine name "YHWH Elohim" rather than "Elohim." Here the man is created **first**, then the garden and plants, then the animals, and finally the woman. In chapter 1, plants and animals come before humans, who are created male and female together. Source critics traditionally assign 2:4b–3:24 to the **Yahwist (J)** source. Its date is now debated: older scholarship placed it in the 10th–9th centuries BCE, while many current scholars place it in the late monarchy or the exile.

### Humans from clay

Forming humans from earth is a widespread ancient motif:

- In **Atrahasis** (Old Babylonian, c. 1700 BCE), the mother goddess mixes clay with the flesh and blood of a slain god to make the first humans.
- In Egyptian tradition, the god **Khnum** shapes people on a potter's wheel.
- In **Gilgamesh** Tablet I, the goddess Aruru pinches off clay to create the wild man Enkidu.

The wordplay *adam*/*adamah* ("human"/"ground") is Hebrew, but the idea is shared.

### Eden and the four rivers

The Tigris (*Ḥiddeqel*) and Euphrates are real Mesopotamian rivers. The Pishon and Gihon are not identifiable. Gihon "goes around the whole land of Cush" (2:13), which elsewhere means Nubia, south of Egypt; Gihon is also the name of Jerusalem's spring (1 Kgs 1:33). Havilah (2:11) is placed in Arabia in Genesis 10:7, 29. The geography thus merges Mesopotamia, Arabia, Africa, and Jerusalem into an idealised world-centre rather than describing a real place.

The word *Eden* may be related to Sumerian/Akkadian *edin(u)*, "steppe," or to a West Semitic root meaning "abundance." An Aramaic–Akkadian bilingual statue inscription from Tell Fekheriye (9th century BCE) uses the root *ʿdn* for making the land abundant. Neither link proves a location.

### Paradise traditions

The Sumerian myth **Enki and Ninhursag** describes Dilmun (usually identified with Bahrain) as a pure land without sickness or death. Samuel Noah Kramer once proposed that the woman made from Adam's rib echoes a Sumerian pun on *ti* ("rib" and "life") in this myth. Most scholars now regard that suggestion as unproven.

### Human origins

Genetic and fossil evidence shows that *Homo sapiens* emerged in Africa roughly 300,000 years ago from earlier hominins and has never descended from a single pair. Genetic diversity in modern populations requires an ancestral population in the thousands at every stage. "Mitochondrial Eve" and "Y-chromosomal Adam," sometimes cited in popular discussions, are statistical common ancestors who lived tens of thousands of years apart and alongside many other humans.

## Points of Agreement

- The text knows real Mesopotamian rivers and a Near Eastern tradition that civilisation and paradise lay "in the east."
- Its imagery fits ancient Near Eastern ideas of sacred gardens and temple-gardens, and of humans formed from earth.

## Points of Conflict

| Genesis 2 | Evidence |
|-----------|----------|
| Man created before plants and animals | Contradicts Genesis 1 and the fossil record |
| Four rivers from one source | The Tigris and Euphrates have separate sources in eastern Anatolia; Pishon and Gihon cannot be located |
| One original man and one woman | Population genetics requires a large ancestral population |
| Woman made from man's side | No biological basis; a symbolic story of kinship (2:23) |

## Assessment

Genesis 2 is an origin story, not a historical narrative. Its value for history lies in what it reveals about Israelite ideas of humanity, marriage, labour, and the land, and about the Mesopotamian motifs the writer knew. The doubling of creation accounts in chapters 1 and 2 is one of the clearest pieces of evidence that Genesis was compiled from more than one source.
