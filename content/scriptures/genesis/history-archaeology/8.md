## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Not applicable (myth); no physical evidence for the ark.** This chapter contains the closest verbal and narrative parallels between Genesis and the Babylonian *Epic of Gilgamesh*. The landing place, "the mountains of Ararat," refers to the Iron Age kingdom of Urartu. Repeated claims to have found Noah's ark on Mount Ararat or nearby have never withstood scientific scrutiny.

## What the Text Claims

- God remembers Noah, a wind blows, and the waters recede (8:1–3).
- The ark rests on "the mountains of Ararat" in the seventh month (8:4).
- Noah sends out a **raven**, which flies back and forth, and then a **dove** three times: it returns, it brings an olive leaf, and finally it does not return (8:6–12).
- The earth is dry in the second month of the following year (8:13–14).
- Noah builds an altar and offers burnt offerings; "the LORD smelled the pleasing odour" and promises never again to curse the ground (8:20–22).

## External Evidence

### Gilgamesh Tablet XI

The most striking parallels in all of Genesis 1–11 are found here. In the Standard Babylonian version, Utnapishtim tells Gilgamesh:

- The boat came to rest on **Mount Nimush** (formerly read "Nisir"), in the Zagros region east of the Tigris.
- After seven days he released a **dove**, which found no resting place and came back; then a **swallow**, which also came back; then a **raven**, which found food and did not return.
- He then made an offering on the mountain top. "The gods smelled the savour, the gods smelled the sweet savour, the gods gathered like flies around the sacrificer."
- The goddess Belet-ili (Ishtar in some versions) swears by her lapis necklace never to forget these days.

The sequence in Genesis 8 is the same: landing on a mountain, sending out birds to test for dry land, a sacrifice on leaving the boat, and a deity smelling the pleasing odour and making a promise. Most scholars regard these as too detailed to be coincidental. The Genesis writers knew this tradition, either through the Gilgamesh epic or through a related version circulating in the Levant. A fragment of Gilgamesh was found at **Megiddo** (14th century BCE), which shows that the epic was known in Canaan in the Late Bronze Age.

### The two sources again

The raven in 8:7 and the dove in 8:8–12 may come from different sources, and the chronology combines two systems. The non-Priestly source counts in blocks of seven and forty days (8:6, 10, 12). The Priestly source uses exact calendar dates (8:4–5, 13–14) and makes the flood last one year and eleven days, perhaps aligning a lunar year of 354 days with a solar year of 365.

### Ararat and Urartu

"Ararat" in Hebrew (*ʾrrṭ*) is the kingdom known in Assyrian texts as **Urartu**, centred around Lake Van in eastern Anatolia. Urartu emerged as a major power in the 9th century BCE and is mentioned in 2 Kings 19:37 and Jeremiah 51:27. Genesis says the ark landed on "the mountains of Ararat," that is, somewhere in that region, not on the peak now called Mount Ararat (Ağrı Dağı), which received the name much later. The use of the name points to an Iron Age writer. Different ancient traditions located the landing elsewhere: Berossus and the Syriac tradition favoured the Gordyene mountains (Jebel Judi), and the Qur'an names al-Judi.

### Searches for the ark

Since the 19th century, many expeditions have claimed to find the ark:

- The **Durupınar formation** near Doğubeyazıt, publicised from 1959 onward, has been shown by geologists to be a natural formation of rock and mud.
- Claims of wood found on Mount Ararat (for example, by Fernand Navarra in 1955 and later groups) were radiocarbon-dated to the 1st millennium CE or later, or could not be verified.
- No claim has been published in a peer-reviewed archaeological or geological journal.

Since the text names a region rather than a peak, even a genuine ancient timber find on Mount Ararat would not by itself connect to Genesis.

## Points of Agreement

- The chapter reflects an authentic and very old Mesopotamian narrative sequence.
- Its geography correctly places the landing in the mountainous region north of Mesopotamia, where the Tigris and Euphrates rise.

## Points of Conflict

| Genesis 8 | Evidence |
|-----------|----------|
| The ark rests in the mountains of Ararat | No verified remains; all claimed discoveries are natural or unverified |
| Waters recede from a global flood | No such flood is recorded in the geological record |
| Animals disperse from one place | Global biogeography contradicts dispersal from Anatolia |

## Assessment

Genesis 8 is the strongest single demonstration that the biblical flood story depends on Mesopotamian tradition. The birds and the sacrifice match Gilgamesh XI almost step by step, while the theology has been transformed: one God acts with purpose and promises the stability of the seasons. The reference to Ararat (Urartu) fits a writer of the 1st millennium BCE. The chapter offers no physical evidence for a historical ark.
