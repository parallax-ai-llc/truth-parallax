## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Not applicable (non-historical genre: schematic genealogy).** Genesis 5 lists ten generations from Adam to Noah with lifespans of up to 969 years. The form is closely paralleled by Mesopotamian lists of kings who reigned before the flood. Its numbers are schematic, differ between ancient versions of the text, and are biologically impossible.

## What the Text Claims

- "This is the book of the generations of Adam" (5:1): ten generations, from Adam to Noah.
- Each entry follows a fixed formula: age at the birth of the first son, years lived afterwards, total lifespan, "and he died."
- Lifespans range from 365 years (Enoch) to 969 years (Methuselah) (5:23, 27).
- Enoch, the seventh, "walked with God; then he was no more, because God took him" (5:24).
- Noah is born to Lamech, who names him as one who will bring relief from the cursed ground (5:28–32).

## External Evidence

### The Sumerian King List

The **Sumerian King List**, known from copies of the early 2nd millennium BCE (the best known is the Weld-Blundell prism, WB 444), begins: "After kingship descended from heaven…" It then lists eight kings in five cities before the flood, with reigns totalling over 240,000 years. Alulim of Eridu reigns 28,800 years. After the flood, kingship "descends from heaven" again and reigns become progressively shorter.

The parallels with Genesis 5 and 11 are structural:

- A fixed list of pre-flood figures with immense lifespans.
- The flood as a dividing line.
- Shorter lifespans afterwards, declining towards normal.

The figures in Genesis are much smaller than the Sumerian ones, but the pattern is the same.

### Berossus and the tenth hero

Berossus (c. 290 BCE) records **ten** kings before the flood, the last of whom, Xisouthros, is the flood hero (the Sumerian Ziusudra). Another early tablet (WB 62) also has ten names. Genesis 5 likewise has ten generations ending with Noah, the flood hero. Most scholars think the Hebrew list was shaped by this Mesopotamian convention.

### Enoch and Enmeduranki

The seventh name in Genesis 5 is Enoch, who "walked with God" and was taken. The seventh king in several Mesopotamian lists is **Enmeduranki** of Sippar, the city of the sun god. In a Babylonian text he is admitted to the gods' presence and taught divination. Enoch's lifespan of 365 years, the number of days in a solar year, strengthens the link with a solar figure. Later Jewish tradition (1 Enoch, 3rd century BCE onward) made Enoch a revealer of heavenly secrets, very like Enmeduranki.

### Three different sets of numbers

The ages in Genesis 5 differ between the major ancient versions of the text:

| Version | Years from creation to flood |
|---------|------------------------------|
| Masoretic Text (Hebrew) | 1,656 |
| Samaritan Pentateuch | 1,307 |
| Septuagint (Greek) | 2,242 (or 2,262 in some manuscripts) |

In the Masoretic numbers, Methuselah dies in the year of the flood. In some Septuagint manuscripts he outlives the flood by 14 years, an obvious problem that the other versions avoid in different ways. This shows that ancient scribes treated the numbers as adjustable chronological schemes. Many scholars think the Masoretic chronology was arranged so that the rededication of the Temple in 164 BCE fell 4,000 years after creation, though this is debated.

### Composition

The formulaic style and the heading "book of the generations" (*sefer toledot*) are characteristic of the **Priestly** source. The list is closely related to the Cainite genealogy in Genesis 4 (see chapter 4).

## Points of Agreement

- Genesis 5 belongs securely to a known ancient Near Eastern genre, the antediluvian list, and follows its conventions.
- Its overall structure (ten pre-flood figures, a seventh with special divine access, a tenth as flood hero) matches Mesopotamian tradition.

## Points of Conflict

| Genesis 5 | Evidence |
|-----------|----------|
| Lifespans of 700–969 years | Maximum documented human lifespan is about 122 years; no skeletal evidence of extreme longevity |
| Flood 1,656 years after creation (Masoretic) | Human history extends hundreds of thousands of years; no global flood |
| Fixed chronology | The three ancient text traditions disagree |

Some conservative interpreters propose that the genealogies contain gaps, or that the numbers are honorific. These proposals concede that the list cannot be read as a straightforward chronology.

## Assessment

Genesis 5 is a Priestly genealogy that adapts the Mesopotamian convention of long-lived pre-flood rulers. Its purpose is to link creation to the flood and to Israel's ancestors in an ordered sequence, not to record biography. The disagreement among ancient manuscripts confirms that its numbers were theological and chronological constructions.
