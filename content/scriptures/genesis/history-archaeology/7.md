## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Low (as a global event); the story is a version of Mesopotamian flood myth.** There is no geological, archaeological, or biological evidence for a worldwide flood that covered the highest mountains and wiped out all land life a few thousand years ago. Severe local floods in southern Mesopotamia are well documented and probably lie behind the Mesopotamian tradition that Genesis inherited.

## What the Text Claims

- Noah brings **seven pairs** of clean animals and one pair of unclean animals (7:2–3), or **two of every kind** (7:8–9, 15).
- In Noah's 600th year, "the fountains of the great deep burst forth and the windows of the heavens were opened" (7:11).
- Rain falls for **40 days and nights** (7:4, 12, 17).
- The waters rise **15 cubits** above the highest mountains (7:19–20).
- Every living thing on land dies (7:21–23).
- The waters prevail for **150 days** (7:24).

## External Evidence

### Mesopotamian flood accounts

- **The Sumerian Flood Story** (sometimes called the Eridu Genesis; a single fragmentary tablet from Nippur, c. 1600 BCE): the gods decide to destroy humankind; the pious king **Ziusudra** is warned, survives seven days and nights of flood in a great boat, and is granted eternal life.
- **Atrahasis** (c. 1700 BCE): a flood of seven days and nights destroys humanity, which the gods had found too noisy.
- **Gilgamesh** Tablet XI (Standard Babylonian version, late 2nd millennium BCE, best known from 7th-century copies from Nineveh): a storm of six days and seven nights; "all of mankind had returned to clay."

The Mesopotamian floods last about a week. Genesis extends the flood to a year and makes it a moral judgement rather than a decision of quarrelling gods.

### Two sources in one chapter

The doubled details in chapter 7 are a classic case for source criticism:

| Non-Priestly (J) | Priestly (P) |
|------------------|--------------|
| Seven pairs of clean animals (7:2–3) | Two of every kind (6:19–20; 7:15–16) |
| Rain for 40 days (7:4, 12, 17) | Waters prevail 150 days (7:24); the flood lasts about a year (7:11; 8:14) |
| Rain as the cause | Cosmic waters released from above and below (7:11) |
| God named YHWH (7:1, 5, 16b) | God named Elohim (7:9, 16a) |

The Priestly version imagines the collapse of the firmament described in Genesis 1, returning the world to the watery chaos before creation.

### Archaeological flood deposits

In 1929, Leonard Woolley announced that he had found a thick layer of clean, water-laid silt at **Ur** and identified it as evidence of "the Flood." Flood layers were also found at **Kish**, **Shuruppak** (Fara), Nineveh, and Uruk. However:

- The deposits are of **different dates** at different sites, from around 4000 BCE to about 2600 BCE.
- Some sites close to Ur show no flood layer at all at the same level.
- They are best explained as **local river floods** of the Tigris and Euphrates, which regularly devastated the flat alluvial plain.

Such floods probably inspired the Mesopotamian flood tradition. The Sumerian King List places the flood before the Early Dynastic kings, and Shuruppak, home of Ziusudra, shows a flood deposit around 2900 BCE. None indicates a global catastrophe.

### Geological and biological evidence

- **Ice cores** from Greenland and Antarctica preserve continuous annual layers going back more than 100,000 years (Greenland) and 800,000 years (Antarctica) with no break or flood signature.
- **Tree-ring chronologies** and annual lake sediments (varves, such as at Lake Suigetsu in Japan) run continuously through the 3rd millennium BCE.
- **Egyptian, Mesopotamian, and Chinese civilisations** show continuous occupation and record-keeping through the dates a biblical chronology would require for the flood (the Masoretic numbers give c. 2350 BCE).
- **Sedimentary rock** shows the marks of deserts, reefs, soils, and glaciation, not a single year-long flood.
- **Biogeography**: the distribution of species (marsupials in Australia, for example) cannot be explained by dispersal from one point in Anatolia a few thousand years ago.

The rejection of a global flood is not new. Christian geologists of the early 19th century, such as Adam Sedgwick and William Buckland, abandoned "flood geology" on the evidence.

## Points of Agreement

- The story is rooted in a real and much older Mesopotamian tradition.
- Catastrophic local floods in southern Mesopotamia are well documented archaeologically.

## Points of Conflict

| Genesis 7 | Evidence |
|-----------|----------|
| Water covers the highest mountains | Would require roughly three times the water on Earth; no source or drainage is possible |
| All land animals and humans die except those in the ark | Continuous civilisations and genetic diversity contradict a recent population bottleneck |
| Flood lasting about a year | Mesopotamian versions last about seven days; no geological deposit fits either |
| Seven pairs or two of every kind | The text itself preserves two incompatible instructions |

## Assessment

Genesis 7 is a theological retelling of a Mesopotamian flood myth, combined from two Israelite versions. The idea of a global flood conflicts with every relevant scientific discipline. The most likely historical root of the tradition is the memory of devastating river floods in southern Mesopotamia during the 4th and 3rd millennia BCE, magnified over time into a world-ending catastrophe.
