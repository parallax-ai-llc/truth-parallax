## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Mixed.** The genealogies of Keturah and Ishmael name real Arabian peoples, but these peoples are known from sources of the 8th–6th centuries BCE, which points to a 1st-millennium date for the lists. The story of Jacob and Esau is a legend about the relationship between Israel and Edom, not a biography of two brothers.

## What the Text Claims

- Abraham's sons by **Keturah** include **Midian**, and his grandsons include **Sheba** and **Dedan** (25:1–4).
- Abraham dies aged 175 and is buried in Machpelah by Isaac and Ishmael (25:7–10).
- **Ishmael's twelve sons** include **Nebaioth, Kedar, Adbeel, Massa, Dumah** and **Tema** (25:12–18).
- Rebekah bears twins: **Esau**, red and hairy (linked with *Edom*, "red," and *Seir*, "hairy"), and **Jacob**, who grasps his heel (25:21–26).
- "The elder shall serve the younger" (25:23). Esau sells his birthright for red stew (25:29–34).

## External Evidence

### The Ishmaelite tribes in Assyrian sources

Several of Ishmael's sons correspond to Arabian peoples and places known from Assyrian and Babylonian records:

| Genesis 25 | External attestation |
|------------|---------------------|
| Kedar | Qedarites, a leading Arab confederation in the annals of Tiglath-pileser III, Sennacherib, Esarhaddon and Ashurbanipal (8th–7th centuries BCE) |
| Nebaioth | Nabayati, in Ashurbanipal's inscriptions |
| Adbeel | Idibi'ilu, an Arab group appointed by Tiglath-pileser III on the Egyptian border |
| Dumah | Adummatu (Dumat al-Jandal), conquered by Sennacherib and Esarhaddon |
| Tema | Tayma in northwest Arabia, where Nabonidus lived c. 552–543 BCE |
| Massa | Mas'a, a group listed by Tiglath-pileser III |

Israel Eph'al's study of these sources shows that this cluster of peoples was prominent in the 8th–6th centuries BCE. The list therefore reflects the knowledge of Judean writers of that period about the north Arabian incense trade.

### Keturah's descendants

Sheba (Saba in South Arabia) and Dedan (al-ʿUla in northwest Arabia) were major centres of the incense trade in the 1st millennium BCE. Midian is associated with northwest Arabia, where a distinctive painted pottery ("Qurayyah ware") of the Late Bronze and early Iron Age has been found. The genealogy maps Israel's trading partners as distant relatives.

### Edom

Esau is identified with Edom (25:30). Edom as a kingdom with towns and administration is attested archaeologically mainly from the 8th to the 6th centuries BCE, for example at Busayra (Bozrah). Excavations by Thomas Levy and Erez Ben-Yosef in the **Faynan** and **Timna** copper districts, however, show an organised society exploiting copper in the 11th–10th centuries BCE, so some form of earlier Edomite polity is now widely accepted. The name Edom also appears in Egyptian texts of the late 13th century BCE (Papyrus Anastasi VI), referring to Shasu pastoralists.

### Jacob in Hosea

The prophet **Hosea** (8th century BCE) already knows the tradition that Jacob "took his brother by the heel in the womb" (Hos 12:3). This is the earliest datable reference to the Jacob story and confirms that it was told in the northern kingdom by then.

## Points of Agreement

- The genealogies accurately reflect real peoples of north and south Arabia.
- Israel's sense of kinship with Edom fits the close cultural and linguistic ties between the two peoples.
- The birthright and the elder son's double share were real inheritance customs (Deut 21:17).

## Points of Conflict

- The Arabian peoples listed flourished in the 8th–6th centuries BCE, not in the early 2nd millennium.
- Lifespans of 175 (Abraham) and 137 (Ishmael) are not realistic.
- The twin story is etiological: Esau's colour and hair explain the names Edom and Seir, and the oracle "the elder shall serve the younger" reflects Judah's domination of Edom under the monarchy (2 Sam 8:14).

## Assessment

Genesis 25 is a valuable document of Judah's view of the world in the Assyrian and Babylonian periods. Its tribal lists are accurate for that time, and its account of Jacob and Esau expresses in family terms the political rivalry between Israel and Edom. As a historical account of individuals, it has little to offer.
