## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Low.** The setting in the Valley of Elah is geographically accurate, and archaeology now shows a fortified Iron Age site there and a powerful Philistine Gath nearby. However, the chapter combines two different versions of the story, the giant's height has grown in transmission, and another biblical text credits the killing of Goliath to a different man, Elhanan. Most critical scholars regard David's defeat of Goliath as a heroic legend attached to David.

## What the Text Claims

- The Philistines camp between **Socoh and Azekah**; Israel camps in the **Valley of Elah** (17:1–3).
- **Goliath of Gath**, "six cubits and a span" tall, wears a bronze helmet, scale armour of 5,000 shekels, bronze greaves, and carries a bronze *kîdôn* and a spear with a shaft "like a weaver's beam" (17:4–7).
- He challenges Israel to single combat. David, a shepherd boy bringing food to his brothers, kills him with a sling stone and beheads him with his own sword (17:12–51).
- Saul asks Abner whose son the youth is; neither knows (17:55–58).

## External Evidence

### Two stories combined: the Septuagint

The Greek Septuagint (Codex Vaticanus) of chapters 17–18 lacks about 45 per cent of the Hebrew, including **17:12–31** (David arrives from Bethlehem), 17:41, 17:48b, **17:50**, and **17:55–18:5** (Saul does not recognise David). Without these passages the story is consistent with chapter 16: David is already Saul's armour-bearer. Scholars debate whether the Greek translator shortened a harmonising problem or whether the Hebrew combined two versions. Emanuel Tov and others argue that the Hebrew text conflated two originally separate accounts. Either way, the Hebrew contains two incompatible introductions of David.

### Elhanan and Goliath (2 Samuel 21:19)

2 Samuel 21:19 states: "**Elhanan** son of Jaare-oregim the Bethlehemite killed **Goliath the Gittite**, the shaft of whose spear was like a weaver's beam." The weaver's-beam description matches 1 Samuel 17:7 exactly. The Chronicler later harmonised this as "Elhanan … killed Lahmi the brother of Goliath" (1 Chr 20:5), apparently by reworking the Hebrew of "the Bethlehemite." Most critical scholars conclude that the feat originally belonged to Elhanan, one of David's warriors, and was transferred to David as his fame grew. In 1 Samuel 17 itself the giant is named only twice (17:4, 23); elsewhere he is simply "the Philistine."

Some conservative scholars propose that Elhanan was another name of David, or that the text of 2 Samuel 21:19 is corrupt. Neither proposal has strong support.

### Goliath's height

The Masoretic text gives "six cubits and a span" (about 2.9 m, 9 ft 9 in). The **Septuagint**, the Qumran scroll **4QSam<sup>a</sup>**, and **Josephus** (*Antiquities* 6.171) read "**four** cubits and a span" (about 2 m, 6 ft 9 in). Many textual critics consider the smaller figure original: a very tall warrior, not a giant.

### Goliath's armour

- Bronze helmet, **scale armour**, and a large spear fit Near Eastern equipment of the Late Bronze and Iron Ages; scale armour is well attested in Egypt and at Nuzi.
- **Bronze greaves** and the combination of helmet, body armour, greaves, round shield and thrusting spear resemble the **Greek hoplite** panoply of the 7th–6th centuries BCE. Israel Finkelstein (2002) argued that the description reflects Greek mercenaries known in Judah in the late monarchic period. Azzan Yadin (2004) compared the scene to Homeric duels.
- Jeffrey Zorn (2010) argued instead that the equipment fits an Iron I Philistine chariot warrior of Aegean background. The question is open.

### Single combat

Ritual duels between champions appear in the Egyptian **Story of Sinuhe** (c. 20th century BCE), where Sinuhe kills a strongman of Retenu, and in Homer's *Iliad* (Paris and Menelaus). The motif is old and widespread.

### The archaeological setting

- **Azekah** (Tel Azekah) and **Socoh** (Khirbet Shuweikeh) are real sites flanking the Elah Valley.
- **Khirbet Qeiyafa**, a fortified site above the valley, has radiocarbon dates around 1020–980 BCE. Garfinkel identifies it with **Shaaraim** (17:52) and as a Judahite fortress of David's time. Others assign it to a Saulide/north Israelite polity or a local Canaanite group.
- **Gath (Tell es-Safi)** was a large and powerful Philistine city in the 11th–9th centuries, only about 10 km from the valley. An inscribed sherd announced in 2005 (fully published in 2008) from an early Iron IIA level there contains the names **ʾLWT** and **WLT**, which are linguistically similar to "Goliath." The excavators stressed that this is **not** Goliath; it shows only that names of a similar, probably non-Semitic type were in use at Gath in that period.

## Points of Agreement

- Precise geography of the Elah Valley, Socoh, Azekah, and the route to Gath and Ekron (17:52).
- Philistine Gath was at its height in the period of the story.
- Goliath's name is of a plausible Philistine (non-Semitic) type.
- Fortification in the valley at roughly the right time (Khirbet Qeiyafa).

## Points of Conflict

| 1 Samuel 17 | Historical and textual evidence |
|-------------|---------------------------------|
| David kills Goliath | 2 Sam 21:19 credits Elhanan |
| Saul does not know David (17:55–58) | David is already Saul's armour-bearer (16:21) |
| Giant of six cubits | Older witnesses read four cubits |
| David carries Goliath's head to Jerusalem (17:54) | Jerusalem was not yet Israelite (2 Sam 5:6–9) |
| Hoplite-style armour | Possibly a late-monarchic description |

## Assessment

The chapter is a masterpiece of storytelling, built from at least two versions and enriched by an older heroic tradition that originally belonged to Elhanan. Its geographical frame is accurate, and archaeology confirms that the Elah Valley was a contested frontier between the Philistines of Gath and highland groups around 1000 BCE. Border clashes and champion duels are entirely plausible there. That David personally killed a giant named Goliath is not historically supportable on the evidence.
