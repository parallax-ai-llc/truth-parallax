## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Low.** The chapter is a second version of the story in chapter 24. Its function is the same: to establish that David repeatedly had the chance to kill Saul and refused.

## What the Text Claims

- The Ziphites again tell Saul that David is hiding on the hill of **Hachilah** (26:1).
- David and **Abishai** enter Saul's camp at night while a "deep sleep from the LORD" lies on everyone; David takes Saul's spear and water jug but forbids Abishai to strike (26:6–12).
- David taunts Abner for failing to guard the king, and Saul again blesses David (26:13–25).

## External Evidence

### A doublet

Nearly every element parallels chapters 23–24 (see the table in chapter 24): the Ziphite report, Saul's pursuit with 3,000 men, David's refusal to harm "the LORD's anointed," a token proving how close he came, and Saul's admission of wrong. Scholars generally see chapter 26 as an independent telling of the same tradition, perhaps the older one, retained by editors who valued both.

### "Go, serve other gods" (26:19)

David complains that his enemies have driven him from "the inheritance of the LORD," saying in effect, "Go, serve other gods." This reflects an early **territorial** idea of religion in which each land had its own god, similar to the assumptions of the Mesha Stele (Kemosh as Moab's god) and 2 Kings 5:17. It predates the monotheistic theology of later texts, an indication of old material.

## Points of Agreement

- Military camps with the commander sleeping at the centre surrounded by troops are realistic.
- The territorial theology of 26:19 fits Iron Age religious thought.

## Points of Conflict

- **Doublet** of chapter 24.
- **Miraculous deep sleep** makes the episode unverifiable.

## Assessment

The existence of two versions of the same story shows how important the theme was: whatever happened between David and Saul, the court tradition insisted that David was innocent of Saul's blood. Historians take the doublet as evidence of the tradition's apologetic purpose rather than of two separate events.
