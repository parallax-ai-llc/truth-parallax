## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Low.** This is the first of two stories in which David spares Saul (the other is chapter 26). The parallel structure and the explicitly apologetic speeches show that both are literary vehicles for the claim that David never raised his hand against the king.

## What the Text Claims

- Saul pursues David to the wilderness of **En-gedi** and enters a cave to relieve himself, unaware that David and his men are inside (24:1–3).
- David secretly cuts off the corner of Saul's robe but refuses to harm "the LORD's anointed" (24:4–7).
- He confronts Saul, who weeps, acknowledges that David will be king, and asks him to spare his descendants (24:8–22).

## External Evidence

### En-gedi

En-gedi is a spring oasis on the western shore of the Dead Sea, surrounded by cliffs and caves. A Chalcolithic temple lies nearby. The permanent settlement at **Tel Goren** was excavated by Benjamin Mazar and others; its earliest Iron Age stratum is generally dated to the late 7th century BCE. Since the story refers to the "wilderness" and its caves rather than a town, this does not create an anachronism.

### The doublet with chapter 26

| Feature | Chapter 24 | Chapter 26 |
|---------|-----------|-----------|
| Betrayal by the Ziphites | 23:19 | 26:1 |
| Location | Cave at En-gedi | Saul's camp at Hachilah |
| Token taken | Corner of Saul's robe | Saul's spear and water jug |
| David's refusal | "the LORD's anointed" (24:6) | "the LORD's anointed" (26:9–11) |
| Saul's response | Weeps, calls David "my son" | Calls David "my son," blesses him |

Most scholars regard the two as variants of one tradition preserved by the editors.

### Royal apology

The robe corner has symbolic weight: cutting the hem of a garment can signify transfer or denial of authority (cf. 15:27–28). Saul's own words, "I know that you shall surely be king" (24:20), put the enemy king's endorsement in his mouth, a familiar strategy of legitimation texts.

## Points of Agreement

- The En-gedi landscape of caves and wild goats (24:2) is accurately described.
- Fugitive bands sheltering in desert caves is realistic; such caves were used throughout antiquity, including during the Bar Kokhba revolt.

## Points of Conflict

- **Doublet** with chapter 26.
- **Saul's confession and oath** are dramatic speeches with an obvious political function.

## Assessment

Chapter 24 is apologetic narrative, not a historical report. It and chapter 26 were written to deny that David harmed Saul, an accusation that would only need answering if David's rise really did involve hostility to Saul's house. The setting is real; the event cannot be confirmed and was probably shaped to make the point.
