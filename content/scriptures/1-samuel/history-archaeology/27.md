## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Mixed to high (core fact).** That David served as a vassal of the Philistine king of Gath is so embarrassing for a national hero that most historians accept it as genuine. The narrator works hard to explain it away by claiming that David secretly raided Israel's enemies while pretending to raid Judah.

## What the Text Claims

- David and 600 men settle with **Achish of Gath**, who gives him **Ziklag** (27:1–6).
- "Therefore Ziklag has belonged to the kings of Judah to this day" (27:6).
- David spends a year and four months raiding the **Geshurites, Girzites, and Amalekites**, leaving no survivors so that no one can report that he is not raiding Judah (27:8–12).

## External Evidence

### Gath as overlord

Tell es-Safi/Gath was the dominant Philistine power in the region in the 10th–9th centuries. A Judahite warlord serving its king as a frontier vassal is entirely plausible and matches Amarna-age patterns in which local strongmen served larger city-states.

### Ziklag

The location of Ziklag is uncertain. Proposed sites include **Tel Sera'** and **Tel Halif** in the northern Negev. In 2019 Yosef Garfinkel, Saar Ganor, and Kyle Keimer announced that **Khirbet al-Ra'i**, near Lachish, could be Ziklag, citing an early Iron IIA settlement above a Philistine-period (Iron I) level. This identification is a proposal and remains debated.

### "To this day" and "kings of Judah"

The phrase "the kings of **Judah**" (27:6) presupposes the separate kingdom of Judah after the division of the monarchy (late 10th century onward). It shows that the narrative, at least in this form, was written well after David's time.

## Points of Agreement

- David's Philistine service fits the political map of the period.
- The southern raiding zone matches the Negev and Sinai borderlands.

## Points of Conflict

| 1 Samuel 27 | Analysis |
|-------------|----------|
| David pretends to raid Judah but kills everyone to hide the truth (27:9–11) | An apologetic explanation that also depicts mass killing to deceive his overlord |
| David flees to Achish as a friend | In 21:10–15 he had to feign madness to escape Achish |
| Amalekites raided | Ch. 15 says they were destroyed |

## Assessment

The chapter preserves a startling and probably authentic fact: before becoming king, David was a Philistine vassal. The narrator cannot deny it, so he reframes it as cunning loyalty to Israel. Historians generally take the existence of such defensive storytelling as a sign that the underlying memory is real.
