## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Mixed.** The chapter's picture of a slow, partial takeover, with major Canaanite cities left untouched, fits the archaeology of Iron I far better than the sweeping conquest of Joshua 1–12. But several specific claims, above all Judah's capture of Jerusalem and three Philistine cities, are contradicted by other biblical texts and by excavation.

## What the Text Claims

- After Joshua's death, Judah and Simeon defeat Adoni-bezek at Bezek (1:1–7).
- Judah captures and burns Jerusalem (1:8), takes Hebron and Debir (1:10–15), and destroys Zephath, renamed Hormah (1:17).
- Judah takes **Gaza, Ashkelon, and Ekron** (1:18), but cannot drive out the inhabitants of the plain, "because they had chariots of iron" (1:19).
- Benjamin fails to drive the Jebusites from Jerusalem (1:21). The house of Joseph takes Bethel (Luz) through an informer (1:22–26).
- A long list of cities is **not** conquered: Beth-shean, Taanach, Dor, Ibleam, Megiddo, Gezer, Acco, Sidon, and others (1:27–36).

## External Evidence

### The unconquered cities

Archaeology confirms that several of the cities listed in 1:27–33 remained Canaanite or Egyptian-controlled in the 12th century BCE:

- **Beth-shean** was an Egyptian garrison town under the 19th and 20th Dynasties. Stelae of Seti I and a statue of Ramesses III were found there.
- **Megiddo** Stratum VIIA continued as a Canaanite city until its destruction in the later 12th century. Stratum VIB–VIA, a renewed Canaanite town, lasted into the 11th century.
- **Gezer** remained outside Israelite control until the 10th century, consistent with 1 Kings 9:16.

### Highland settlement

Surveys show that the new Iron I villages clustered in the hill country, away from the lowland city-states. This matches the chapter's statement that the tribes held the hills but not the plains.

### Bethel and Jerusalem

Bethel (usually identified with Beitin) was excavated by W. F. Albright and J. L. Kelso. Kelso reported a Late Bronze destruction followed by a poorer Iron I settlement. Finkelstein and Singer-Avitz have since argued that the published material is too poor to support a clear sequence. Jerusalem's Late Bronze and Iron I remains are scanty, and there is no evidence of a burning in this period.

## Points of Agreement

- A gradual, partial, tribe-by-tribe process rather than a single campaign.
- Canaanite and Egyptian strongholds in the valleys (Beth-shean, Megiddo, Gezer) survived into Iron I.
- Settlement focused on the highlands, with lowland chariot-using powers beyond reach.

## Points of Conflict

| Judges 1 | Other evidence |
|----------|----------------|
| Judah captures Jerusalem and burns it (1:8) | 1:21 says the Jebusites stayed; 2 Sam 5:6–9 has David take the city. No burning layer is known |
| Judah takes Gaza, Ashkelon, Ekron (1:18) | Judg 3:3 lists the Philistine lords as unconquered; the Septuagint reads "did *not* take." Excavations show flourishing Philistine cities |
| Hebron and Debir taken by Judah/Caleb (1:10–15) | Joshua 10:36–39 credits Joshua with the same victories |
| Chariots "of iron" (1:19) | Iron was still rare in the early 12th century; the phrase likely reflects later usage |

## Assessment

Judges 1 is widely regarded as a compilation of older tribal notices, some possibly early, joined to the end of Joshua. Its "negative list" of unconquered cities is historically valuable because it contradicts the triumphal ideology of Joshua and matches the archaeological map. The editors also inserted items that glorify Judah, such as the conquest of Jerusalem and of Philistine cities. These sit awkwardly with the rest of the Bible and with excavation results.
