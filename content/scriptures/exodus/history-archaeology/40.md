## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Low.** The chapter describes the erection of the tabernacle and the glory of God filling it. There is no archaeological trace of the tabernacle at any of its later reported locations, and older biblical texts describe a temple building, not a tent, at Shiloh. The scene closely mirrors the dedication of Solomon's Temple.

## What the Text Claims

- On the **first day of the first month of the second year**, Moses sets up the tabernacle (40:1–2, 17).
- He places the ark, table, lampstand, incense altar, altar of burnt offering and laver, and anoints the sanctuary and the priests (40:3–33).
- The **cloud** covers the tent of meeting and the **glory of YHWH** fills the tabernacle, so that Moses cannot enter (40:34–35).
- The cloud's lifting signals when Israel is to set out; fire is in it by night (40:36–38).

## External Evidence

### The Temple parallel

1 Kings 8:10–11 describes Solomon's Temple dedication in almost the same terms: a cloud fills the house, and the priests cannot stand to minister "because of the cloud, for the glory of YHWH filled the house." The parallel supports the view that the tabernacle account was modelled on Temple traditions.

### The tabernacle after Sinai

- Joshua 18:1, a Priestly text, places the tent of meeting at **Shiloh**. But 1 Samuel 1–3 describes the sanctuary at Shiloh as a "temple" (*hekal*) with doorposts and doors.
- 2 Samuel 7:6 has God say he has moved about "in a tent and a tabernacle" since the exodus.
- 2 Chronicles 1:3 puts the tabernacle at **Gibeon** in Solomon's time, a claim made only by the late Chronicler.

**Shiloh** (Khirbet Seilun) was excavated by a Danish expedition in the 1920s–30s and by Israel Finkelstein in 1981–84. Finkelstein found an Iron I settlement destroyed in the mid-11th century BCE, plausibly linked with the Philistine wars, but no remains of a tabernacle or temple. Claims by some recent excavators to have located the site of the tabernacle have not been accepted by most archaeologists.

### Priestly chronology

The dating to New Year's Day echoes the Priestly flood story, where the earth dries on the first day of the first month (Gen 8:13). The Priestly writer frames history with a deliberate calendar of beginnings.

## Points of Agreement

- **An ark sanctuary at Shiloh** is consistent with archaeological evidence for an important Iron I settlement there.
- **Tent traditions.** 2 Samuel 7:6 and David's tent for the ark (2 Sam 6:17) suggest an older memory of a tent sanctuary.

## Points of Conflict

| Exodus 40 | Evidence |
|-----------|----------|
| Tabernacle as Israel's sanctuary until the Temple | Shiloh had a temple building; locations shift between texts |
| Erected on New Year's Day | Part of a schematic Priestly calendar |
| Glory-cloud dedication | Mirrors 1 Kgs 8:10–11 |

## Assessment

The final chapter of Exodus brings the Priestly account to its goal: God now dwells among Israel. As history, the tabernacle remains unattested, and the biblical traditions about its later history are inconsistent. Most critical scholars therefore see it as a retrojection of the Temple, perhaps incorporating memories of earlier tent shrines associated with the ark.
