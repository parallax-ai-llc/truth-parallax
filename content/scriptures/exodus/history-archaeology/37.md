## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Not applicable (cultic narrative).** Bezalel makes the sanctuary furniture described in chapters 25 and 30. The text has no external confirmation. The lampstand is its most discussed item: its design draws on ancient sacred-tree imagery, but the seven-branched form is not depicted until the late Second Temple period.

## What the Text Claims

- Bezalel makes the acacia **ark**, overlaid with gold, with its mercy seat and two cherubim (37:1–9).
- He makes the **table** for the bread of the Presence and its vessels (37:10–16).
- He hammers a pure gold **lampstand** with six branches and almond-flower cups, weighing a talent (37:17–24).
- He makes the **incense altar**, the anointing oil and the incense (37:25–29).

## External Evidence

### The lampstand

Carol Meyers studied the lampstand's design and argued that its stylised tree form, with branches, buds and flowers, matches Late Bronze Age artistic motifs of the sacred tree. She also noted that the text as we have it is later, so the design may preserve older artistic conventions. Iron Age sites have produced pottery lamps with multiple spouts and cultic stands, but no seven-branched lampstand.

The first clear depictions of the seven-branched menorah are on coins of the Hasmonean king **Mattathias Antigonus** (40–37 BCE) and on the **Arch of Titus** in Rome (after 70 CE), which shows the menorah carried off from the Second Temple. The Temple of Solomon is said instead to have had ten lampstands (1 Kgs 7:49).

### The ark

The ark's dimensions (2.5 by 1.5 by 1.5 cubits, roughly 1.1 by 0.7 by 0.7 metres) are those of a portable chest, comparable in scale to Egyptian chests carried on poles. No physical trace of the ark has ever been found. Its fate after the Babylonian destruction of Jerusalem in 586 BCE is unknown, and Jeremiah 3:16 already speaks of it as gone.

## Points of Agreement

- **Sacred tree motif.** The lampstand reflects a widespread ancient Near Eastern symbol, consistent with Israelite artistic culture.
- **Portable chests on poles** are well attested from Egypt.

## Points of Conflict

| Exodus 37 | Evidence |
|-----------|----------|
| One seven-branched lampstand | Solomon's Temple had ten lampstands (1 Kgs 7:49) |
| Seven-branched design from Sinai | First depictions date to the 1st century BCE |
| Incense altar part of the original furniture | Appears as a supplement in the instructions (ch. 30) |

## Assessment

The furniture of chapter 37 reflects a mixture of old artistic traditions and the reality of the Second Temple. The single seven-branched lampstand best matches what later Jews knew from their own Temple, while its tree imagery may be older. Taken together, the furnishings describe an ideal sanctuary rather than recovered objects.
