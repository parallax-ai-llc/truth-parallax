## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Not applicable (theological narrative).** The chapter deals with whether God's presence will go with Israel and with Moses' wish to see God's glory. Historically, its main interest is that it preserves a different picture of the sanctuary from the Priestly tabernacle: a simple tent outside the camp. No external evidence bears on the events.

## What the Text Claims

- God will send an angel ahead but will not go "in your midst"; the people mourn and strip off their ornaments (33:1–6).
- Moses pitches the **tent of meeting outside the camp**; the pillar of cloud descends at its door, and God speaks with Moses "face to face"; **Joshua** stays in the tent (33:7–11).
- God promises "my presence (literally 'my face') will go with you" (33:14).
- Moses asks to see God's glory; he is placed in a cleft of the rock and sees only God's back (33:18–23).

## External Evidence

### Two sanctuaries

In the Priestly account (chs. 25–31, 35–40) the tabernacle stands in the **centre** of the camp and is staffed by Aaronide priests. In 33:7–11 (and Num 11:16–30; 12:4–10; Deut 31:14–15) the tent stands **outside** the camp, is attended by Joshua, and is a place of oracle, not sacrifice. Menahem Haran and others identify this as an older, non-Priestly (often called Elohistic) tradition. Its tent is a simple place of revelation, closer to what desert tent shrines may actually have been like.

### Tent shrines

Archaeology offers only indirect support: the possible Midianite tent shrine at **Timna** (12th century BCE) and Arabian traditions of portable tent sanctuaries (the pre-Islamic *qubba*) show that tent shrines existed among desert peoples.

### The "face" of God

The idea of a deity's "face" as a mode of presence also appears in Phoenician and Punic religion, where the goddess Tanit is called "face of Baal." In Exodus the term negotiates between God's nearness and the danger of seeing God directly.

## Points of Agreement

- **Tent shrines** are a realistic feature of pastoral religion.
- **An older layer.** The tent outside the camp likely preserves an earlier sanctuary concept than the Priestly tabernacle.

## Points of Conflict

- The chapter speaks of the tent of meeting **before** it is built in chapters 35–40, a sign that different sources have been joined.
- Moses speaks with God "face to face" (33:11) yet cannot see God's face (33:20), a tension within the chapter itself.

## Assessment

Chapter 33 is a theological reflection on presence after the golden calf crisis, built from more than one source. For the historian it is chiefly evidence of the Pentateuch's layered composition and of a simpler tent tradition behind the Priestly tabernacle.
