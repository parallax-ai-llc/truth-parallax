## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Not applicable (genealogy and theological speech).** The chapter retells Moses' commission in Priestly language and inserts a genealogy of Reuben, Simeon, and Levi. Its significance is for the composition of the Pentateuch and for biblical chronology, which it complicates.

## What the Text Claims

- God says he appeared to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob as **El Shaddai**, "but by my name YHWH I did not make myself known to them" (6:2–3).
- God promises to bring Israel out "with an outstretched arm" (6:6–8).
- A genealogy lists the clans of Reuben, Simeon, and Levi (6:14–25).
- Moses and Aaron are sons of **Amram** and **Jochebed**; Amram is son of Kohath, son of Levi (6:16–20).
- Lifespans are given: Levi 137, Kohath 133, Amram 137 (6:16–20).

## External Evidence

### A second call narrative

Exodus 6:2–13 repeats the call already narrated in chapters 3–4, again with Moses objecting that he cannot speak well (6:12; compare 4:10). Its vocabulary ("I am YHWH," "El Shaddai," "covenant," "I will take you as my people") is typical of the **Priestly source** (P), generally dated to the exilic or early Persian period (6th–5th centuries BCE). The statement that the patriarchs did not know the name YHWH contradicts Genesis passages where they use it (e.g. Gen 15:7; 28:13). This contradiction was a starting point for the documentary hypothesis in the 18th and 19th centuries.

### Genealogy and chronology

Moses is only the fourth generation from Levi (Levi–Kohath–Amram–Moses). This agrees with Genesis 15:16 ("in the fourth generation they shall come back here") but clashes with the 430 years in Egypt claimed in 12:40. Even with the long lifespans given, four generations struggle to fill 430 years. The Septuagint and Samaritan Pentateuch reduce the problem by making the 430 years include the patriarchs' time in Canaan. Many scholars conclude that the biblical chronology combines schematic numbers from different traditions.

### Names

Some names in the Levite list have Egyptian connections. **Phinehas** (6:25) is Egyptian *Pa-nehesy*, "the Nubian," a name attested in New Kingdom Egypt. **Putiel** (6:25) may combine Egyptian *p3-di* ("the one given by") with the Semitic *El*, a hybrid pattern seen in the 1st millennium BCE. Names of this kind support the idea that the Levites, or the traditions about them, had Egyptian ties.

### Ancient Near Eastern parallels

Segmented genealogies that link a leader to an eponymous ancestor are common in tribal societies and in ancient Near Eastern king lists. They usually serve to define group membership and rank, here the priestly status of Aaron's line through Eleazar and Phinehas. Anthropological work (e.g. Robert Wilson) shows that such genealogies shift over time to reflect current social relations.

## Points of Agreement

- Egyptian names among the Levites (Phinehas, perhaps Putiel and Hophni) are linguistically authentic.
- Lineage lists are a standard ancient form for establishing priestly legitimacy.

## Points of Conflict

| Exodus 6 | Historical assessment |
|----------|----------------------|
| The patriarchs did not know the name YHWH | Contradicts Genesis (J source) |
| Four generations from Levi to Moses | Hard to reconcile with 430 years in Egypt (12:40) |
| Lifespans of 133–137 years | Schematic; not demographically realistic |
| Amram marries his aunt Jochebed (6:20) | Forbidden by later Priestly law (Lev 18:12), suggesting an old tradition kept despite the law |

## Assessment

This chapter is a Priestly composition that duplicates the earlier call story and attaches a genealogy legitimating the Aaronide priesthood. It is not historical reporting. Its incidental details are still informative. The marriage of Amram to his aunt, which later law forbade, may show that the editors preserved older tradition rather than inventing freely. The Egyptian names in the Levite line are among the evidence that scholars such as Friedman use for a "Levite exodus."
