## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Low.** The chapter's inventory of metals is tied arithmetically to a census of 603,550 men, which implies a total population of two million or more. No archaeological evidence supports a population anywhere near this size in Sinai, and the figure is widely regarded as a Priestly construction.

## What the Text Claims

- Bezalel makes the bronze **altar** and the bronze **laver**, the latter from "the mirrors of the women who served at the entrance of the tent of meeting" (38:1–8).
- The courtyard is built with linen hangings and bronze posts (38:9–20).
- An **inventory** lists 29 talents and 730 shekels of gold, 100 talents and 1,775 shekels of silver, and 70 talents and 2,400 shekels of bronze (38:24–29).
- The silver came from a half-shekel paid by **603,550 men** aged twenty and over (38:25–26).

## External Evidence

### The numbers

The arithmetic is exact: 603,550 half-shekels equal 301,775 shekels, which is 100 talents (at 3,000 shekels to the talent) plus 1,775 shekels. The same total appears in the census of Numbers 1:46. The gold amounts to roughly a tonne, the silver to over three tonnes.

A population of 603,550 adult men suggests two to three million people with their flocks. That is larger than the estimated population of the whole southern Levant in the Late Bronze Age, which survey archaeology puts in the low hundreds of thousands at most. Such a group would also have left traces in Sinai and at sites like Kadesh-barnea, and none have been found.

### The *eleph* proposal

George Mendenhall and later Colin Humphreys suggested that Hebrew *eleph* ("thousand") originally meant a clan or military unit. On this view, a figure like "603 *eleph* 550" would indicate far fewer people, perhaps around 20,000. The proposal is ingenious, but critics point out that the Priestly writer clearly understood the numbers as thousands, since the silver total in 38:25–26 only works if the full number is read literally.

### Bronze mirrors

Polished bronze mirrors with handles are common in Egypt and the Levant from the 2nd millennium onward. The "women who served at the entrance" are mentioned again only at Shiloh (1 Sam 2:22).

## Points of Agreement

- **Real objects.** Bronze mirrors, bronze altars and casting techniques are archaeologically well attested.
- **Weight system.** Talents and shekels, and a ratio of 3,000 shekels to a talent, are consistent with ancient weight standards.

## Points of Conflict

| Exodus 38 | Evidence |
|-----------|----------|
| 603,550 men | Population estimates for all of Late Bronze Canaan are far lower |
| Tonnes of gold and silver | Wealth on the scale of a royal treasury |
| Two million people in Sinai | No archaeological trace in surveys or at Kadesh-barnea |

## Assessment

The inventory is a carefully calculated literary device linking the tabernacle to the whole community of Israel. It reflects the Priestly writer's interest in order and completeness, not an archive. Whatever historical group lies behind the exodus traditions, most historians think it was small, far smaller than this chapter claims.
