## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Low.** Locust swarms and dust-laden darkness are real Near Eastern phenomena, but no Egyptian source records these plagues. Popular attempts to link the darkness with the eruption of Thera (Santorini) fail on chronology.

## What the Text Claims

- Pharaoh's officials urge him to let the people go: "Do you not yet understand that Egypt is ruined?" (10:7).
- Pharaoh offers to let only the men go; Moses insists on young and old, flocks and herds (10:8–11).
- An **east wind** brings locusts that eat everything left by the hail; a **west wind** drives them into the *yam suf* (10:12–20).
- Thick darkness covers Egypt for three days, but the Israelites have light where they live (10:21–23).
- Pharaoh threatens Moses with death if he sees his face again (10:24–29).

## External Evidence

### Locusts

Desert locust plagues have struck Egypt and the Levant throughout recorded history. Egyptian texts mention locusts as a scourge, and Assyrian reliefs show locusts being eaten. Swarms are carried by winds; an east wind bringing them from Arabia and a wind driving them into the sea are realistic. The Hebrew *ruaḥ yam* (10:19) literally means "sea wind," that is, a west wind in Canaanite geography, a sign the writer's orientation was from the land of Israel rather than Egypt, where a north wind blows from the sea.

### Darkness: khamsin and Thera

- **Khamsin**: spring sandstorms in Egypt can darken the sky for days. Many commentators see this as a natural background for the ninth plague.
- **Thera**: the Late Bronze Age eruption of the Aegean volcano Thera has been proposed as the cause of the darkness and other plagues. Radiocarbon dating places the eruption around **1600 BCE** (some archaeologists still argue for c. 1500), well before any biblical date for the exodus. Egyptian texts do not clearly describe it. The Tempest Stela of Ahmose (c. 1540 BCE), which describes a great storm and darkness, has been linked to Thera by some scholars, but the connection is disputed, and the stela does not mention Semites leaving Egypt.

### Darkness and the sun god

Darkness would strike at **Amun-Ra**, the sun god, the supreme deity of New Kingdom Egypt. Interpreters, both critical and conservative, read the ninth plague as a symbolic defeat of Egypt's chief god, consistent with 12:12 ("on all the gods of Egypt I will execute judgments").

## Points of Agreement

- Locust swarms driven by wind and spring sandstorms are both real.
- The narrative's religious symbolism fits the importance of the sun in Egyptian religion.

## Points of Conflict

| Exodus 10 | Historical assessment |
|----------|----------------------|
| Locusts destroy everything in Egypt | No Egyptian record |
| Three days of darkness except where Israel lives | Not explicable naturally; no record |
| Thera eruption as cause | Dated c. 1600 BCE, a century or more before even the earliest biblical date |
| "Sea wind" as west wind | Reflects a Canaanite, not Egyptian, viewpoint |

## Assessment

Chapter 10 continues the stylised escalation of the plague cycle. The bargaining over who may go (men only, then all but the herds) builds suspense rather than reflecting negotiations. The Thera theory, though popular in documentaries, is not accepted by most archaeologists or biblical scholars because the dates do not match and no source connects the eruption with Israel. The wind terminology is a small but telling clue that the story was told and written in Canaan.
