## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Not applicable (cultic narrative).** Chapters 35–40 report the carrying out of the instructions of chapters 25–31, often word for word. The main historical questions are about composition. The Greek Septuagint version of these chapters differs markedly, which suggests the text was still being shaped in the Second Temple period.

## What the Text Claims

- Moses repeats the Sabbath law, adding: "You shall kindle no fire in all your dwellings on the Sabbath day" (35:1–3).
- He calls for a freewill offering of gold, silver, bronze, blue, purple and scarlet yarn, linen, goat hair, skins, acacia wood, oil, spices and gems (35:4–9).
- Men and women bring jewellery and materials; skilled women spin yarn and goat hair (35:20–29).
- Bezalel and Oholiab are introduced as master craftsmen and teachers (35:30–35).

## External Evidence

### The Septuagint's different version

In the Septuagint, Exodus 35–40 is shorter than the Hebrew and arranges the material in a different order, for example describing the priestly garments before the tabernacle structure. D. W. Gooding argued that the Greek translators rearranged and abbreviated a Hebrew text like ours. Others think the Greek reflects a different Hebrew edition. Either way, these chapters show more textual variation than almost any other part of the Pentateuch.

### Dyes and textiles

Blue (*tekhelet*) and purple (*argaman*) were dyes made from murex sea snails, a famous Phoenician industry. Textile fragments from **Timna** dated to about 1000 BCE have been shown to contain genuine murex purple. This shows that such dyes reached the Arabah by the early Iron Age, though they remained luxury goods.

### Spinning

Spindle whorls and loom weights are among the most common finds at Iron Age sites. The picture of women spinning (35:25–26) reflects real household production.

## Points of Agreement

- **Real materials and crafts.** The listed materials and the gendered division of textile work fit the ancient Levant.
- **Freewill offerings** for sanctuaries are known; compare the voluntary gifts for the Second Temple (Ezra 1:4–6; 2:68–69).

## Points of Conflict

- The large quantities of luxury materials, including murex-dyed fabric, fit a royal or temple economy rather than a wilderness camp.
- The ban on kindling fire on the Sabbath (35:3) appears only here and reflects the kind of detailed Sabbath regulation seen in post-exilic sources.

## Assessment

Chapter 35 begins the fulfilment section that balances the instructions of chapters 25–31. The repetition is deliberate and reflects the Priestly concern with exact obedience. The Septuagint's different version reminds us that these chapters were among the last parts of Exodus to reach their final form.
