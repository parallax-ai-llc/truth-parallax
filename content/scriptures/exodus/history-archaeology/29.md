## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Not applicable (ritual text).** The chapter prescribes a seven-day ordination for Aaron and his sons and a twice-daily offering. Comparable installation rituals are known from Late Bronze Syria, so the ritual type is ancient. But the details of the daily offering match post-exilic practice more closely than practice under the monarchy.

## What the Text Claims

- Aaron and his sons are washed, clothed, and Aaron is **anointed** with oil (29:1–9).
- A bull and two rams are sacrificed; blood is put on the right ear, thumb and big toe of the priests (29:10–21).
- The ordination lasts **seven days** (29:35–37).
- Two lambs are offered every day, one in the morning and one at twilight, as a continual (*tamid*) burnt offering (29:38–42).

## External Evidence

### Installation rituals

Texts from **Emar** in Syria (13th–12th centuries BCE) describe the multi-day installation of a high priestess of the storm god. It includes anointing her head with oil, sacrifices, a feast, and a period of seclusion. Daniel Fleming's study of these texts showed that elaborate, multi-day installation ceremonies with anointing were part of the Syrian cultic world long before the Priestly source. Anointing with oil also marked kings and officials elsewhere; the Amarna letters refer to a pharaoh anointing a Syrian vassal king.

### The daily offering

2 Kings 16:15, set in the reign of Ahaz (8th century BCE), speaks of a **morning burnt offering and an evening grain offering**. Exodus 29:38–42 instead requires a lamb at both times, as does Numbers 28:3–8. Ezekiel 46:13–15 mentions only a morning lamb. The evening lamb appears to be a later development, and Second Temple sources describe the twice-daily lamb offering as standard.

## Points of Agreement

- **Ancient ritual forms.** Washing, anointing, blood rites and seven-day periods are attested across the ancient Near East.
- **Ordination by "filling the hand"** (29:9) uses an idiom with possible Akkadian parallels, though what the Akkadian expression meant is debated.

## Points of Conflict

| Exodus 29 | Evidence |
|-----------|----------|
| Twice-daily lamb offering from Sinai | 2 Kgs 16:15 shows a different pattern in the 8th century |
| Aaronide priesthood ordained once for all | Older texts show varied priestly lines (Levites, Zadokites, Elides) |

## Assessment

The ordination ritual draws on genuinely ancient models, as the Emar texts show. But the Priestly system of an Aaronide priesthood and a twice-daily lamb offering reflects later development in the Temple cult. Leviticus 8 narrates the carrying out of these instructions, a sign of how carefully the Priestly source was planned as a unit.
