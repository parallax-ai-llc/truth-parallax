## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Not applicable (cultic and theological text).** The chapter names the craftsmen of the tabernacle and closes the instructions with a Sabbath law. Its pattern of divinely revealed plans and divinely gifted builders belongs to a long ancient Near Eastern tradition of temple-building accounts. The craftsmen cannot be verified, and the Sabbath-as-sign theme is typical of the exilic Priestly writers.

## What the Text Claims

- God calls **Bezalel** son of Uri son of Hur, of Judah, and fills him with "the spirit of God" for metal, stone and woodwork (31:1–5).
- **Oholiab** son of Ahisamach, of Dan, is appointed to assist him (31:6–11).
- The **Sabbath** is a "perpetual covenant" and a "sign" between God and Israel; whoever works on it is to be put to death (31:12–17).
- God gives Moses the two **tablets of the testimony**, "written with the finger of God" (31:18).

## External Evidence

### Temple-building accounts

Victor Hurowitz showed that biblical temple-building stories follow a common ancient Near Eastern pattern: a divine command, a revealed plan, the gathering of materials and skilled workers, construction, dedication and blessing. The **Gudea Cylinders** from Lagash (c. 2100 BCE) describe the ruler Gudea receiving the plan of the Eninnu temple from the gods in a dream. In the Ugaritic Baal Cycle, the craftsman god **Kothar-wa-Khasis** builds Baal's palace. Exodus 25–40 fits this genre closely.

### Mirroring the Temple builders

The Temple account features a skilled craftsman from Tyre, Hiram/Huram-abi, whose mother came from the tribe of Dan (2 Chr 2:14) or Naphtali (1 Kgs 7:14). Oholiab of Dan and Bezalel of Judah appear to mirror the Temple's builders. Bezalel's grandfather Hur is placed in a Judahite genealogy in 1 Chronicles 2:19–20, and a man named Bezalel appears among the returnees in Ezra 10:30.

## Points of Agreement

- **Genre.** The chapter matches a well-documented ancient literary form.
- **Craft specialisation.** The trades listed (gold, silver, bronze, gem-cutting, carpentry) correspond to real ancient workshop crafts.

## Points of Conflict

- **Sabbath as covenant sign.** In the Priestly source, covenant signs are the rainbow (Gen 9), circumcision (Gen 17) and the Sabbath. Emphasis on the Sabbath as Israel's distinguishing mark is strongest in exilic and post-exilic texts (Ezek 20:12; Neh 13:15–22), when it became a key identity marker.
- **Name echoes.** The names cluster with late genealogies (Chronicles, Ezra) rather than with any early source.

## Assessment

Chapter 31 completes the Priestly blueprint using the standard features of a temple-building story. Its links to Solomon's Temple builders and its Sabbath theology point to composition in the exile or Persian period. It tells us how a sanctuary should be founded in the ideal past, not who actually built one.
