## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Low.** The core event, the Ark's entry into Jerusalem, comes from 2 Samuel 6:12–16. Almost everything else, the Levitical bearers, the organised choirs and orchestra, and the named musicians, is the Chronicler's addition and reflects the Temple of the Persian period.

## What the Text Claims

- David prepares a tent and declares that "no one but the Levites may carry the Ark," since Uzza's death came from neglecting this rule (15:1–2, 13).
- He assembles 862 Levites from six clans and the priests Zadok and Abiathar (15:3–11).
- The Levites carry the Ark on poles "as Moses had commanded" (15:15).
- Heman, Asaph, and Ethan lead singers with **cymbals**, **harps**, and **lyres**, and priests blow **trumpets** (15:16–24).
- David wears a robe of **fine linen** (*būṣ*) and a linen ephod (15:27).
- Michal sees David "dancing and making merry" and despises him (15:29).

## External Evidence

### Musical instruments

The instruments named are real Iron Age types:

- **Lyres** (*kinnor*) are depicted on the Megiddo ivory (Late Bronze Age), on a painted jar from Kuntillet 'Ajrud (c. 800 BCE), and in the Lachish reliefs of Sennacherib (701 BCE), where captives carry lyres.
- **Bronze cymbals** have been found at several Bronze and Iron Age sites in the Levant.
- **Trumpets** (*ḥaṣoṣerot*) played by priests reflect Numbers 10:1–10. Silver trumpets are shown being carried from the Second Temple on the Arch of Titus in Rome.

Sennacherib's inscription on his campaign of 701 BCE lists male and female musicians among Hezekiah's tribute. Royal and temple musicians in Judah are therefore historical. What is not documented for the 10th century is the elaborate Levitical guild structure.

### The language

The word *būṣ*, "fine linen" or "byssus" (15:27), occurs only in late biblical books (Chronicles, Esther, and Ezekiel 27:16). 2 Samuel 6:14 mentions only a linen ephod. The vocabulary belongs to the Chronicler's own era.

### Levitical bearers

The Pentateuch's rule that Levites carry the Ark (Num 4:15; Deut 10:8) belongs to the Priestly and Deuteronomic layers, which most scholars date to the late monarchy and exile. 2 Samuel 6 mentions no Levites at all. The Chronicler retrojects the law to explain Uzza's death and to establish the Levites' role.

## Points of Agreement

- The Ark's procession into Jerusalem with music and dancing, and Michal's contempt, follow 2 Samuel 6:12–16.
- The instruments mentioned are genuine ancient types.

## Points of Conflict

| 1 Chronicles 15 | 2 Samuel 6 |
|-----------------|------------|
| Levites carry the Ark on poles | Bearers are unspecified; no Levites appear |
| 862 Levites, named choirs and instrumentalists | "David and all the house of Israel" with instruments |
| David wears a robe of *būṣ* and a linen ephod | David, girded with a linen ephod, dances "with all his might" |
| Michal's contempt briefly noted (15:29) | A full quarrel between David and Michal (6:20–23) |
| Abiathar and Zadok serve together as priests (15:11) | Zadok is not introduced until 2 Sam 8:17 |

## Assessment

Chapter 15 is a good example of the Chronicler projecting the institutions of the Second Temple back to David. The guild names, Heman, Asaph, and Ethan, correspond to singers known from Psalm headings and post-exilic lists. The emphasis on proper Levitical carrying answers the question raised by chapter 13. The underlying memory of a procession bringing the Ark to Jerusalem is shared with Samuel, but the Chronicler's details describe worship in his own day.
