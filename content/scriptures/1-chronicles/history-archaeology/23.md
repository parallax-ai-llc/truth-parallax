## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Low (as an account of David's reign); moderate as evidence for Second Temple practice.** The organisation of the Levites attributed to the aged David has no basis in Samuel or Kings. Its numbers are unrealistic, and its details, especially the age of service and the Levites' duties, match the post-exilic Temple.

## What the Text Claims

- When David was old and made Solomon king, he counted the Levites **thirty years old and upward**: **38,000** men (23:1–3).
- 24,000 oversee the work of the Temple, 6,000 are officers and judges, 4,000 gatekeepers, and 4,000 musicians "with instruments that I made for praise" (23:4–5).
- The Levitical houses of **Gershon, Kohath, and Merari** are listed (23:6–23).
- The Levites are to serve **from twenty years old and upward**, since they no longer need to carry the tabernacle (23:24–27).
- Their duties: assisting the Aaronite priests, caring for courts and chambers, purifying holy things, the showbread, flour, baking, measures, and praise every morning and evening (23:28–32).

## External Evidence

### The age of service

The minimum age of Levitical service varies across the biblical texts:

| Text | Age |
|------|-----|
| Numbers 4:3, 23, 30 | 30 years |
| Numbers 8:24 | 25 years |
| 1 Chronicles 23:3 | 30 years |
| 1 Chronicles 23:24, 27; 2 Chr 31:17 | 20 years |
| Ezra 3:8 | 20 years |

The contradiction inside this chapter (30 in 23:3, 20 in 23:24) suggests that the text was revised, with the lower age added to match post-exilic practice. Ezra 3:8, set c. 537–520 BCE, uses the same age. A shortage of Levites after the exile may explain the lower age: Ezra 2:40 counts only 74 Levites among the returnees, against over 4,000 priests, and Ezra 8:15–20 describes the difficulty of recruiting them.

### The Levites' role

The duties in 23:28–32 present the Levites as assistants to the priests, not as priests. This subordinate status matches Ezekiel 44:10–16 and the Priestly code, and reflects the Second Temple hierarchy. The Levitical families of Gershon, Kohath, and Merari belong to the same Priestly tradition (Num 3–4).

## Points of Agreement

- The distinction between priests and Levites, and the Levitical duties listed, accurately reflect the Second Temple as known from Ezra–Nehemiah and later sources.
- Temple musicians and gatekeepers as Levitical groups match post-exilic lists.

## Points of Conflict

| Claim | Assessment |
|-------|------------|
| 38,000 Levites in David's day | Ezra 2:40 counts 74 Levites returning; even with singers and gatekeepers, a few hundred |
| David organised the Levites | Not mentioned in Samuel or Kings; the institutions are post-exilic |
| Levites aged 30 and 20 in the same chapter | Evidence of redactional layers |
| 4,000 musicians with instruments David made | No earlier tradition |

## Assessment

Chapters 23–27 form a block of lists that interrupts the transition from David to Solomon. Many scholars, including Williamson, think some of this material was added in stages after the Chronicler's main work, as different Temple groups sought authority for their status. The chapter tells us how the Levites of the late Persian or early Hellenistic Temple understood their role and how they grounded it in David's authority.
