## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Low.** The psalm placed in David's mouth is a compilation of later psalms, and the double arrangement of worship, the Ark in Jerusalem and the tabernacle at Gibeon, is a harmonising construction. The chapter is valuable as evidence for the liturgy and the Psalter of the Chronicler's time.

## What the Text Claims

- The Ark is placed in the tent David pitched; he offers sacrifices and gives every Israelite bread, a portion of meat, and a raisin cake (16:1–3), following 2 Samuel 6:17–19.
- David appoints **Asaph** and his kinsmen to minister before the Ark with praise (16:4–7).
- A long psalm of thanksgiving (16:8–36).
- Asaph serves before the Ark in Jerusalem, while **Zadok** and the priests offer burnt offerings morning and evening "before the tabernacle of YHWH in the high place at **Gibeon**" (16:37–42).

## External Evidence

### The psalm

The psalm is assembled from three psalms in the Psalter:

| 1 Chronicles 16 | Source |
|-----------------|--------|
| 16:8–22 | Psalm 105:1–15 |
| 16:23–33 | Psalm 96:1–13 (lightly adapted) |
| 16:34–36 | Psalm 106:1, 47–48 |

Psalm 106:47 asks God to "gather us from among the nations," a prayer that presupposes the exile. Psalm 106:48 is the doxology that closes **Book IV** of the Psalter. Its inclusion here suggests that the Chronicler knew the Psalms already arranged in books, a Persian-period development. The people's response, "and all the people said 'Amen' and praised YHWH" (16:36), turns the doxology's instruction into narrative.

### The Gibeon tabernacle

1 Kings 3:4 says that Solomon sacrificed at Gibeon, "the great high place," which troubled later readers committed to a single sanctuary. The Chronicler explains it by placing the Mosaic tabernacle and bronze altar at Gibeon (16:39; 21:29; 2 Chr 1:3–6). No earlier source puts the tabernacle there. The Iron Age remains at el-Jib, including the large rock-cut pool and wine installations excavated by James Pritchard, show a prosperous town but no identifiable sanctuary.

### Daily worship

The twice-daily burnt offering (16:40) reflects the *tamid* of Exodus 29:38–42 and Numbers 28:3–8, part of the Priestly legislation. It was central to Second Temple worship.

## Points of Agreement

- The distribution of food (16:1–3) follows 2 Samuel 6:17–19.
- The Asaphite singers are a real post-exilic guild (Ezra 2:41; Neh 11:17).

## Points of Conflict

| Claim | Assessment |
|-------|------------|
| David composed or commissioned this psalm | The text is a medley of later psalms, one of which assumes the exile |
| The tabernacle stood at Gibeon | No earlier tradition; a harmonisation of 1 Kgs 3:4 with the law of one sanctuary |
| Organised daily Levitical praise | Reflects Second Temple liturgy |

## Assessment

Chapter 16 shows how the Chronicler worked with sacred texts already authoritative in his community. By placing a medley of psalms in David's time, he presents the founder of the dynasty as the author of Jerusalem's worship, a role echoed in the Psalm headings. For historians, the chapter is valuable evidence that the Psalter had taken a recognisable form by the 4th century BCE.
