## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Low to mixed (secondary account).** The chapter is a lightly edited copy of 1 Samuel 31 with a new theological conclusion. Its historical value depends entirely on its source. Where it differs from Samuel, the changes are explained by the Chronicler's purposes.

## What the Text Claims

- The Philistines defeat Israel on Mount Gilboa and kill Saul's sons Jonathan, Abinadab, and Malchi-shua (10:1–2).
- Saul, wounded, falls on his own sword when his armour-bearer refuses to kill him (10:3–5).
- "So Saul and his three sons died, and **all his house died together**" (10:6).
- The Philistines fasten Saul's head in the **temple of Dagon** (10:10). The men of Jabesh-gilead bury the bones (10:11–12).
- Saul died for his unfaithfulness, for not keeping YHWH's word, and for consulting a medium; so YHWH "turned the kingdom over to David" (10:13–14).

## External Evidence

### The setting

Mount Gilboa overlooks the Jezreel and Beth-shean valleys. A Philistine advance into this region to cut the highlands off from the northern valleys is strategically plausible. Beth-shean (Tel Beth-shean / Tell el-Husn) was an Egyptian garrison town in the Late Bronze Age and early 12th century BCE. Amihai Mazar's excavations showed that it continued as a Canaanite town in Iron I. Distinctive Philistine material culture is rare there, so a Philistine victory display at Beth-shean, as in 1 Samuel 31:10, is possible but not supported by direct evidence.

### No external attestation

No extra-biblical source mentions Saul or the battle of Gilboa.

## Points of Agreement

- Chronicles and Samuel agree on the core: a Philistine victory at Gilboa, the deaths of Saul and three sons, and burial by the people of Jabesh-gilead.
- The geography is realistic.

## Points of Conflict

| 1 Chronicles 10 | 1 Samuel 31 |
|-----------------|-------------|
| "All his house died together" (10:6) | Saul's men died; his son Ishbaal and his household survived and reigned for two years (2 Sam 2:8–10) |
| Saul's **head** is fastened in the temple of **Dagon** (10:10) | Saul's **armour** is put in the temple of **Ashtaroth**, and his **body** is fastened to the wall of Beth-shan (31:10) |
| The Jabesh men bury the bones (10:12) | They first burn the bodies, then bury the bones (31:12) |
| A theological verdict on Saul (10:13–14) | No closing verdict |

The burning of the bodies may have been dropped because cremation was unacceptable. The difference over the temples may partly reflect a text of Samuel different from the Masoretic one.

## Assessment

Chapter 10 is the Chronicler's entire account of Saul's reign. He skips Saul's rise and the long story of David's flight and opens with Saul's death, so that David can succeed directly. The statement that "all his house died together" erases the seven-year civil war between David and Saul's dynasty (2 Sam 2–4). This is selective rewriting, not independent history. Its value lies in showing how the Chronicler read Saul: as a model of the unfaithfulness that loses the kingdom, a warning repeated throughout the book.
