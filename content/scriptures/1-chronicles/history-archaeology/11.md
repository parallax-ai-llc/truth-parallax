## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Mixed (secondary account).** The chapter reproduces 2 Samuel 5 and 23 with significant edits. Its kernel, David's kingship and his capture of a Jebusite stronghold that became Jerusalem's royal centre, is plausible and may be historical. The Chronicler's framing, in which "all Israel" makes David king at once, removes seven years of division.

## What the Text Claims

- "All Israel" gathers at Hebron and anoints David king, "according to the word of YHWH by Samuel" (11:1–3).
- David and all Israel take **Jebus**, "that is, Jerusalem," and the stronghold of Zion becomes the "city of David" (11:4–5).
- **Joab** wins command by being first to attack the Jebusites (11:6), and he repairs the rest of the city (11:8).
- A roster of David's **mighty men**, with their exploits, including the Three and the Thirty (11:10–41a).
- Sixteen further names not found in Samuel, several from Transjordan (11:41b–47).

## External Evidence

### Jerusalem before David

Jerusalem is attested as **Urusalim** in the 14th-century BCE **Amarna letters**, where its ruler Abdi-Heba writes to Pharaoh. The Middle Bronze Age fortifications around the Gihon Spring, excavated by Ronny Reich and Eli Shukron, show that the spring was strongly protected long before David. Iron I remains on the City of David ridge are modest but real, including the lower phases of the Stepped Stone Structure.

### "Jebus"

The name **Jebus** for the city is not found in any extra-biblical source; ancient texts call it Jerusalem (Urusalim). J. Maxwell Miller argued that the identification of Jebus with Jerusalem is a biblical construction, derived from the name of the Jebusite inhabitants. The Chronicler took the equation from Joshua and Judges (Josh 18:28; Judg 19:10).

### The capture

2 Samuel 5:6–8 mentions "the blind and the lame" and the *ṣinnor* ("water shaft" or "channel"). Chronicles omits both and instead credits Joab. The traditional identification of the *ṣinnor* with **Warren's Shaft** has been undermined: Reich and Shukron showed that the vertical shaft was not reachable in the Bronze Age and that the main water system was the Middle Bronze spring tower and channels.

## Points of Agreement

- Jerusalem was a fortified Canaanite town before Israelite rule.
- The roster of warriors (11:10–41) closely matches 2 Samuel 23:8–39. Benjamin Mazar argued that such a list reflects a real royal guard.
- The additional names in 11:41b–47 have no apparent ideological purpose, and some commentators think they come from an older source.

## Points of Conflict

| 1 Chronicles 11 | 2 Samuel |
|-----------------|----------|
| All Israel anoints David immediately after Saul's death | David reigns over Judah alone for seven and a half years before the northern tribes accept him (2 Sam 2–5) |
| Joab becomes commander by leading the assault (11:6) | Joab is already commander (2 Sam 2:13; 8:16) |
| Jashobeam killed **300** (11:11) | Josheb-basshebeth killed **800** (2 Sam 23:8) |
| No "blind and lame," no *ṣinnor* | Both present (2 Sam 5:6–8) |

One detail survives the Chronicler's editing: **Uriah the Hittite** is listed among the mighty men (11:41), although the story of David's crime against him is omitted.

## Assessment

The capture of Jerusalem is one of the best-founded episodes in the David tradition. A Jebusite town did become the seat of the Judahite dynasty, and the Tel Dan Stele confirms that dynasty's link to David. But Chronicles adds nothing that can be checked. Its version smooths the account into a unanimous national acclamation and elevates Joab's role. The roster of warriors is the most archival-looking material in the chapter.
