## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Low to mixed (secondary account).** The story of Uzza's death comes from 2 Samuel 6:1–11 and is a cultic legend about the danger of the holy. Chronicles moves it earlier in David's reign and frames it as a decision of the whole national assembly, which serves its theology rather than history.

## What the Text Claims

- David consults "every leader" and proposes to bring back the Ark, neglected "in the days of Saul" (13:1–4).
- He assembles all Israel "from the **Shihor of Egypt** to **Lebo-hamath**" (13:5).
- The Ark is brought from **Kiriath-jearim** (Baalah) on a new cart (13:6–8).
- At the threshing floor of Chidon, **Uzza** steadies the Ark and is struck dead (13:9–11).
- David, afraid, leaves the Ark in the house of **Obed-edom the Gittite** for three months, and Obed-edom's house is blessed (13:12–14).

## External Evidence

### Kiriath-jearim

Kiriath-jearim is identified with **Deir el-Azar**, near Abu Ghosh west of Jerusalem. Excavations from 2017 by Israel Finkelstein, Thomas Römer, and colleagues uncovered a large artificial platform on the summit. They dated it to the first half of the 8th century BCE and suggested it was built under the northern kingdom of Israel. They proposed that the Ark Narrative was composed in that context, linking a northern cult object with a sanctuary on the border with Judah. These conclusions are new and debated, but they offer a possible historical setting for the tradition, much later than David.

### The Ark itself

No physical trace of the Ark exists. Portable chests or shrines carried in procession are well attested in the ancient Near East: Egyptian bark shrines, and the chest-like box shrines of Tutankhamun's tomb. These provide a cultural background for the Ark, but not evidence of a specific object.

### The ideal borders

"From the Shihor of Egypt to Lebo-hamath" (13:5) describes Israel's ideal frontiers (Josh 13:3–5; 1 Kgs 8:65). No state ruled from Jerusalem ever controlled this territory. The phrase is a statement of the Chronicler's "all Israel" ideal.

## Points of Agreement

- The core story (new cart, Uzza's death, Obed-edom) follows 2 Samuel 6:3–11 closely.
- The tradition that the Ark rested at Kiriath-jearim before Jerusalem is shared with 1 Samuel 7:1–2.

## Points of Conflict

| 1 Chronicles 13 | 2 Samuel |
|-----------------|----------|
| Ark transfer comes **before** Hiram's embassy and the Philistine wars (chs 13–14) | It comes **after** them (2 Sam 5:11–25; 6:1) |
| A national assembly decides the move (13:1–5) | David and 30,000 chosen men act (2 Sam 6:1) |
| Threshing floor of **Chidon** | Threshing floor of **Nacon** (2 Sam 6:6) |
| The Ark was ignored "in the days of Saul" (13:3) | 1 Sam 14:18 (Masoretic text) has Saul call for the Ark |

## Assessment

The Chronicler's reordering puts David's concern for the Ark as his first act after taking Jerusalem, which displays his piety. Uzza's death sets up the lesson of chapter 15: only Levites may carry the Ark. Obed-edom the Gittite, a man from Philistine Gath in Samuel, will later reappear as a Levite gatekeeper and musician (15:18, 24; 26:4). Historically, the episode is a sanctuary legend whose origins are uncertain, possibly northern and possibly of the 8th century.
