## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Mixed.** The Benjaminite genealogy mixes early and late material. Its list of Saul's descendants preserves older forms of personal names than Samuel does, which supports its use of authentic tradition. Other parts reflect Persian-period settlement.

## What the Text Claims

- A genealogy of Benjamin with clans in Geba, Moab, Ono, Lod, Aijalon, and Jerusalem (8:1–28).
- The ancestors of Saul lived at **Gibeon** (8:29–32).
- **Saul's family**: his sons Jonathan, Malchi-shua, Abinadab, and **Eshbaal**; Jonathan's son **Merib-baal**; and a long line of descendants down to the sons of Ulam, "mighty warriors, archers" (8:33–40).

## External Evidence

### The "Baal" names

In Samuel, Saul's son is called **Ish-bosheth** ("man of shame") and Jonathan's son **Mephibosheth**. Chronicles gives **Eshbaal** ("man of Baal" or "Baal exists") and **Merib-baal** (8:33–34). Most scholars think the Chronicles forms are original. Later scribes replaced *ba'al* with *bošet*, "shame," when *ba'al* was associated with a foreign god. In the early Iron Age, however, *ba'al* ("lord") could also be an epithet of Israel's God.

An inked inscription on a jar from **Khirbet Qeiyafa**, published by Yosef Garfinkel and colleagues in 2015, reads "**Eshbaal son of Beda**." It dates to the late 11th or early 10th century BCE. This is not Saul's son, but it shows that the name Eshbaal was in use in Judah at the time, as Chronicles implies.

### Post-exilic geography

Several places in 8:12, notably **Ono** and **Lod**, appear among the towns resettled after the exile (Ezra 2:33; Neh 11:35). The list of Benjaminites who lived in Jerusalem (8:28) parallels the post-exilic lists in chapter 9 and Nehemiah 11. Parts of the genealogy evidently describe the Benjamin of the Persian period.

### Benjaminites in Moab

The note about Shaharaim fathering children "in the country of Moab" (8:8) is obscure. It recalls the Moabite links in the book of Ruth and Benjaminite connections across the Jordan (e.g. Jabesh-gilead, 1 Sam 11; 31). It cannot be verified.

## Points of Agreement

- The Baal-names of Saul's family are probably earlier than the "-bosheth" forms in Samuel.
- The name Eshbaal is attested epigraphically in the right period.
- Gibeon as a centre of Saul's ancestry fits the Benjaminite geography of Saul's power base in 1 Samuel.

## Points of Conflict

| 1 Chronicles 8 | Samuel |
|----------------|--------|
| Saul's father is **Kish** son of **Ner** (8:33) | Kish is son of **Abiel**, and Ner is Kish's brother or Abner's father (1 Sam 9:1; 14:50–51) |
| Saul's sons: Jonathan, Malchi-shua, Abinadab, Eshbaal | Jonathan, Ishvi, Malchi-shua (1 Sam 14:49) |
| Saul's line continues for many generations | Samuel implies the house of Saul was almost extinguished (2 Sam 9; 21) |

## Assessment

Chapter 8 shows the Chronicler preserving material that does not serve his pro-Davidic agenda: a long and flourishing line of Saul's descendants. That makes it likelier that he drew on a real Benjaminite genealogy, perhaps maintained by families who claimed descent from Saul in the Persian period. The Baal-names are a small but telling sign that Chronicles sometimes preserves older readings than Samuel.
