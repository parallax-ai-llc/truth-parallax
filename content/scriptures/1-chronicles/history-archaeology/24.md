## Historical Verdict

**Reliability: Low as Davidic history; high as a description of a real Second Temple institution.** The 24 priestly courses existed and served in rotation for centuries, as later inscriptions and Qumran texts confirm. Their origin in David's reign is not supported. The evidence indicates that the system took shape after the exile.

## What the Text Claims

- The sons of Aaron are divided into descendants of **Eleazar** (16 houses) and **Ithamar** (8 houses) (24:1–4).
- David, Zadok, and Ahimelech assign the courses **by lot**, recorded by the scribe Shemaiah (24:5–6).
- The 24 courses, in order, from **Jehoiarib** (1st) and Jedaiah (2nd) to Maaziah (24th), including **Hezir** (17th) and **Abijah** (8th) (24:7–19).
- A list of other Levitical heads, also chosen by lot (24:20–31).

## External Evidence

### Later evidence for the courses

- **Qumran.** Several calendrical texts from Qumran (the *Mishmarot*, 4Q320–330) track the rotation of the priestly courses through the year, using names from this chapter.
- **The Hezir tomb.** A monumental tomb in the Kidron Valley in Jerusalem, dated to the 2nd–1st centuries BCE, bears a Hebrew inscription naming members of the **Bene Hezir**, a priestly family. Hezir is the 17th course in 24:15.
- **Caesarea.** Fragments of a Hebrew inscription found at Caesarea in 1962 list the 24 courses with the Galilean villages where they settled after 70 CE. One course is linked with **Nazareth**. The inscription is late Roman or Byzantine.
- **The Hasmoneans.** 1 Maccabees 2:1 traces Mattathias, father of the Maccabees, to the priests of **Joarib** (Jehoiarib), the first course.
- **New Testament.** Luke 1:5 places Zechariah, father of John the Baptist, in the "division of **Abijah**," the eighth course.

### Earlier evidence

The post-exilic lists show a less developed system. **Ezra 2:36–39** (Neh 7:39–42) names just **four** priestly families among the returnees: Jedaiah, Immer, Pashhur, and Harim. **Pashhur**, one of the largest, does not appear in 1 Chronicles 24. Nehemiah 10:2–8 and 12:1–21 give lists of 21 or 22 priestly heads, which overlap only partly with the 24 courses. This progression suggests that the 24-course system was formed in the late Persian or early Hellenistic period.

## Points of Agreement

- The courses are a real institution, attested from the Hellenistic period to Late Antiquity.
- Assigning service by lot is consistent with later Temple practice (Luke 1:9; Mishnah *Tamid*).

## Points of Conflict

| 1 Chronicles 24 | Other evidence |
|-----------------|----------------|
| David organises 24 courses | Ezra 2 knows four priestly families after the exile |
| Pashhur absent | Pashhur is a major post-exilic priestly family (Ezra 2:38; 10:22) |
| Ahimelech son of Abiathar serves with Zadok (24:3, 6) | Elsewhere Abiathar son of Ahimelech is David's priest (1 Sam 22:20; 2 Sam 20:25) |

## Assessment

Chapter 24 is a good example of the Chronicler's method: an institution of his own era is given Davidic origins. H. G. M. Williamson argued that the list reflects a reorganisation of the priesthood in the Persian period, possibly added to Chronicles somewhat later. The order of the courses, with Jehoiarib first, may reflect that family's prominence, which the Hasmoneans later exploited. Historically, the chapter is primary evidence for the priesthood of the Second Temple.
